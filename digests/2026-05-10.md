**Models & ML Research: modular/sparse MoEs go mainstream, a Muon successor, pretraining-recipe surprises, and third-party frontier evals**

The through-line is *structure* — getting modularity, sparsity, and well-conditioned optimization to emerge rather than be hand-imposed — while eval houses weigh in on what frontier models can actually do and a running argument plays out over which 2026 marketing numbers are real.

- **Ai2 (Allen Institute) — EMO.** A 1B-active / 14B-total MoE (128 experts, k=8) pretrained so modular structure emerges from data with no domain labels — the trick is constraining all tokens in a *document* to draw from a shared expert pool, using document boundaries as the only grouping signal. Result: prune to ~25% of experts (~12.5% in the tightest setting) for ~1pt accuracy loss where standard MoEs collapse, with expert subsets specializing *semantically* (math, code) rather than syntactically. RT'd by both co-author @sewon__min (with Ryan Wang, Akshita Bhagia) and @berkeley_ai ([post](https://x.com/berkeley_ai/status/2052832292620661187), [post](https://x.com/sewon__min/status/2052900406792257627)). @cwolferesearch called the adaptive-routing idea "really interesting" ([post](https://x.com/cwolferesearch/status/2052812794576114127)); @prasann_singhal and @mikelasby (who'd seen it teased at ICLR) flagged it as a "fresh style of pre-training work" with big implications for domain-specific compression ([post](https://x.com/sewon__min/status/2052837438859719152)). The broader vibe — @code_star: "all sparsity and distillation research is relevant again it seems" ([post](https://x.com/code_star/status/2052859702275658157)).
- **Tilde Research — Aurora optimizer.** A "leverage-aware" optimizer for rectangular matrices that fixes a *neuron-death feedback loop* Tilde diagnosed in Muon on MLP layers — its advantage over Muon grows monotonically with MLP expansion factor.
