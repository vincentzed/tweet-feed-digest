**Models & ML Research — DeepSeek V4 lands with a single-kernel MoE, and inference unit economics start to fracture**

Quieter on the open-weights cascade after this week's barrage, but DeepSeek's release is consequential and the pricing dynamics on hosted inference are starting to look structurally weird.

- **DeepSeek V4 + MegaMoE** — @SemiAnalysis_'s breakdown: "As we've come to expect from a DeepSeek release, DeepSeek V4 comes with more flashy ML systems optimizations. This time? MegaMoE, a 1400 line fused CUDA kernel that computes the entire MoE forward pass" ([post](https://x.com/SemiAnalysis_/status/2055423177841353184)). Compressing the entire MoE forward into a single kernel is the kind of aggressive systems bet that lets DeepSeek price inference toward zero. Practitioner confirmation from @Michaelzsguo: "My Deepseek V4 Pro agent (inside codex) has been pursuing goal for more than 13 hours, burning ~100M tokens, and has only costed me $1.85" ([post](https://x.com/Michaelzsguo/status/2055317248923848713)). 13 hours of agentic compute for $1.85 reframes the Anthropic-billing complaints from earlier this week — the alternative when one frontier shop ships single-kernel MoEs is "I don't care that the new model is good, why am I paying anything?".
- **Gemma-4-31B is 3–6x cheaper than Qwen-3.6-27B on OpenRouter — and the mechanism is becoming clearer** — Buried signal from @hu_yifei: "Why is gemma 4 31b so much cheaper than qwen3.6 27b on openrouter? Pairs directly with @simran_s_arora's launch note that the Gemma-4-31B-it-Pearl endpoint **simultaneously generates tokens and a digital asset that subsidizes inference** ([post](https://x.com/simran_s_arora/status/2055348155051569474)) — Pearl's Proof of Useful Work is the structural reason a comparably-sized open model can list 3-6x below another. - **PyTorch SPMD types for tensor parallelism / @ezyang at PyTorchCon Europe 2026** — Posted by @PyTorch: Ed Yang explains why developers find tensor parallelism so unforgiving and how PyTorch is exploring SPMD types to catch mesh/sharding mistakes through type checking ([post](https://x.com/PyTorch/status/2055019525679231062)). Practical relevance for anyone touching DTensor or production inference engines — "I changed the mesh and everything broke silently" is exactly the bug a type system can prevent.
