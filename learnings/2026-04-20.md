# Learnings - 2026-04-20 01:29

## AI / Machine Learning

- **Kubernetes as control theory**: Kubernetes's real contribution was popularizing control system theory at scale — a reconciliation loop comparing desired state to actual state, which lets you run software reliably at massive scale. Understanding it as "control theory for distributed systems" reframes why it's valuable beyond container orchestration. _Source: @fatih ([post](https://x.com/fatih/status/2045752294298279960))_
- **Residual connections haven't evolved since 2015**: Since ResNet introduced `x + F(x)`, the depth residual has been the only "highway" for inter-layer communication in deep networks — a decade of depth scaling without rethinking how layers talk to each other, suggesting an architectural frontier. _Source: @lianghui_zhu ([post](https://x.com/lianghui_zhu/status/2045868757869080695))_
- **softmax is misnamed**: `exp(x) / sum(exp(x))` is technically softargmax — a smooth approximation of argmax, not of max. The true soft-max is `log-sum-exp`. The distinction matters when reasoning about what the operation actually approximates. _Source: @AlexShtf ([post](https://x.com/AlexShtf/status/2045812200372089247)), @ylecun ([post](https://x.com/ylecun/status/2045917773625233598))_
- **Language is countably infinite, not uncountable**: Finite alphabets produce only countable sets of strings, so any claim that "language is uncountably infinite" is mathematically wrong and undermines related claims about LLM limits. _Source: @wolftivy ([post](https://x.com/wolftivy/status/2045738900241649887))_
- **Demis Hassabis's "Einstein test" for AGI**: Train a model on all human knowledge with a 1911 cutoff and see if it can independently derive general relativity. This operationalizes AGI as the ability to produce genuinely novel scientific discovery, not just recombine known facts. _Source: @rohanpaul_ai ([post](https://x.com/rohanpaul_ai/status/2045912351690592708))_
- **Async RL failure mode — stale rollouts and policy mismatch**: In asynchronous RL, stale rollouts make importance weights highly uneven, collapsing effective sample size so a few bad trajectories dominate the update. The underlying issue is that the data-generating policy diverges from the policy being updated, causing off-policy drift, credit-assignment degradation over long horizons, and unstable policy mixing. Mitigations preserve on-policy-ness while decoupling inference from training. _Source: @novasarc01 ([posts](https://x.com/novasarc01/status/2045551589717655720), [post](https://x.com/novasarc01/status/2045555838270795869), [post](https://x.com/novasarc01/status/2045562314674634826))_
- **Pretraining vs RL scaling laws are distinct**: Pretraining has a clean compute axis, but RL scaling laws must account for environment cost, rollout parallelism, and reward noise — which is why RL scaling curves look messier. Treating them interchangeably is a common conceptual error that distorts strategic compute decisions. _Source: @cwolferesearch ([posts](https://x.com/cwolferesearch/status/2045637757133890034), [post](https://x.com/cwolferesearch/status/2045987953848041925))_
- **Cross-datacenter PD disaggregation**: Kimi's PRFaaS architecture separates long-context prefill (sent to remote compute-optimized clusters) from decode/short requests (local), enabling cross-datacenter + heterogeneous-hardware inference with lower latency and cheaper tokens. _Source: @Kimi_Moonshot via @novasarc01 ([post](https://x.com/novasarc01/status/2045761450560647430)), @crystalsssup ([post](https://x.com/crystalsssup/status/2045476183157170618)), @Ar_Douillard ([post](https://x.com/Ar_Douillard/status/2045585751883018391))_
- **Hardware-software co-design for inference stages**: Different inference stages have different bottlenecks — prefill is compute-bound (favoring GPUs like Vera Rubin), decode is memory-bandwidth-bound (favoring LPUs like Groq). Homogeneous datacenters leave performance on the table. _Source: @Halex623 ([post](https://x.com/Halex623/status/2045654272327512342))_
- **MORI-IO KV connector on single node**: AMD + EmbeddedLLM's MORI-IO KV connector achieves 2.5× goodput with PD disaggregation on a single node — useful intro to the fundamentals without multi-node complexity. _Source: @vllm_project ([post](https://x.com/vllm_project/status/2045381618928582995))_
- **Sim2real via physics randomization**: If you don't have exact physics specs, randomize the sim's physical parameters — the resulting policy is robust enough to work in physical environments with different gravity/friction (e.g. Earth and the ISS). _Source: @kalomaze ([post](https://x.com/kalomaze/status/2045597186864697422))_
- **Robot language grounding is hard**: "Open the top drawer and watch out for the vase" requires grounding language into 3D spatial understanding, object affordances, and physical constraints simultaneously — which is why household chores remain unsolved. _Source: @rohanpaul_ai citing Fei-Fei Li ([post](https://x.com/rohanpaul_ai/status/2045931489758683175))_
- **LongCoT benchmark — long-horizon reasoning is unsolved**: A 2.5K-question benchmark (chemistry, math, chess, logic, CS) measuring reasoning over tens to hundreds of thousands of tokens. SOTA frontier models score under 10% — a sharp reminder that current LLMs are weak at multi-turn coherence despite headline benchmark wins. _Source: @natashajaques ([post](https://x.com/natashajaques/status/2045557384769413627)), @lateinteraction (RT @sumeetrm) ([post](https://x.com/lateinteraction/status/2045603377560670562))_
- **Recursive Language Models (RLMs) beat raw frontier models on long context**: An RLM harness can absorb tens of millions of tokens reliably. DSPy.RLM with Qwen3.5-9B reportedly hits ~15.69% on LongCoT-full vs GPT-5.2's 9.83% — harness design is now where outsized gains live. _Source: @lateinteraction (RT @samhogan) ([post](https://x.com/lateinteraction/status/2045525495459074558)), @lateinteraction (RT @raw_works) ([post](https://x.com/lateinteraction/status/2045581766501519367))_
- **Metal verify kernels for quantized inference**: DFlash v0.1.4 shows custom Metal kernels yield 2.2× speedup on Qwen3.6-35B-A3B-4bit at 1024-token context on an M5 Max, plus peak memory reductions — kernel-level work still pays even against stock mlx_lm. _Source: @bstnxbt ([post](https://x.com/bstnxbt/status/2045591537661190209))_
- **MiniMax M2.7 + Blackwell Ultra optimization**: A new fused QK RMS Norm kernel demonstrates that day-0 model support that is also fast on brand-new silicon requires custom kernel work, not just framework support. _Source: @vllm_project ([post](https://x.com/vllm_project/status/2045435727018893330))_
- **Quantization debugging patterns**: (1) Sharp accuracy drop after quantizing attention projections or first/last layers signals layer sensitivity due to wider dynamic range. (2) Long-context degradation (fine at 2K, broken at 32K+) typically indicates KV-cache quantization errors accumulating. (3) MoE instability after quantization usually points to imprecision in the routing/gating network rather than experts. _Source: @Eigen_AI_Labs ([posts](https://x.com/Eigen_AI_Labs/status/2045653672676274236), [post](https://x.com/Eigen_AI_Labs/status/2045654743310336045), [post](https://x.com/Eigen_AI_Labs/status/2045655330345091283))_
- **Origin of "tensor" terminology in ML**: The tensor engine naming convention traces back to SN3/Lush — a Lisp dialect developed by Leon Bottou and Yann LeCun at Bell Labs in 1992. The original SN paper in French didn't use the word at all. Useful grounding for people who think tensor libraries started with TensorFlow. _Source: @ylecun ([post](https://x.com/ylecun/status/2045606881125642536)), @norpadon ([post](https://x.com/norpadon/status/2045515535643758596)), @ylecun ([post](https://x.com/ylecun/status/2045607759836549509))_
- **Larry Page's 2007 bet on compute**: Page predicted AI would come from "a lot of computation" rather than clever algorithms — a direct anticipation of the bitter lesson over a decade before it was named. _Source: @rohanpaul_ai ([post](https://x.com/rohanpaul_ai/status/2045930684343316850))_
- **Transformers v5 breakage**: vLLM v0.19.1's jump to Transformers v5.5.4 is a major version bump worth pulling for if you serve Gemma 4, GLM 5.1, or Kimi K2.5 — upstream deps in the LLM-serving stack ship breaking changes quickly. _Source: @vllm_project ([post](https://x.com/vllm_project/status/2045427930323714079))_
- **JAX vs PyTorch trade-offs**: JAX is conceptually more elegant and shines for compiler-driven workflows (functional transforms, vmap/pmap, compiler-level thinking), while PyTorch dominates groundbreaking research output. PJRT and StableHLO are strong shared foundations regardless of framework. _Source: @novasarc01 ([post](https://x.com/novasarc01/status/2045608385949884689)), @steeve ([post](https://x.com/steeve/status/2045578142471860625)), @bingxu_ ([post](https://x.com/bingxu_/status/2045636597362892994))_
- **RL benchmark monoculture problem**: Many RL papers run experiments only on Qwen-2.5 + MATH-500, making results hard to trust as non-spurious. Diversity in models and benchmarks is needed. _Source: @siddarthv66 ([post](https://x.com/siddarthv66/status/2045643996106526912))_
- **MoE economics undermine local inference**: With Mixture-of-Experts architectures, large inference services can serve at a fraction of local-inference cost, putting pressure on the viability of local frontier models. _Source: @Dorialexander ([post](https://x.com/Dorialexander/status/2045914477200101645))_

## Software Engineering

- **Biological limits are a feature, not a bug**: Tiny working memory and shallow calculation depth force abstraction, compression, and intuition. Infinite resources would remove the pressure that produces intelligence in the first place. _Source: @fchollet ([post](https://x.com/fchollet/status/2046019725449458135))_

## Security

- **Vercel breach — audit Google Workspace OAuth grants**: To check if your Google Workspace was compromised: Admin Console → Security → Access and Data Control → API Controls → Manage app access → Accessed Apps, and filter by the specific OAuth app ID. The incident also highlighted lack of timely email transparency from Vercel. _Source: @BrendanFalk ([post](https://x.com/BrendanFalk/status/2045953132770025769)), @AIandDesign ([post](https://x.com/AIandDesign/status/2045992972190425370)), @jessethanley ([post](https://x.com/jessethanley/status/2045980539501650276))_
- **Third-party OAuth grants as attack surface**: The Vercel incident appears to trace to a third-party AI tool (context.ai) via a Chrome extension that had been granted OAuth access — third-party integrations granted broad scopes are a durable attack vector even when the main vendor is secure. _Source: @jaimeblascob ([post](https://x.com/jaimeblascob/status/2045960143209152981))_
- **Unencrypted secrets at rest is catastrophic**: The Vercel compromise leaked thousands of npm/pypi tokens and cloud keys in plaintext — encrypt-at-rest for secrets is not optional even for trusted internal systems. _Source: @shawmakesmagic ([post](https://x.com/shawmakesmagic/status/2045952673107833332))_
- **Don't let AI coding agents read `.env` files**: Feeding credentials into a coding agent's context window can leak them through logging, tool outputs, or provider-side retention. Keep secrets out of agent-accessible paths. _Source: @Hesamation ([post](https://x.com/Hesamation/status/2045853585196437934))_
- **Threat-actor attribution is often wrong**: The Vercel breach was attributed to "ShinyHunters," but the real group disclaimed involvement — an impersonator was using the name. Attribution based on claimed identity alone is unreliable. _Source: @vxunderground ([post](https://x.com/vxunderground/status/2045913185799037263))_
- **Key rotation has downstream fallout**: Rotating DB keys after the Vercel breach can cascade into infra errors on managed providers (Supabase login down), pushing operators toward self-hosting for control. _Source: @jackfriks ([post](https://x.com/jackfriks/status/2045918273439465498))_
- **Self-hosting isn't inherently safer**: "We use a VPS" isn't more secure — just less observed. Well-resourced platforms get compromised; so do you, you just may not detect it. _Source: @SIGKITTEN ([post](https://x.com/SIGKITTEN/status/2045892993027035475)), @glcst ([post](https://x.com/glcst/status/2046013438363685110))_
- **Phone calls via car speakers are not private**: In many cars, the other party's voice is audible to anyone within ~250 feet with surprising clarity — useful threat model for confidential conversations in parking lots. _Source: @RKalland ([post](https://x.com/RKalland/status/2045543384039694697))_

## Key Takeaways

- **Long-horizon reasoning is the open frontier, and harness design beats model size**: LongCoT shows SOTA frontier models scoring under 10%, while DSPy's RLM harness with a 9B Qwen model beats GPT-5.2 on the same slice. The gains live in how you wrap the model. _Sources: @natashajaques ([post](https://x.com/natashajaques/status/2045557384769413627)), @lateinteraction (RT @raw_works) ([post](https://x.com/lateinteraction/status/2045581766501519367))_
- **Compute capacity and PD disaggregation are the new strategic axes in inference**: Anthropic reportedly can't serve its own models due to compute; meanwhile Kimi's cross-DC PRFaaS and AMD's MORI-IO show 2.5×+ goodput from separating prefill and decode. Capacity and topology are now first-class competitive moats. _Sources: @The_AI_Investor ([post](https://x.com/The_AI_Investor/status/2045980230066790761)), @novasarc01 ([post](https://x.com/novasarc01/status/2045761450560647430)), @vllm_project ([post](https://x.com/vllm_project/status/2045381618928582995))_
