- **TPU 8 (Ironwood successor)**: Google's 8th-gen TPU comes in two variants — TPU 8t (training) and TPU 8i (inference). A TPU v8t pod is 9,600 chips delivering ~121 exaflops FP4 (~3× Ironwood's 42.5). _Source: @OfficialLoganK ([post](https://x.com/OfficialLoganK/status/2046998921508249624)), @demishassabis ([post](https://x.com/demishassabis/status/2046995068343742752)), @JeffDean ([post](https://x.com/JeffDean/status/2047405389856297387))_
- **Inference compute = intelligence**: Comparing models by a single capability number ignores that today's frontier models trade intelligence for inference compute. The right axis of comparison is intelligence per token (or per dollar). _Source: @polynoamial ([post](https://x.com/polynoamial/status/2047387675762802998))_
- **Scaling laws differ for pretraining vs RL**: Both are called "scaling laws" but they're modeled differently — most pretraining scaling is an inverse power law on compute/data, while RL scaling behaves differently and the shared label is misleading. _Source: @cwolferesearch ([post](https://x.com/cwolferesearch/status/2046963975548702786))_
- **Muon optimizer**: Muon changes the optimization geometry to make the practical search problem easier and the large-batch regime less punishing — particularly when parameters are genuinely matrix-valued. Open question: whether it is provably better than AdamW or merely produces lower loss under fixed tuning budgets. _Source: @xidulu ([post](https://x.com/xidulu/status/2046771050453537257)), @novasarc01 ([post](https://x.com/novasarc01/status/2046864660788625537))_
- **End-to-end learned KV-cache eviction**: Treating KV cache eviction as a learning problem — directly optimizing task performance under memory constraints — outperforms neural compaction methods that just minimize divergence from full-context attention. A step toward neural memory beyond hand-tuned heuristics. _Source: @chrmanning ([post](https://x.com/chrmanning/status/2047102064183202133)), @StanfordAILab ([post](https://x.com/StanfordAILab/status/2047365082435490280)), @novasarc01 ([post](https://x.com/novasarc01/status/2047375282575995146))_
- **Multi-turn LLM degradation**: LLMs systematically "get lost" in multi-turn conversations — performance degrades as dialogue continues. ICLR 2026 Best Paper, with direct implications for conversational-agent design. _Source: @MSFTResearch ([post](https://x.com/MSFTResearch/status/2047354622377906505))_
- **Self-play plateaus for LLMs**: Long-run self-play plateaus like RL does, explaining why it hasn't produced superhuman LLMs the way it did for Go. A new self-play algorithm scales better — a 7B model can solve as many problems as pass@4 of a model 100x larger. _Source: @tatsu_hashimoto ([post](https://x.com/tatsu_hashimoto/status/2047348673278038403))_
- **Edge-cloud "commit-and-continue"**: Tiny on-device µLMs (8M–30M params) generate the first 4–8 words of a response in ~55 ms, then a cloud LLM seamlessly continues — masking cloud latency and enabling near-instant context-grounded AI on wearables. _Source: @LukeZettlemoyer ([post](https://x.com/LukeZettlemoyer/status/2046982704713937160))_
- **Test-time scaling for coding agents**: Better representations, selection, and reuse of agentic trajectories let Claude 4.5 Opus improve by +6.7% on SWE-Bench Verified and +12.1% on Terminal-Bench 2.0. _Source: @LukeZettlemoyer ([post](https://x.com/LukeZettlemoyer/status/2047322058225324103))_
- **RL beats frontier models on MLE**: A small model trained with RL for machine-learning-engineering tasks outperforms a frontier model — targeted RL on a narrow agentic task can beat general-purpose scale. _Source: @stanfordnlp ([post](https://x.com/stanfordnlp/status/2046972757091713268))_
- **AutoMetrics from sparse feedback**: With <100 human feedback points, you can auto-generate evaluation metrics for subjective AI tasks that beat hand-crafted LLM-as-a-Judge rubrics — useful when human evaluation is costly. _Source: @StanfordAILab ([post](https://x.com/StanfordAILab/status/2047359550366572625))_
- **Diversity-encouraging RL**: A mathematically sound method for encouraging diversity during RL on language models improves both pass@1 and pass@k, and notably increases out-of-distribution generalization. _Source: @StanfordAILab ([post](https://x.com/StanfordAILab/status/2047364702376960489))_
- **SAW-INT4 quantization**: A new INT4 scheme achieves near-BF16 accuracy while keeping the end-to-end speed and memory benefits of INT4 inference. _Source: @tri_dao ([post](https://x.com/tri_dao/status/2047016249973170225))_
- **Pretraining is still the cake**: Recent strong models (Spud, Mythos) reinforce that pretraining dominates and RL is the cherry — RL alone cannot substitute for a strong base model. _Source: @Yuchenj_UW ([post](https://x.com/Yuchenj_UW/status/2047402424605032610))_
- **LeWorldModel**: World models often freeze a pretrained ViT (e.g., DINO-WM) so the encoder can't co-adapt with the predictor. LeWorldModel learns physics from pixels end-to-end with just two losses, producing stable world models. _Source: @ylecun ([post](https://x.com/ylecun/status/2047367559133978733))_
- **Skala DFT functional**: A deep-learned exchange-correlation functional for DFT achieves 2.8 kcal/mol on GMTKN55, wins 32/55 subsets, and surpasses SOTA hybrids in accuracy at semi-local computational cost. _Source: @MSFTResearch ([post](https://x.com/MSFTResearch/status/2046932801740779805))_
- **Speculative decoding leaves tokens on the floor**: Standard speculative decoding discards draft tokens when they don't match the target. Recent work explores keeping/reusing those tokens to boost throughput further. _Source: @charles_irl ([post](https://x.com/charles_irl/status/2047315799094734902))_
- **GEPA + DSPy for AI safety**: Prompt-optimized monitors using GEPA+DSPy achieve ~90% safety with only a 1% audit budget vs. baseline monitors — prompt optimization as a lever for AI control research. _Source: @lateinteraction ([post](https://x.com/lateinteraction/status/2047406456748593424))_

## Key Takeaways

- **The MoE + RL post-training stack is the new default, and it's being pushed to hardware peak.** Since DeepSeek V3, frontier open models are sparse MoEs; pretraining remains the cake with RL as the cherry. New kernels (SonicMoE, DeepSeek TileLang) are landing 35–54% TFLOPS gains over DeepGEMM and saving >2× activation memory on Qwen3-235B-A22B. _Source: @LambdaAPI ([post](https://x.com/LambdaAPI/status/2047027149102891304)), @Yuchenj_UW ([post](https://x.com/Yuchenj_UW/status/2047402424605032610)), @tri_dao ([post](https://x.com/tri_dao/status/2047015011365904639))_
- **Single-number model comparisons are obsolete.** Frontier intelligence is now a function of inference compute — compare models on intelligence-per-token or per-dollar, not leaderboard rank. _Source: @polynoamial ([post](https://x.com/polynoamial/status/2047387675762802998))_
- **Distributed training is going global and fault-tolerant.** DeepMind's Decoupled DiLoCo enables pre-training across geographically distributed datacenters with heterogeneous hardware and no system halts on failure — a structural shift in how frontier models can be trained. _Source: @JeffDean ([post](https://x.com/JeffDean/status/2047339995682529313)), @novasarc01 ([post](https://x.com/novasarc01/status/2047345909382660143))_

- **Open-source AI is a value-capture trap — so far.** DeepSeek invented GRPO, saved the industry billions, and is now negotiating a $20B valuation on minimal revenue — a stark live experiment in whether open-sourcing core IP is commercially viable for frontier labs. _Source: @bryancsk ([post](https://x.com/bryancsk/status/2047114051327803779)), @seconds_0 ([post](https://x.com/seconds_0/status/2047128842868023754))_
