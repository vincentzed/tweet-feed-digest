# Learnings - 2026-05-07 00:46

## AI / Machine Learning

- **Model Spec Midtraining (MSM)**: Standard alignment trains AIs on examples of desired behavior, but this can fail to generalize to new situations. MSM adds a phase that first teaches an AI about its constitution/spec and *why* certain behaviors are wanted, shaping how subsequent alignment training generalizes. _Source: @AnthropicAI ([post](https://x.com/AnthropicAI/status/2051758528562364902))_
- **MSM generalization is spec-dependent**: A toy demo trained an AI only to say it likes certain cheeses. With a spec explaining the preferences via "pro-America" values, the model learned broad pro-America values; swapping in a "pro-affordability" spec produced affordability-aligned behavior — showing that the *justifying* spec, not just examples, drives generalization. _Source: @AnthropicAI ([post](https://x.com/AnthropicAI/status/2051758532869910872))_
- **Alignment ≠ just safety**: Mechanisms like Anthropic's Model Spec Midtraining can be used to enforce any model characteristic — correctness, accuracy, style — not just safety guardrails. _Source: @atbeme ([post](https://x.com/atbeme/status/2051820518450373062))_
- **Weight-based interpretability**: Goodfire is decomposing a language model's weights rather than its activations. The decomposition natively handles attention and behaves less like a lookup table and more like a generalizing algorithm. _Source: @leedsharkey ([post](https://x.com/leedsharkey/status/2051717264286609516))_
- **Dataset cleaning kills loss spikes**: GPT-3's training had loss spikes traced back to a scraped subreddit of microwave noises (raw text like "mmmmmmmmm"). Removing that data eliminated the spikes — a concrete case of why pretraining-data hygiene matters. _Source: @gabriberton ([post](https://x.com/gabriberton/status/2051873677998956851)), @hu_yifei ([post](https://x.com/hu_yifei/status/2051697991493583200))_
- **Look at your data**: Whether evals are worse, better, or as expected — actually inspect the data. Most ML problems (variance, loss spikes, generalization) are first a "look at your data" problem, not a math problem. _Source: @josh_wills ([posts](https://x.com/josh_wills/status/2051673286397022284), [post](https://x.com/josh_wills/status/2051796997028577400))_
- **The curse of multilinguality**: Anthropic's training mix went all-in on English and code, which trades off non-English performance — visible in cross-lingual benchmarks. _Source: @gordic_aleksa ([post](https://x.com/gordic_aleksa/status/2052106700933755294))_
- **Open-weight vs open-source vs open-development**: Restaurant analogy from Percy Liang — open-weight (e.g. DeepSeek): the dish is delivered but the kitchen is closed; open-source (e.g. OLMo): full recipe and ingredients; open-development (e.g. Marin): you can watch the cooking happen. Useful taxonomy when discussing "open" models. _Source: @stanfordnlp ([post](https://x.com/stanfordnlp/status/2052041231400829177))_
- **DPO ≈ SFT, not RL**: Direct Preference Optimization is substantially more similar to supervised fine-tuning than to reinforcement learning, despite often being grouped with RL methods. _Source: @ArthurConmy ([post](https://x.com/ArthurConmy/status/2051648364442202262))_
- **GRPO variant: distribution-deviation reweighting**: Re-weighting the advantage toward tokens that deviate most from the original model's distribution is a promising tweak to GRPO; tricks are needed to make it stable. _Source: @f14bertolotti ([post](https://x.com/f14bertolotti/status/2051895674254373026))_
- **Token-trajectory geometry encodes uncertainty**: New ICML 2026 work shows the shape of internal token trajectories in an LLM has a direct geometric link to behavioral uncertainty (output entropy) — a route to model confidence without external calibration. _Source: @eghbal_hosseini ([post](https://x.com/eghbal_hosseini/status/2052112511105982643))_
- **Train fp4 from scratch to prevent inference cheating**: Modern model devs train mixed-fp4 quants from scratch so inference providers can't cheat with further quantization — quality falls apart if you push lower. _Source: @teortaxesTex ([post](https://x.com/teortaxesTex/status/2051717949682032834))_
- **Diffusion-style speculative decoding (DFlash) on TPUs**: Combining diffusion-style drafting with speculative decoding produces a 3.13× LLM-inference speedup on Google Cloud TPUs, breaking the autoregressive bottleneck. _Source: @haozhangml ([post](https://x.com/haozhangml/status/2051786528448549234))_
- **Gemma 4 MTP speculative decoding**: Novel variant of multi-token-prediction speculative decoding that shares the KV cache with the target model and uses a centroid-masked logits head — distinct from traditional Eagle/MTP. _Source: @ying11231 ([post](https://x.com/ying11231/status/2051701992880545886))_
- **MoE + speculative decoding idea**: Since MoE experts live on different GPUs, you could route a request to expert X on GPU X, hash to GPU Y, and run speculative decoding in parallel on Y — exploiting otherwise-idle GPUs for lower latency than Medusa-style heads on a single GPU. _Source: @shreybirmiwal ([post](https://x.com/shreybirmiwal/status/2051902016893469132))_
- **VLMaxxing skips static pixels in video VLMs**: Continuous-video agents (computer use, robotics, static scenes) waste compute re-ingesting pixels that didn't move. VLMaxxing teaches a frozen video VLM to skip reruns — 54 fps on Gemma 4 26B, training-free, no accuracy drift. _Source: @swyx ([post](https://x.com/swyx/status/2052029093793902613))_
- **MLA inference on Blackwell (TokenSpeed)**: TokenSpeed rebuilt the LLM inference stack end-to-end (kernel → scheduler → modeling → frontend) in <2 months to ship the fastest open-source MLA attention on Blackwell for agentic workloads, claiming TensorRT-LLM-level performance with vLLM-level usability. _Source: @zhyncs42 ([post](https://x.com/zhyncs42/status/2052058547698184413))_
- **GB300 NVL72 vs B200**: SGLang disaggregation on GB300 NVL72 with @deepseek_ai's MegaMoe kernels (which fully fuse and overlap EP dispatch, EP combine, and the GEMMs into a single kernel) achieves up to 6.5× better performance than B200 on DeepSeekv4 Pro 1.6T. _Source: @lm_zheng ([post](https://x.com/lm_zheng/status/2051479670844170428))_
- **ZAYA1-8B reasoning MoE**: Zyphra's release pairs <1B active parameters with bigger experts (the opposite of DeepSeek-MoE), a more expressive router, compressed attention (different scheme than MLA), residual scaling, and post-training that includes test-time compute — and is trained on AMD hardware. _Source: @teortaxesTex ([post](https://x.com/teortaxesTex/status/2052106600882528326)), @eliebakouch ([post](https://x.com/eliebakouch/status/2052126118891729148)), @rawsh0 ([post](https://x.com/rawsh0/status/2052124438263714255))_
- **Mooncake distributed KV cache for agentic workloads**: In production agentic traces, contexts grow to 80K+ tokens by turn 30 with 131:1 input-to-output ratios and 94%+ reusable prefixes; a single session's KV cache can reach multiple GB, and routers migrate sessions so the next instance starts cold. Integrating Mooncake Store as a distributed KV cache recovers reuse across instances. _Source: @vllm_project ([post](https://x.com/vllm_project/status/2052113331927060840)), @woosuk_k ([post](https://x.com/woosuk_k/status/2052122964926362084))_
- **Sparse-attention efficiency**: With query-dependent sparse attention you can decide which keys to load via an indexer (similar to DSA's lightning indexer) and only fetch the KV cache for the selected positions, sidestepping the dense load. _Source: @eliebakouch ([post](https://x.com/eliebakouch/status/2051768141340872868))_
- **Dense attention memory scaling**: Dense attention with Flash Attention also has linear scaling memory — a reminder when comparing against sparse alternatives. _Source: @SonglinYang4 ([post](https://x.com/SonglinYang4/status/2051827227097239819))_
- **You can know what to attend to before running attention**: ANN indices and late-interaction indices have always done sub-linear retrieval before computing attention scores; this is a viable architectural primitive, not a contradiction. _Source: @lateinteraction ([post](https://x.com/lateinteraction/status/2051782809916780550))_
- **Late interaction beats dense retrievers at small scale**: A 0.1B-parameter late interaction model (LateOn from LightOn) competes with much larger dense retrievers like Gemini-class embeddings — retrieval is about signal and architecture, not raw power. _Source: @lateinteraction ([post](https://x.com/lateinteraction/status/2052095891100631500))_
- **PyLate 1.5.0**: Adds XTR training and WARP indexes for late-interaction retrieval; the WARP index runs on GPU and shines when models are trained with the XTR procedure. _Source: @lateinteraction ([post](https://x.com/lateinteraction/status/2051670187175215609))_
- **OBLIQ-Bench raises the IR ceiling**: After ~6–7 years of slowed progress in information retrieval (post-MS MARCO/BEIR), OBLIQ-Bench is positioned as the most ambitious retrieval benchmark to date, designed to surface real headroom for retriever research. _Source: @lateinteraction ([post](https://x.com/lateinteraction/status/2052055143038713875))_
- **cuDNN opens 20+ MoE + NSA sparse-attention kernels**: After 12 years of being closed-source (apart from headers), cuDNN has open-sourced over 20 MoE kernels and NSA sparse-attention kernels — meaningful for anyone optimizing inference stacks. _Source: @charles_irl ([post](https://x.com/charles_irl/status/2051890786862874720))_
- **"Kittens" GPU VM**: A virtual machine that takes over the whole GPU and enables co-optimization beyond the sequential-kernel model — including fusing entire training runs into a single kernel. _Source: @arb8020 ([post](https://x.com/arb8020/status/2052111827333702058))_
- **XGrammar2**: Structural-tag abstraction for dynamic agent tool calling; scales to 500+ strictly-typed tools and is integrated into vLLM, SGLang, and TensorRT-LLM. _Source: @BeidiChen ([post](https://x.com/BeidiChen/status/2051456924978221521))_
- **Recursive self-improvement via earlier model generations**: Cursor uses earlier Composer models in its autoinstall system to set up dev environments for RL training of the next generation. _Source: @srush_nlp ([post](https://x.com/srush_nlp/status/2052134551020089746))_
- **Scaling law under data repetition**: New study explores how repeated-data training interacts with weight decay — higher weight decay can be beneficial when re-reading the same tokens. _Source: @srush_nlp ([post](https://x.com/srush_nlp/status/2051801300174885325))_
- **ABC time-series generative model**: Any-Subset Autoregressive Diffusion Bridges generate continuous-time, continuous-space time series — bridging diffusion and autoregressive paradigms. _Source: @StefanoErmon ([post](https://x.com/StefanoErmon/status/2052062727561216214))_
- **RAE for image generation**: SOTA image gen by denoising frozen 768D DINOv2 latents; key ingredients are a wider model and a dimension-dependent noise schedule shift to handle the higher effective signal-to-noise ratio of high-dim latents. _Source: @gabriberton ([post](https://x.com/gabriberton/status/2052178811899597024))_
- **Diffusion models are Markovian**: They only know their current state, not the trajectory — recent work shows that adding memory/history conditioning improves dynamics modeling. _Source: @NaveenGRao ([post](https://x.com/NaveenGRao/status/2052116728860348717))_
- **Continual Learning Bench 1.0**: First realistic benchmark for measuring how AI systems improve in online settings. Most existing benchmarks treat models as stateless and discard prior interactions; this one explicitly tests learning across tasks. _Source: @tyler_griggs_ ([post](https://x.com/tyler_griggs_/status/2051524377221689497))_
- **NeuralBench v1.0**: Open-source unified framework to benchmark NeuroAI models — 36 EEG tasks, 94 datasets, with MEG/fMRI support coming, MIT-licensed. _Source: @ylecun ([post](https://x.com/ylecun/status/2052130032244392427))_
- **LLM-from-scratch coding benchmark**: New benchmark by @jyangballin et al. asks how much of SQLite, FFmpeg, or PHP an LM can rebuild from scratch given just an executable and no starter code — Anthropic models lead, illustrating the gap among frontier labs on long-horizon code synthesis. _Source: @eisokant ([post](https://x.com/eisokant/status/2051916125441597513)), @MatternJustus ([post](https://x.com/MatternJustus/status/2052054372755157103))_
- **SWE benchmark validity bug**: In swe-rebench v2, repos with split frontend/backend (e.g. statamic/cms) can score correct fixes even when only the unrelated frontend test suite runs — illustrating why test-suite coverage matters in agentic-coding evals. _Source: @rasdani_ ([post](https://x.com/rasdani_/status/2052061864968810730))_
- **Vendor-verifier tools matter for inference**: When benchmarks of a model look "off," it's often the inference provider, not the model — tools like K2 vendor verifier compare official APIs to third-party providers to detect divergence. _Source: @eliebakouch ([post](https://x.com/eliebakouch/status/2051873970505334853))_
- **Positional encoding pedagogy**: The original transformer's use of both sines and cosines for positional encoding is often invoked as a "clever" detail — but understanding *why* (sinusoidal pairs let the model represent relative offsets as linear functions of position) is a real test of transformer literacy. Note: jeremyphoward pushes back that calling it "very clever" reveals more about the speaker than the math. _Source: @allTheYud ([post](https://x.com/allTheYud/status/2051512098287747500)), @jeremyphoward ([post](https://x.com/jeremyphoward/status/2051687975520711049))_
- **Negative results are hard because failure has many causes**: Successful experiments require everything to go right (one explanation), while failures admit many explanations — which is one structural reason research culture under-publishes negatives. _Source: @xiao_ted ([post](https://x.com/xiao_ted/status/2051842812262703526))_
- **First-person video as a robotics scaling axis**: Sensorized first-person human video may be the data path that gives robotics a true scaling law with manageable cost — a bet emerging from Jim Fan's Sequoia talk. _Source: @BillSun_AI ([post](https://x.com/BillSun_AI/status/2051877451341697115))_
- **MolmoAct 2 (VLA)**: Allen AI's robotics model couples a spatial VLM backbone to a continuous action expert with per-layer KV conditioning; runs up to 37× faster than v1, handles two-armed tasks without per-task fine-tuning, and reasons in 3D before acting — fully open weights, training code, action tokenizer, plus the largest open bimanual robotics dataset of its kind. _Source: @allen_ai ([posts](https://x.com/allen_ai/status/2051708880455868501), [post](https://x.com/allen_ai/status/2051728835658256826), [post](https://x.com/allen_ai/status/2051774632449966082))_

## Software Engineering

- **AWS AZ identifiers are randomized per account**: `us-east-1a`/`1b`/`1c` are *not* globally consistent — what is "1a" in one account can be "1c" in another. AWS does this to balance load across actual physical zones, so cross-account capacity planning by AZ name is misleading. _Source: @jamesacowling ([post](https://x.com/jamesacowling/status/2052127668213272743))_
- **CUDA IPC Pool Handle Cache**: SGLang added a pool/cache for CUDA IPC handles to speed cross-process GPU memory sharing — a great example that inference-engine perf wins don't always require deep GPU arcana. _Source: @lm_zheng ([post](https://x.com/lm_zheng/status/2051479721645576335))_
- **`squeue` is unpronounceable**: The Slurm queue command is a perennial trap — useful trivia for anyone joining an HPC team. _Source: @soldni ([post](https://x.com/soldni/status/2051822728282624001))_
- **GitHub still lacks a "hide whitespace" keyboard shortcut**: A workflow gap worth knowing if you review a lot of diffs; you'll need to reach for the URL `?w=1` parameter. _Source: @julien_c ([post](https://x.com/julien_c/status/2052029811833323735))_
- **Async agents and "higher-order prompts"**: Agentic engineering increasingly looks like functional programming — routines are higher-order prompts, and the default pattern is "I will now have Claude prompt Claude Code." Verification matters more as more work goes async. _Source: @swyx ([post](https://x.com/swyx/status/2052068086019457341))_
- **Doom's networking lineage**: Doom shipped with serial, modem, and IPX code (Carmack-authored) — but no internet play; modem multiplayer was the third-party DWANGO service. A reminder that "we played online in 1993" usually means LAN/IPX or modem-via-DWANGO, not the public internet. _Source: @ID_AA_Carmack ([post](https://x.com/ID_AA_Carmack/status/2051719561460740469)), @SandyofCthulhu ([post](https://x.com/SandyofCthulhu/status/2051686324374241614))_
- **Chrome's Prompt API ships against the standards process**: Mozilla and WebKit opposed, Microsoft and the W3C TAG raised concerns, developer sentiment was mostly negative — Chrome shipped anyway. A live example of how multi-stakeholder web standards can be bypassed by a single dominant implementer. _Source: @jaffathecake ([post](https://x.com/jaffathecake/status/2051962549545963951))_
- **Windows input-lag fixes**: Default Windows pointer acceleration plus USB selective-suspend power saving causes inconsistent tracking and random input-lag spikes. Setting acceleration to 0/0 thresholds (raw input) and disabling USB sleep removes both. _Source: @ParagonTweaks ([post](https://x.com/ParagonTweaks/status/2051333185087086663))_
- **Sandboxes/VMs as agent-native git**: When LLMs generate and modify code at scale, version control of *environment state* (sandboxes/VMs) becomes more important than text-diff version control — a candidate for the next durable infra primitive. _Source: @confusedqubit ([post](https://x.com/confusedqubit/status/2051842785951842484))_
- **Homebrew is a supply-chain attack waiting to happen**: macOS developers running `brew install` execute arbitrary maintainer-controlled scripts with the user's privileges; the trust model is fragile. Worth treating brew taps and casks with the same scrutiny as `npm install`. _Source: @gf_256 ([post](https://x.com/gf_256/status/2052106143271354859))_

## Systems & Infrastructure

- **OpenAI's Multipath Reliable Connection (MRC)**: AI supercomputers need new networks to keep tens-of-thousands of chips in sync. OpenAI (with AMD, Broadcom, Intel, Microsoft, NVIDIA) released MRC: a multiplane 800GbE design (2 tiers, 131k endpoints), a custom protocol extending RoCE for packet spraying, and a switch from dynamic to static routing using SRv6 — a step toward open systems research as deep-learning supercomputers grow. _Source: @OpenAI ([post](https://x.com/OpenAI/status/2052039800384057348)), @JordanNanos ([post](https://x.com/JordanNanos/status/2052053987365671364)), @trevorycai ([post](https://x.com/trevorycai/status/2052058032243372157)), @timzaman ([post](https://x.com/timzaman/status/2052055041880772883))_
- **FHE was memory-bound, not compute-bound**: Fully homomorphic encryption was invented in the 1980s but had a ~100,000× slowdown that blocked adoption. CipherSonic AI brought it under 2× by attacking the *memory-boundedness* of the workload — the same root cause that limits LLM inference. _Source: @cerebras ([post](https://x.com/cerebras/status/2052127605235851364))_
- **TokenSpeed (open-source inference engine)**: MIT-licensed engine claiming TensorRT-LLM-level performance with vLLM-level usability, built in two months. vLLM is the day-0 launch partner via the integrated MLA library, optimized for long-context multi-turn agentic workloads on Kimi 2.5/2.6 and DeepSeek R1 on NVIDIA. _Source: @lightseekorg ([post](https://x.com/lightseekorg/status/2052048105412141376)), @vllm_project ([post](https://x.com/vllm_project/status/2052051210530914510))_
- **Perplexity ROSE + CuTeDSL**: Perplexity built ROSE (Runtime-Optimized Serving Engine) covering embeddings to trillion-parameter LLMs, with CuTeDSL integrated so they can author specialized GPU kernels faster as new models ship. _Source: @perplexity_ai ([post](https://x.com/perplexity_ai/status/2052041903970148647))_
- **Inference is the production cost driver**: Inference accounts for 80–90% of the lifetime cost of a production AI system — most teams leave performance and margin on the table by under-optimizing it. _Source: @togethercompute ([post](https://x.com/togethercompute/status/2051689626708983904))_
- **Compute capacity is the binding constraint at the frontier**: Anthropic's deal with SpaceX/xAI takes ALL of Colossus 1's capacity, not just idle slack, and unlocks 300+ MW of new capacity (~220,000 NVIDIA GPUs) within the month — an unusually concrete signal of how scarce frontier compute really is. _Source: @eliebakouch ([post](https://x.com/eliebakouch/status/2052066609896808473)), @ClaudeDevs ([post](https://x.com/ClaudeDevs/status/2052064938840228237)), @_arohan_ ([post](https://x.com/_arohan_/status/2052065871552819647))_
- **Compute is hard to overbuild**: Capital is the easy part; you also need GPUs, power, substations, colo, cooling, and operators — each link has its own lead time, so a capital bubble doesn't automatically translate to a compute bubble. _Source: @carmenli ([post](https://x.com/carmenli/status/2052075418795012435))_
- **Bottleneck dynamics**: If demand growth slows, every supply bottleneck (GPUs, power, real estate) tends to clear simultaneously — important when reasoning about compute glut scenarios. _Source: @bernhardsson ([post](https://x.com/bernhardsson/status/2052110595172802754))_
- **Datacenters in space**: Terrestrial datacenters are increasingly bottlenecked by permitted real-estate space, motivating proposals for orbital compute. _Source: @itsclivetime ([post](https://x.com/itsclivetime/status/2052135729086845079))_
- **GPU utilization wins are real ROI**: Multiverse Computing doubled GPU utilization across 1,000+ GPUs by orchestrating with SkyPilot — no new hardware required. _Source: @skypilot_org ([post](https://x.com/skypilot_org/status/2051712620361904281))_
- **Apple Silicon for local ML**: A corporate M4 Max laptop runs a local voice transcription model 4× faster than an M2 Max — on-device AI is becoming genuinely usable on premium consumer hardware. _Source: @ClementDelangue ([post](https://x.com/ClementDelangue/status/2051475752613183613))_
- **DNSSEC has serious problems**: Long-running internet-protocol critique — DNSSEC's complexity-to-benefit ratio is poor enough that engineers are openly questioning whether to retire it. _Source: @mitsuhiko ([post](https://x.com/mitsuhiko/status/2051934997368447472))_

## Key Takeaways

1. **Compute is the choke point — and it's supply-chain-bound, not capital-bound**: Anthropic taking *all* of Colossus 1, unlocking ~220,000 GPUs in a month, and immediately raising Claude Code limits makes frontier compute the binding constraint. But money arrives faster than GPUs, substations, colo, cooling, and operators — so the "overbuild" thesis is harder to act on than it sounds, and if demand growth slows every bottleneck clears at once. _Sources: @eliebakouch ([post](https://x.com/eliebakouch/status/2052066609896808473)), @skorusARK ([post](https://x.com/skorusARK/status/2052102247970955719)), @_arohan_ ([post](https://x.com/_arohan_/status/2052065871552819647)), @carmenli ([post](https://x.com/carmenli/status/2052075418795012435)), @bernhardsson ([post](https://x.com/bernhardsson/status/2052110595172802754))_
2. **Inference is where the money and the engineering go**: Inference is 80–90% of production AI lifetime cost, and the stack is moving fast — RadixArk's $100M seed for SGLang, TokenSpeed's Blackwell-optimized MLA engine, vLLM × Mooncake for distributed KV, OpenAI's MRC networking, and 6.5× perf gains from GB300+SGLang on DeepSeekv4. _Sources: @togethercompute ([post](https://x.com/togethercompute/status/2051689626708983904)), @radixark ([post](https://x.com/radixark/status/2051655994921595219)), @zhyncs42 ([post](https://x.com/zhyncs42/status/2052058547698184413)), @lm_zheng ([post](https://x.com/lm_zheng/status/2051479670844170428))_
3. **MSM teaches *why*, not just *what***: Anthropic's Model Spec Midtraining shows alignment generalizes better when you train the model on the constitution and its rationale before showing examples of compliant behavior — and the same mechanism can enforce correctness, accuracy, or style, not just safety. _Source: @AnthropicAI ([post](https://x.com/AnthropicAI/status/2051758528562364902)), @atbeme ([post](https://x.com/atbeme/status/2051820518450373062))_
4. **Memory-bound is the unifying performance story**: FHE went from 100,000× to <2× slowdown by attacking memory-boundedness — the same root cause that limits LLM inference. This explains why kernel- and serving-stack work (open-sourced cuDNN MoE kernels, ROSE+CuTeDSL, TokenSpeed, MRC, Mooncake KV reuse, VLMaxxing skipping static pixels) is where the next wins live. _Sources: @cerebras ([post](https://x.com/cerebras/status/2052127605235851364)), @charles_irl ([post](https://x.com/charles_irl/status/2051890786862874720)), @swyx ([post](https://x.com/swyx/status/2052029093793902613)), @vllm_project ([post](https://x.com/vllm_project/status/2052113331927060840))_
5. **Look at your data, and clean it before scaling**: GPT-3's loss spikes vanished after removing one noisy subreddit; most ML problems (variance, loss spikes, generalization) are dataset problems first and math problems second. _Source: @josh_wills ([posts](https://x.com/josh_wills/status/2051673286397022284), [post](https://x.com/josh_wills/status/2051796997028577400)), @gabriberton ([post](https://x.com/gabriberton/status/2051873677998956851)), @hu_yifei ([post](https://x.com/hu_yifei/status/2051697991493583200))_
