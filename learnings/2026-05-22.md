# Learnings - 2026-05-22 14:32

## AI / Machine Learning

- **Native Multimodal Models (NMMs)**: A Meta paper argues future multimodal LLMs will drop separate vision encoders and process all modalities natively — more elegant and potentially higher-quality than modular designs, but data-hungry, and for very long-context tasks they need a *dynamic* compressor (compression rate adapts to content) rather than a fixed encoder. _Source: @gabriberton ([post](https://x.com/gabriberton/status/2056988286821032174))_
- **Gated DeltaNet-2 (NVIDIA Labs)**: A new linear-attention architecture that *decouples erase and write* — prior delta-rule models (Gated DeltaNet, Kimi Delta Attention) tied both to a single scalar gate, even though erasing acts on key-side and writing on value-side coordinates. Channel-wise erase/write gates beat KDA and Mamba-3 head-to-head at 1.3B (100B FineWeb-Edu tokens), with the biggest gains in long-context retrieval (RULER S-NIAH-3 jumps 63→90 over KDA). _Source: @jeremyphoward ([post](https://x.com/jeremyphoward/status/2057627196223857050))_
- **Parallel transformer block (Cohere Command A+)**: Computing attention and the MoE/FFN in parallel rather than sequentially gives equivalent quality but significantly higher throughput, because the feed-forward transform is independent along the sequence axis and needs no activation communication. Command A+ is a 218B-total / 25B-active sparse MoE that runs on as few as two H100s, with an unusually large query dimension (~4× hidden), released under Apache 2.0. _Source: @rasbt ([post](https://x.com/rasbt/status/2057241574161932339)), @eliebakouch ([post](https://x.com/eliebakouch/status/2057198733759008989))_
- **MoE shared/routed-expert ratios are still unsettled**: Analysis of the same 218B/A22B model (128 experts, 8 active; configs disagreed on 1 vs 4 shared) flags that activating 4 shared + 8 routed is unusually high — most research (e.g. "Towards Greater Leverage") favors *one* or minimal shared experts, with an open argument that shared-expert *size* should be decoupled from count. Also notable: 128 attention heads with GQA. _Source: @stochasticchasm ([post](https://x.com/stochasticchasm/status/2057150551696261607))_
- **ReLoRA (stacking low-rank updates)**: You can approximate full-rank fine-tuning by repeatedly training a LoRA adapter, merging it into the weights, then training a fresh LoRA on top. Each update is low-rank, but a sum of low-rank updates can reach full rank — better results than a single LoRA while keeping each step cheap. _Source: @rosmine ([post](https://x.com/rosmine/status/2057226528950305083))_
- **Post-training is what makes a model useful**: A base model is essentially autocomplete; the post-training pipeline (SFT, preference optimization, etc.) is what turns it into something that can follow instructions and do tasks. _Source: @liquidai ([post](https://x.com/liquidai/status/2057477723539599523))_
- **"A Bitter Lesson for Data Filtering"**: New scaling studies (Mohri/Duchi/Hashimoto, arXiv 2605.19407) argue that in the high-compute, data-scarce (effectively infinite-compute) regime the best data filter may be *no* filter — sufficiently large models tolerate and can even benefit from nominally "low-quality" data, directly contradicting the model-based-filtering orthodoxy from the original DCLM work. _Source: @percyliang ([post](https://x.com/percyliang/status/2057642309190296028)), @stanfordnlp ([post](https://x.com/stanfordnlp/status/2057553038911381899))_
- **Today's default hyperparameters have lineage**: Many hyperparameter values now treated as standard became the norm because of the Madry et al. (adversarial robustness) papers — useful context when wondering *why* a default is what it is. _Source: @vipul_1011 ([post](https://x.com/vipul_1011/status/2057544799121559554))_
- **Sampling args can swing eval scores ~10%**: Simply switching to a model's *recommended* sampling parameters produced a 10% relative jump in eval scores — decoding config is a frequently-overlooked benchmarking confound, and misconfigured sampling can silently understate a model. Practitioners report sampling params are getting harder to tune correctly. _Source: @xeophon ([post](https://x.com/xeophon/status/2057566921835401344)), @cwolferesearch ([post](https://x.com/cwolferesearch/status/2057593720128627073))_
- **Good agent evals mirror real users**: Effective evals aren't random benchmark tasks; they encode your priors about likely user behavior so you test the agent on what it will actually face before and after shipping to prod. _Source: @Vtrivedy10 ([post](https://x.com/Vtrivedy10/status/2057175860910964967))_
- **Evals are environments**: For any fuzzy claim about a frontier model's capability, the practical move is "make an eval for it" — turning a vague capability question into a measurable, closed-loop improvement system. _Source: @novasarc01 ([post](https://x.com/novasarc01/status/2057188573502374367))_
- **Don't over-compress your evals**: A 30+ minute agent run over thousands of traces can often be condensed into a smaller subset that still exercises the intended behavior, making iteration affordable — but a shrunken eval can become a clean-room proxy the agent passes *without* fixing the real long-horizon failure mode unless it preserves the original causal mechanism. _Source: @palashshah ([post](https://x.com/palashshah/status/2057199462095667310)), @novasarc01 ([post](https://x.com/novasarc01/status/2057203394482458769)), @arb8020 ([post](https://x.com/arb8020/status/2057215520265633866))_
- **OCR benchmarks are nearly meaningless — so visualize instead**: Numeric OCR benchmarks correlate poorly with real quality, but quality is easy to *grok* visually; a new visualizer overlays what an OCR system catches vs. misses — a more honest eval than a single score. _Source: @soldni ([post](https://x.com/soldni/status/2057209676056719389))_
- **Benchmarks ≠ reality**: Coding-agent leaderboards (e.g. Cursor Composer 2.5 ranking third on the Artificial Analysis index at 10–60× lower cost) can mislead because they cover a narrow task set — "a bit like political polls." Cost-efficiency and headline rank don't equal real-world superiority. _Source: @LouisLsvr ([post](https://x.com/LouisLsvr/status/2057573551712944589))_
- **Small computer-use agents are catching frontier models**: Microsoft's Fara1.5-9B (Qwen3.5-based pixel-to-action browser agent) hits 63.4% on Online-Mind2Web — nearly double its 7B predecessor (34.1%) — while the 27B variant (72%) beats OpenAI Operator (58.3%) and Gemini 2.5 Computer Use (57.3%). Capable computer-use no longer requires huge cloud models; these run an observe-think-act loop over the last few screenshots. _Source: @HsseinMzannar ([post](https://x.com/HsseinMzannar/status/2057538344775565512))_
- **Agent tool ablations**: A clean way to measure what actually helps a coding agent is to ablate its toolset — running Codex with (a) no tools, (b) scratchpad read/write only, (c) code read/write/execute only, and (d) scratchpad + code — to isolate each tool's contribution on ARC-AGI-3. _Source: @GregKamradt ([post](https://x.com/GregKamradt/status/2057486154140917904))_
- **Sandboxes are foundational infrastructure for RL**: Every RL rollout needs its own clean, replayable environment, and that single requirement shapes the rest of the training loop's design — a key reason sandbox/compute platforms matter for post-training. _Source: @modal ([post](https://x.com/modal/status/2057176658684076476))_
- **ColBERT generalizes to unseen languages**: The late-interaction retrieval model transfers to languages it wasn't explicitly trained on — useful for multilingual retrieval without per-language training. _Source: @lateinteraction ([post](https://x.com/lateinteraction/status/2057071133988442184))_
- **CoT reasoning is currently interpretable**: Frontier models are solving long-standing discrete-geometry problems while "thinking" in plain-English chains-of-thought — a property that is striking, easy to inspect, and not guaranteed to persist as architectures change. _Source: @charliermarsh ([post](https://x.com/charliermarsh/status/2057220034112496126))_
- **Capabilities democratize fast**: A reliable pattern — techniques that seem out of reach for anyone but frontier labs quickly collapse into accessible research areas doable with relatively little compute. _Source: @josh_wills ([post](https://x.com/josh_wills/status/2057463206227128404))_
- **Inference-speed frontier (wafer-scale)**: Cerebras is running the trillion-parameter Kimi K2.6 at ~1,000 tokens/s — the fastest frontier-model speed measured by Artificial Analysis — illustrating how wafer-scale hardware changes what "large model" latency looks like. _Source: @Kimi_Moonshot ([post](https://x.com/Kimi_Moonshot/status/2057005904776868040))_

- **Agentic coding is reshaping robotics**: A large multi-lab project ("Agentic Coding for Robotics") shows the same coding-agent skills powering software are making it materially easier to build and deploy robots — robotics may follow a path parallel to frontier-model scaling. _Source: @Ken_Goldberg ([post](https://x.com/Ken_Goldberg/status/2057186210267967847)), @avisingh599 ([post](https://x.com/avisingh599/status/2057185684130234724))_

## Software Engineering

- **Code-review skill that subtracts complexity**: Cursor's most-used internal skill, `/thermo-nuclear-code-quality-review`, enforces concrete rules — delete complexity instead of relocating it, block files over 1k lines, flag thin wrappers and leaked logic, and reject PRs that "work" but leave the codebase messier. A usable checklist for keeping AI-generated code maintainable. _Source: @ericzakariasson ([post](https://x.com/ericzakariasson/status/2057521364622553442))_
- **Cross-agent code review**: Use one agent to write code and a *different* agent to review it — e.g., Claude implements, Gemini reviews and leaves inline comments sent back to the original agent. Different models catch different issues. _Source: @getsome_air ([post](https://x.com/getsome_air/status/2051659753034514505))_
- **Codex vs. Claude Code (thoroughness)**: Hands-on reports describe Codex as more *thorough* — when asked for a change it does a better job understanding the whole system and the different areas a change will touch, rather than just the local edit. _Source: @charliermarsh ([post](https://x.com/charliermarsh/status/2057079677668253739))_
- **See where your tokens go**: In Claude Code, `/usage` breaks down which Skills, Agents, MCPs, and Plugins are consuming your token budget — a practical way to find context-window waste. _Source: @bcherny ([post](https://x.com/bcherny/status/2057476878110261587))_
- **Making computer-use agents reliable**: Production reliability for UI-operating agents comes down to a few concrete levers — click accuracy, choosing the right thinking-effort level, keeping long sessions within the context window, and recording demonstrations the model can replay. _Source: @bcherny ([post](https://x.com/bcherny/status/2057071400343437810))_
- **AI coding adoption at scale**: Spotify reports >99% of engineers use AI coding tools and ships ~4,500 deployments/day, with adoption accelerating after Opus 4.5 — a real-world data point on how fast AI-assisted dev is becoming default. _Source: @bcherny ([post](https://x.com/bcherny/status/2057071805664288917))_
- **Hardening "vibecoded" prototypes**: A long-running agent skill can convert a fragile MVP into a production-ready, e2e-tested, parallelizable repo — one such run went ~16 hours and 103 commits to produce the same app, but maintainable. _Source: @swyx ([post](https://x.com/swyx/status/2057559570177007912))_
- **Micro-optimizations compound**: A targeted "find silly one-line optimizations" pass sped up a parser by 20–30% — small, mechanical wins are still on the table even in mature code. _Source: @charliermarsh ([post](https://x.com/charliermarsh/status/2057450223073464592))_
- **Coding agents now support Windows VMs**: Devin added Windows VM support, extending autonomous agents beyond Linux/macOS to Windows-only desktop workflows. _Source: @anneouyang ([post](https://x.com/anneouyang/status/2057530840532541638))_

## Systems & Infrastructure

- **CODA — the whole Transformer block is GEMM + epilogue**: After an algebraic rewrite, every transformer op reduces to a matmul followed by an epilogue, so memory-bound work (RMSNorm, activations, residuals, reductions, log-sum-exp) can be reparameterized to run *while the GEMM output tile is still on-chip* — hidden in the matmul's shadow, avoiding extra trips through global memory. This is the CODA abstraction (arXiv 2605.19269, Guo/Dao et al.), and it matters more as FP8/NVFP4 make FLOPs cheap and data movement the real bottleneck. Strikingly, with a few composable epilogue primitives even an LLM (or a novice) can author near-speed-of-light CODA/CuTe kernels. _Source: @tri_dao ([post](https://x.com/tri_dao/status/2057640492020469845), [post](https://x.com/tri_dao/status/2057640753090736349)), @jeremyphoward ([post](https://x.com/jeremyphoward/status/2057673229427499463))_
- **Serving stack beats silicon**: Eigen AI topped the Artificial Analysis leaderboard on Kimi K2.6 at 265 tok/s running on **B200, not GB300** — proof that hitting #1 without the newest Blackwell Ultra is "a serving-stack story, not a silicon story." Every layer of the inference stack matters more than raw hardware generation. _Source: @nebiustf ([post](https://x.com/nebiustf/status/2057192574067093925)), @hanrui_w ([post](https://x.com/hanrui_w/status/2057189415072764016))_
- **GPU-cluster deploy time is shrinking ("Elon scaling laws")**: Reported buildout figures — 100k H100 (130 MW) in 122 days, 110k GB200 (210 MW) in 91 days, 110k GB300 (220 MW) in 64 days — show newer generations standing up faster despite higher power draw. A useful mental model for datacenter capacity planning. _Source: @JordanNanos ([post](https://x.com/JordanNanos/status/2057225045119004970))_
- **Elastic Expert Parallelism (vLLM)**: A vLLM MoE deployment's DP/EP topology used to be fixed at launch — resizing meant a full restart that dropped in-flight traffic. Elastic EP resizes a *live* deployment with a single API call (`POST /scale_elastic_ep`), no restart. _Source: @NVIDIAAI ([post](https://x.com/NVIDIAAI/status/2057651742725267508))_
- **PyTorch 2.11 fixes aarch64 GPU installs**: As of PyTorch 2.11.0, CUDA-enabled aarch64 wheels are published to the default PyPI index, so `pip install torch` "just works" on GB200/GB300/GH200 — no more custom index URLs for ARM+GPU. _Source: @woosuk_k ([post](https://x.com/woosuk_k/status/2056985082989261251))_
- **PD-disaggregated SGLang on AMD GPUs**: Prefill/decode-disaggregated inference can now be deployed on multi-node AMD GPU clusters using SGLang + dstack from a single config — a concrete recipe for disaggregated serving outside NVIDIA. _Source: @lmsysorg ([post](https://x.com/lmsysorg/status/2057553547978518913))_
- **ThunderKittens internals**: A deep blog post dissects ThunderKittens bottom-up — what each abstraction in the compact kernel DSL buys you and which hardware detail it hides — a good study path for learning high-performance AI kernel design. _Source: @realDanFu ([post](https://x.com/realDanFu/status/2057654340320694352))_
- **fabric-lib for RDMA in LLM systems**: A new library provides RDMA point-to-point communication primitives tailored for LLM serving/training systems (presented at MLSys 2026). _Source: @tqchenml ([post](https://x.com/tqchenml/status/2057208387285536927))_
- **A minimal ML stack is possible**: tinygrad pitches a ground-up framework that's a ~1MB pip install with zero C++20 (pure Python), arguing the conventional heavyweight C++ approach to ML systems isn't working for many teams. _Source: @__tinygrad__ ([post](https://x.com/__tinygrad__/status/2057498473650598253))_
- **Hardware cost signal**: Suppliers are raising Blackwell card prices by ~$2,200 each, pushing up downstream box prices — a concrete read on GPU supply economics right now. _Source: @__tinygrad__ ([post](https://x.com/__tinygrad__/status/2057535097537233243))_
- **Compute is becoming a service across vendors**: Anthropic is scaling GB200 capacity in xAI's Colossus 2, and SpaceX is offering AI compute as a service at scale (with orbital data centers floated long-term) — frontier labs increasingly rent capacity from whoever has power and silicon. _Source: @_arohan_ ([post](https://x.com/_arohan_/status/2057196810503106789)), @elonmusk ([post](https://x.com/elonmusk/status/2057228707606196434))_
- **The CUDA moat is real — but maybe not for long**: Modal's CEO is bullish on alternative accelerators over a 2–3 year horizon, *despite* currently seeing zero customer demand for TPUs etc., because today's cost equation will shift. _Source: @modal ([post](https://x.com/modal/status/2057642983185400276))_
- **Concurrency-only bugs are real**: Some inference bugs only surface under high concurrency; upstreaming fixes to SGLang required debugging issues that never appear at low load — a reminder to load-test serving stacks at production concurrency. _Source: @lmsysorg ([post](https://x.com/lmsysorg/status/2057558294185853284))_
- **Quantization quality is a buyer's first question**: "Quantize first, ask later" is a poor stance for an inference provider — customers care deeply about *whether* quantization degrades quality, so quality guarantees (not just speed/cost) drive provider selection. _Source: @charles_irl ([post](https://x.com/charles_irl/status/2057343787895279889))_
- **Untangling the CUDA stack**: A useful orientation for GPU-profiling newcomers — CUDA architectures, cuDNN, cuBLAS, and cuBLASlt are distinct layers, and Modal's GPU glossary is a good map of how they relate. _Source: @ariG23498 ([post](https://x.com/ariG23498/status/2057059987198013749))_

## Security

- **Autonomous exploitation is no longer hypothetical**: In ExploitGym, AI agents turned security vulnerabilities into real, working attacks even against complex targets — a concrete benchmark for measuring frontier AI's offensive-cyber impact, and a warning that "find→exploit" automation is now demonstrated. _Source: @berkeley_ai ([post](https://x.com/berkeley_ai/status/2057567247783399688))_
- **Kernel-level anti-cheat can brick hardware**: Riot's Vanguard update blocked DMA-cheat firmware that spoofs SATA/NVMe devices — demonstrating both the power of ring-0 anti-cheat to detect hardware-level cheating and the risk that such software can render a machine unusable. A live lesson in the trade-offs of installing kernel-mode software. _Source: @riotgames ([post](https://x.com/riotgames/status/2057604027941302564)), @Peach_Fightr ([post](https://x.com/Peach_Fightr/status/2057623564690526564))_
- **Agent "skills" are an attack surface**: Skills extend an agent's capabilities but can also introduce vulnerabilities. NVIDIA's verified-skills approach provides provenance and transparency — what a skill does, where it came from, what risks it carries, and whether it's been modified — a model worth applying before installing third-party agent extensions. _Source: @NVIDIAAI ([post](https://x.com/NVIDIAAI/status/2057496919425900834))_
- **The quantum threat is not crypto-specific**: At ACCESS 26, Microsoft framed quantum's scope bluntly — "everything you assume is locked today will be unlocked by quantum." This is a general cryptography problem (all classically-encrypted data), not just a blockchain/crypto-asset issue, raising the urgency of post-quantum migration. _Source: @HalbornSecurity ([post](https://x.com/HalbornSecurity/status/2055302191318024679))_
- **Phishing recognition basics**: Common phishing tells — urgency, mismatched sender/links, and requests for credentials — remain the front-line defense against email-based attacks. _Source: @NordVPN ([post](https://x.com/NordVPN/status/2038277752718274843))_

## Business & Strategy

## Science & Research

- **AI autonomously disproved an 80-year-old Erdős conjecture**: A *general-purpose* OpenAI reasoning model — not math-specialized, not scaffolded, not targeted at the problem — refuted Erdős's 1946 planar unit-distance conjecture, constructing point arrangements yielding at least n^(1+δ) unit-distance pairs and beating the long-assumed-optimal square-grid (Gaussian-integer) bound. It reached the result via algebraic number theory and infinite class field towers; Princeton's Will Sawin pinned the improvement at δ ≈ 0.014 (≈ n^1.014) and Fields Medalist Tim Gowers called it "a milestone in AI mathematics." This appears to be the first prominent open problem in a math subfield solved largely autonomously by AI (peer review pending) — and the rate of progress (IMO-gold ~a year ago) is the real story. _Source: @polynoamial ([post](https://x.com/polynoamial/status/2057178198228586824), [post](https://x.com/polynoamial/status/2057179104315670826), [post](https://x.com/polynoamial/status/2057554973987102778)), @_aidan_clark_ ([post](https://x.com/_aidan_clark_/status/2057180858017313253)), @eliebakouch ([post](https://x.com/eliebakouch/status/2057200390353846321)), @novasarc01 ([post](https://x.com/novasarc01/status/2057195423857852626)), @memecrashes ([post](https://x.com/memecrashes/status/2057478155246440929))_
- **AI math results don't require formal provers**: Notably, this proof did *not* use Lean or any formal-verification scaffold — pushing back on the assumption that AI mathematical breakthroughs must be machine-checked to count. _Source: @polynoamial ([post](https://x.com/polynoamial/status/2057216766221115562))_
- **Crys-JEPA for materials discovery**: A joint-embedding predictive architecture for crystals learns an *energy-aware* latent space and uses it as a *screening filter before generation*, rather than asking a generative model to self-regulate. This breaks the stability–novelty trade-off (the "useful region" of stable-and-novel crystals is extremely narrow), improving the V.S.U.N. metric by up to ~82% over baselines like MatterGen on MP-20 — part of a broader shift from likelihood-maximization to multi-objective optimization in scientific generative AI. _Source: @ylecun ([post](https://x.com/ylecun/status/2057474214693834963))_
- **Lagrangian Flow Matching**: A new generative-modeling approach grounds the transport of probability mass from noise to data in the *principle of least action* (classical mechanics), connecting flow matching to Lagrangian dynamics. _Source: @JunzheZhang12 ([post](https://x.com/JunzheZhang12/status/2057283661716750632))_
- **Race-neutral neuroimaging models**: A new PNAS paper shows "CentileBrain" race-neutral models maintain robust accuracy across ethnoracially diverse samples (defined by both self-report and genetic ancestry) — progress toward brain-based clinical tools that generalize equitably rather than encoding demographic bias. _Source: @Frangougroup ([post](https://x.com/Frangougroup/status/2054266250021233147))_
- **Reversing accelerated aging in dogs**: Loyal (founder Celine Halioua) is running the largest veterinary clinical trial ever to extend dog lifespans — addressing the fact that selective breeding inadvertently shortened many dogs' healthy lifespans. A notable real-world longevity-biotech trial at scale. _Source: @abundanceinst ([post](https://x.com/abundanceinst/status/1986127622800130235))_

## Key Takeaways

- **A general-purpose AI model autonomously disproved an 80-year-old Erdős conjecture** in discrete geometry — with no math-specific training, no scaffold, and no formal prover — using algebraic number theory no human had connected to the problem, marking arguably the first AI-autonomous resolution of a prominent open problem in a math subfield. _Source: @polynoamial ([post](https://x.com/polynoamial/status/2057178198228586824), [post](https://x.com/polynoamial/status/2057216766221115562)), @_aidan_clark_ ([post](https://x.com/_aidan_clark_/status/2057180858017313253))_
- **Almost the entire Transformer block reduces to GEMM + epilogue**, letting memory-bound ops fuse into the matmul's on-chip shadow — an increasingly decisive systems lever as FP8/NVFP4 make compute cheap and data movement the bottleneck, and one where LLMs themselves can now write near-optimal kernels (CODA). _Source: @tri_dao ([post](https://x.com/tri_dao/status/2057640492020469845)), @jeremyphoward ([post](https://x.com/jeremyphoward/status/2057673229427499463))_
- **The best data filter may be no filter**: at sufficient (effectively infinite) compute, large models tolerate and even benefit from "low-quality" data, overturning the model-based-filtering orthodoxy — a "bitter lesson" for anyone curating pretraining corpora. _Source: @percyliang ([post](https://x.com/percyliang/status/2057642309190296028)), @stanfordnlp ([post](https://x.com/stanfordnlp/status/2057553038911381899))_
- **Value is migrating from raw models to systems and infrastructure**: "the model alone is no longer the product," and the breakout companies of the week were all infra (Modal, Exa, Cerebras, turbopuffer) — design products to ride model improvements, and bet on the "boring" layer. _Source: @gdb ([post](https://x.com/gdb/status/2057670776803996110)), @Vtrivedy10 ([post](https://x.com/Vtrivedy10/status/2057688482236289357)), @swyx ([post](https://x.com/swyx/status/2057119153337545096)), @tobi ([post](https://x.com/tobi/status/2057474133324095668))_
- **Serving stack > silicon generation**: topping an inference leaderboard on B200 (not GB300) shows software/serving optimization can outweigh having the newest hardware — and, relatedly, decoding/sampling configuration is a hidden ~10% lever on eval scores, so optimize the stack and config before blaming the chip or the model. _Source: @nebiustf ([post](https://x.com/nebiustf/status/2057192574067093925)), @xeophon ([post](https://x.com/xeophon/status/2057566921835401344))_
