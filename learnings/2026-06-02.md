# Learnings - 2026-06-02 04:20

## AI / Machine Learning

- **JEPA vs. token prediction has a provable data-efficiency gap**: For data with hidden hierarchical structure, world models that predict their own latent abstractions (JEPA, data2vec) need exponentially less data than token-prediction LLMs. This is a formal proof on a toy model, not just intuition — giving theoretical grounding to representation-space prediction. _Source: @MatthieuWyart ([post](https://x.com/MatthieuWyart/status/2061317203857739846)), @ylecun ([post](https://x.com/ylecun/status/2061465659469402147))_

- **Why bigger models outperform smaller ones — resource competition**: A Stanford paper traces the advantage of larger models to a data-induced "competition for resources" (neurons) during pretraining, analyzed via formal analysis, idealized tasks, and real pretraining runs. It reframes scale not as raw capacity but as relieving internal contention. _Source: @StanfordAILab ([post](https://x.com/StanfordAILab/status/2061540307565760618))_

- **Setting temperature=0 for "determinism" in benchmarks corrupts results**: Forcing temperature 0 across all LLMs to make evaluations deterministic significantly degrades model performance, making comparative results meaningless — yet it appears in submitted benchmark papers. A concrete eval-design pitfall to avoid. _Source: @j_dekoninck ([post](https://x.com/j_dekoninck/status/2061525896004055526)), @paradite_ ([post](https://x.com/paradite_/status/2061539981399818620))_

- **Async RL is shifting from optimization-limited to rollout-limited**: As reasoning trajectories grow longer and more heterogeneous, the bottleneck in frontier RL moves to GPU utilization and learner-generator synchronization (train-inference mismatch), not the optimizer. A survey of 8 open-weight labs catalogs the algorithmic and systems fixes. _Source: @whatthelukh ([post](https://x.com/whatthelukh/status/2061509262069923868)), @novasarc01 ([post](https://x.com/novasarc01/status/2061549983854366866))_

- **Async RL bias trade-offs**: Techniques that deliberately add bias to the RL objective (e.g., TIS + CISPO) stabilize training with smaller batch sizes, but they scale more poorly than unbiased methods — a key tension when choosing infra for frontier asynchronous RL. _Source: @cwolferesearch ([post](https://x.com/cwolferesearch/status/2061580533830697095))_

- **SFT as a special case of distillation**: Supervised fine-tuning can be framed as off-policy distillation (OPD) where the actor is the teacher instead of the student — a unifying lens that connects imitation and distillation objectives. _Source: @willccbb ([post](https://x.com/willccbb/status/2061582479694451001)), clarified by @lateinteraction ([post](https://x.com/lateinteraction/status/2061591349179339019))_

- **Evals should be living, not static — mine production failures**: Benchmarks like CursorBench stay relevant by continually pulling new eval tasks from real coding sessions. The hard part is the infrastructure to harvest and curate failure cases from production agent usage. _Source: @cwolferesearch ([post](https://x.com/cwolferesearch/status/2061214434605842694))_

- **Model sparsity varies widely across frontier MoEs**: Nemotron 3 activates ~10% of params vs. ~3% for Kimi K2 / DeepSeek V4 — i.e., it is significantly *less* sparse. Active-vs-total param ratio is a key architectural lever distinguishing otherwise-similar MoE models. _Source: @eliebakouch ([post](https://x.com/eliebakouch/status/2061607195268038777))_

- **GPU-friendly ranking loss without sorting**: A ranking loss can be implemented to be GPU-friendly with full gradient propagation and no sort operation — avoiding the subpar variants commonly used. Sorting in the loss path is a frequent and unnecessary performance/gradient pitfall. _Source: @MParakhin ([post](https://x.com/MParakhin/status/2061095593225920926))_

- **Audio source separation is now local and open**: StemDeck (open-source, built on the Demucs model) splits any track into 6 stems — vocals, drums, bass, guitar, piano, other — running entirely locally with no upload or signup. Demucs originated at FAIR-Paris. _Source: @XAMTO_AI ([post](https://x.com/XAMTO_AI/status/2061030928986878151)), @ylecun ([post](https://x.com/ylecun/status/2061470365906333873))_

- **Backpropagation, from the ground up**: Stanford CS224N's "Computing Neural Network Gradients" notes walk through the chain rule, computational graphs, and vectorized derivatives — a clean reference for the math behind gradient computation. _Source: @stanfordnlp ([post](https://x.com/stanfordnlp/status/2061616976628523185))_

- **NVIDIA Cosmos 3 — fully open omnimodel for Physical AI**: Cosmos 3 uses a Mixture-of-Towers (MoT) architecture to unify vision reasoning, world generation, and action generation in one model, shipping as Super (32B) and Nano (8B) variants that top Physics-IQ, PAI-Bench, and other Physical AI benchmarks. A "Cosmos Coalition" (Runway, NVIDIA, others) forms to open-source frontier world models — signaling world models becoming a shared, open infrastructure layer. _Source: @JonhernandezIA ([post](https://x.com/JonhernandezIA/status/2061406559914070191)), @haozhangml ([post](https://x.com/haozhangml/status/2061621174313123920)), @runwayml ([post](https://x.com/runwayml/status/2061315089869721682)), @songhan_mit ([post](https://x.com/songhan_mit/status/2061501413499322394))_

- **Semantic masking to prevent answer leakage in RLVR**: "Hide to Guide" addresses the problem that expert traces guiding RLVR can leak key values, executable code, or answer entities — using semantic masking so traces guide without giving away the answer. _Source: @songhan_mit ([post](https://x.com/songhan_mit/status/2061158011788304708))_

- **CodeAct vs. tool calling convergence**: OpenHands started with CodeAct (agent writes code to act), added tool calling in late 2024, then dropped the CodeAct side in 2025 — because LLMs post-trained for tool calling became comparatively much better, and usability/performance favored tools. Counterpoint: a terminal tool is arguably still CodeAct in spirit, since it gives the agent the ability to write and run code. _Source: @engelnyst ([post](https://x.com/engelnyst/status/2061154652305985618)), @gneubig ([post](https://x.com/gneubig/status/2061161541290864702))_

- **Video models inherit intelligence from language**: A former xAI world-model / NVIDIA Cosmos researcher argues video generation models get most of their intelligence from language rather than from video data, and that AI video may follow the same trajectory as coding agents. _Source: @swyx ([post](https://x.com/swyx/status/2061494196469645771)), @swyx ([post](https://x.com/swyx/status/2061474228235563325))_

- **MiniMax M3 architecture**: An open-weights model combining coding/agentic capability with strong inference efficiency, built on a new MSA (sparse-attention) architecture aimed at long context. Designers highlight unusually low serving cost relative to other, much sparser models — a notable design choice rather than a flaw. _Source: @MiniMax_AI ([post](https://x.com/MiniMax_AI/status/2061410380635860994)), @eliebakouch ([post](https://x.com/eliebakouch/status/2061626333373809023)), @togethercompute ([post](https://x.com/togethercompute/status/2061271571159822402))_

- **Mellum2 / MTP findings**: JetBrains' Mellum2 report shows Multi-Token Prediction (MTP) giving a significant eval-quality boost but no improvement in eval loss, run at only 0.1 loss weight — a useful data point that MTP's benefits may not show up in standard loss curves. _Source: @iamgrigorev ([post](https://x.com/iamgrigorev/status/2061485093051347393)), @iamgrigorev ([post](https://x.com/iamgrigorev/status/2061485590025998582))_

- **Compact late-interaction retrievers stay competitive**: A 149M-parameter late-interaction model (built on GTE-ModernColBERT-v1 via PyLate) trained for clause extraction from legal contracts is the best in its size class on the MLEB benchmark — evidence that small, specialized retrieval models remain very competitive. _Source: @lateinteraction ([post](https://x.com/lateinteraction/status/2061643303007961404))_

- **Memory over compute in large-scale training**: Liquid AI's CTO lecture on massively parallel training argues that in production, memory is more precious than compute, and covers the tradeoffs that actually matter at scale. _Source: @JosephJacks_ ([post](https://x.com/JosephJacks_/status/2061619850951459305))_

- **LFM2.5-8B-A1B for local tool calling**: A lightweight Mixture-of-Experts model purpose-built for local tool calling (demonstrated driving a Blender assistant) — illustrating the trend toward small MoE models for on-device agentic use. _Source: @JosephJacks_ ([post](https://x.com/JosephJacks_/status/2061606831181767166))_

- **Internal vs. public frontier gap**: Based on METR's time-horizon evals, the internal frontier appears to be roughly 2 months ahead of the public frontier, with no observed breakdowns in chain-of-thought monitorability or "neuralese." _Source: @METR_Evals ([post](https://x.com/METR_Evals/status/2061219610591170707))_

- **LLMs beating superforecasters**: Foresight-v3 sits #2 on ForecastBench's preliminary leaderboard, potentially the first publicly available model to beat human superforecasters on forecasting tasks. _Source: @BTurtel ([post](https://x.com/BTurtel/status/2061523763124986125))_

- **Streaming STT benchmarking**: The AA-WER Streaming benchmark measures real-time speech-to-text models on both accuracy and latency, reflecting that voice agents and live captioning require balancing the two rather than optimizing accuracy alone. _Source: @ArtificialAnlys ([post](https://x.com/ArtificialAnlys/status/2061491937518244332))_

## Software Engineering

- **Binary search is best derived from invariants**: A common binary-search snippet harbors a real bug; thinking in terms of a loop invariant (what must remain true about the search interval) makes the implementation reliably correct and memorable, versus memorizing off-by-one boundary cases. _Source: @ChShersh ([post](https://x.com/ChShersh/status/2061063482489897408)), @gaunernst ([post](https://x.com/gaunernst/status/2061092519447048267))_

- **Protobufs are effectively unavoidable infrastructure**: Protocol buffers have become a universal serialization standard that persists even when you leave the ecosystem that created them — worth learning as a durable, cross-org primitive rather than a Google-specific tool. _Source: @_arohan_ ([post](https://x.com/_arohan_/status/2061513120372379947)), @MillionInt ([post](https://x.com/MillionInt/status/2061521749242732910))_

- **Off-the-shelf media engines can be the bottleneck**: For high-performance video, ExoPlayer, AVFoundation, and ffmpeg were found insufficient, prompting a fully custom AV engine and composition library — a reminder that mature libraries sometimes can't hit demanding perf/power targets. _Source: @margelo_com ([post](https://x.com/margelo_com/status/2061492877474033738))_

- **Versioned defaults to fix footguns**: Some PyTorch footguns persist because changing defaults would be backward-compatibility-breaking. A proposed fix is "language editions" (à la C++20) that batch default changes into opt-in versions, making safer defaults adoptable without breaking existing code. _Source: @ezyang ([post](https://x.com/ezyang/status/2061460568003743762))_

- **Debug up the stack**: A strong engineering instinct is to keep debugging *up the stack* — into third-party software — rather than stopping at your own code, since many "your bug" symptoms are actually upstream. _Source: @ekzhang1 ([post](https://x.com/ekzhang1/status/2061631857200890049))_

- **AI code review responsibility**: A PR should generally be code you're willing to stand behind. When AI surfaces a bug you haven't investigated, the more responsible move is often filing a well-researched issue with a proof-of-concept fix rather than a "slop PR" — and if a reviewer carefully walks through your PR, there's an implied duty to respond just as carefully. _Source: @jeremyphoward ([post](https://x.com/jeremyphoward/status/2060961743762084226)), @jeremyphoward ([post](https://x.com/jeremyphoward/status/2060962045043097790))_

- **AI's hidden failure mode is silent assumptions**: When AI generates code against a large codebase, it pattern-matches and assumes the rest of the repo works like the slice it saw. Unflagged assumptions — not visible errors — are the real bug; reading every generated line is the defense. _Source: @CodingBlindTech ([post](https://x.com/CodingBlindTech/status/2061501630890070361))_

- **The value of abstraction**: "The purpose of abstracting is not to be vague, but to create a new semantic level in which one can be absolutely precise" (Dijkstra) — a reminder that good abstractions sharpen precision rather than hide detail. _Source: @drisspg ([post](https://x.com/drisspg/status/2061647478945128698))_

- **Pure-Rust zstandard decoder**: Trifecta Tech shipped a pure-Rust zstd decoder (funded by Astral, Chainguard, and NLnet), expanding the memory-safe, dependency-light tooling available for compression in Rust pipelines. _Source: @josh_wills ([post](https://x.com/josh_wills/status/2061480582811726045))_

## Systems & Infrastructure

- **Choose btrfs over ext4 for a new Linux machine**: btrfs offers advantages (snapshots, copy-on-write, checksumming) that make it a better default than ext4 when setting up a fresh system. _Source: @thdxr ([post](https://x.com/thdxr/status/2061207444240445587))_

- **AI-lab infra load can double weekly for months**: Capacity planning that assumes "this'll be fine unless traffic doubles" repeatedly breaks because load genuinely doubles every week for ~10 weeks straight. Provision for sustained exponential growth, not linear. _Source: @ekzhang1 ([post](https://x.com/ekzhang1/status/2061187101446570227))_

- **GPU supply is severely constrained**: Sophisticated GPU providers are sold out 9–10 months minimum, demanding 50% prepayment upfront on 12–24 month contracts. A concrete look at why compute access is a real moat right now. _Source: @ivanburazin ([post](https://x.com/ivanburazin/status/2061462784114622858))_

- **HBM is a three-name oligopoly**: High-bandwidth memory — which every AI accelerator depends on — is supplied by just three vendors, with capacity sold out through 2026; market-share shifts there are a leading indicator of who's winning in AI hardware. _Source: @stackmaps ([post](https://x.com/stackmaps/status/2061471685157556277))_

- **Co-packaged optics cut AI-cluster network power**: Lambda's adoption of NVIDIA Quantum-X InfiniBand Photonics switches brings co-packaged optics to large clusters — reducing network power overhead and failure points to deliver more tokens per watt. Networking, not just compute, is a throughput lever. _Source: @LambdaAPI ([post](https://x.com/LambdaAPI/status/2061319330433032658))_

- **Weight movement as a bottleneck**: For large-scale AI systems, moving model weights is quietly becoming as critical a bottleneck as raw compute; ScitiX reports gains from system-level optimization of the weight-loading path rather than faster GPUs. _Source: @scitix ([post](https://x.com/scitix/status/2060755306721005912))_

- **The data layer is the new AI ceiling**: Enterprise AI increasingly hits limits at storage bandwidth, data orchestration, and GPU efficiency — the "token economy" is won or lost at the data layer, not just model quality. _Source: @TheSixFiveMedia ([post](https://x.com/TheSixFiveMedia/status/2061576021816852927))_

- **RL workloads need three coupled systems, and it's now an infra problem**: Modern reinforcement learning requires inference, training, *and* sandboxes together — explaining why RL infra is harder than plain training. Frontier RL is bottlenecked less by algorithms than by infra ("tangled in spaghetti code and shackled by YAML"); Modal's writeup distills recurring patterns and ships an open-source library to bootstrap RL at scale. _Source: @bernhardsson ([post](https://x.com/bernhardsson/status/2061615757708906517)), @charles_irl ([post](https://x.com/charles_irl/status/2061527879607841040)), @josh_wills ([post](https://x.com/josh_wills/status/2061506553740722393)), @BanghuaZ ([post](https://x.com/BanghuaZ/status/2061582197476491529))_

- **Video generation is a full-stack infra problem**: Long video generation requires co-design across models, memory, KV cache, VAE decoding, scheduling, and deployment — not just a bigger model — as explored in the LongLive 2.0 work. SANA Streaming already runs video-to-video on a single RTX 5090, signaling frontier-class generation moving onto local hardware. _Source: @songhan_mit ([post](https://x.com/songhan_mit/status/2061501427357302791)), @songhan_mit ([post](https://x.com/songhan_mit/status/2061327607845572741))_

- **CPU offload for VLM serving**: In image-heavy vision-language-model serving, the vision encoder is the bottleneck. SGLang's EPD (Encode-Prefill-Decode) disaggregation offloads vision encoding to Intel Xeon CPUs to cut time-to-first-token (TTFT) and raise throughput — a heterogeneous CPU+GPU serving pattern. _Source: @ying11231 ([post](https://x.com/ying11231/status/2061614261500252538))_

- **vLLM v0.22.0 — concrete inference-engine advances**: 459 commits bring DeepSeek V4 hardening (NVFP4 fused MoE, full+piecewise CUDA graph, ROCm support), an experimental in-tree Rust frontend, and batch-invariant Cutlass FP8 yielding 28.9% lower end-to-end latency. Batch-invariance plus FP8 is a real latency win. _Source: @PyTorch ([post](https://x.com/PyTorch/status/2061255903605620994))_

- **MoE efficiency: activate a fraction of params**: JetBrains' Mellum2-12B-A2.5B activates just 2.5B of 12B params with 128K context, runs in vLLM from day zero with reasoning parser and tool calling — illustrating how MoE delivers large-model capability at small-model inference cost. _Source: @vllm_project ([post](https://x.com/vllm_project/status/2061621691995005301))_

- **Transformers adopting pluggable kernels**: Hugging Face is gradually integrating community kernels into Transformers to handle device-specific optimizations, quantization, and easy installs, with a request process for impactful kernels — a path to share kernel work across the ML ecosystem. _Source: @ClementDelangue ([post](https://x.com/ClementDelangue/status/2061475221438112180))_

- **CUDA mma_throughput pragma**: A new `mma_throughput` pragma yields a consistent 2–6% improvement on A100 GEMMs in testing — a small but real lever from NVIDIA exposing more ptxas tuning surface. _Source: @aryanvs_ ([post](https://x.com/aryanvs_/status/2061638212771275191))_

- **parakeet.cpp**: A GGML-based Parakeet (speech recognition) inference pipeline runs ~2x faster than an ONNX-based Parakeet pipeline on Apple Silicon — a concrete win for local, llama.cpp-style audio inference. _Source: @jeremyphoward ([post](https://x.com/jeremyphoward/status/2061390427576824311))_

- **Dataset dedup makes "disk smaller than data" workable**: Hugging Face's xet dedup let a 68 TB dataset clone to a private bucket in under a minute from a machine with only 4 TB local disk — content-addressed dedup decouples working-set size from local storage. _Source: @ClementDelangue ([post](https://x.com/ClementDelangue/status/2061427783251759366))_

- **Agent harness lineage preservation**: A teardown of NousResearch's open-source Hermes agent harness highlights one standout design choice: it preserves lineage across context-compression boundaries, so history isn't lost when context is compacted. _Source: @arizeai ([post](https://x.com/arizeai/status/2061505196283564359))_

- **Real-world frontier training hardware**: A sample R&D cluster: 16× B200 (180 GB) + 3.2 Tb/s InfiniBand across 2 nodes (208 vCPUs, 2,900 GiB RAM, 22 TiB SSD per node) — a useful reference point for what a small behavioral-LLM training setup looks like today. _Source: @realmaud ([post](https://x.com/realmaud/status/2061576173751042234))_

## Security

- **Giving an AI support agent account-modification powers with no identity verification is catastrophic**: Meta reportedly let its AI support agent modify Instagram accounts without identity verification, enabling mass takeovers of any non-2FA account — and 2FA itself was bypassed by design. A textbook lesson in not granting agents privileged write actions without strong authorization checks. _Source: @kalomaze ([post](https://x.com/kalomaze/status/2061511867324076454)), @GergelyOrosz ([post](https://x.com/GergelyOrosz/status/2061474330609872928))_

- **AI-generated media breaks selfie/face-scan verification**: "Take a selfie" liveness checks are now defeatable because AI can generate convincing images and video — identity verification flows that assumed cameras can't be faked are obsolete. _Source: @GergelyOrosz ([post](https://x.com/GergelyOrosz/status/2061474330609872928)), @kornbuilds ([post](https://x.com/kornbuilds/status/2061508020715053381))_

- **LLM token theft is a high-margin attack class**: Because a single prompt can cost millions of times more than an HTTP request, stolen API tokens are extremely valuable — attackers will tolerate awkward injected system prompts ("You are a support agent for X…") just to burn your tokens. Treat token leakage as a financial, not just access, risk. _Source: @cramforce ([post](https://x.com/cramforce/status/2061451519778758918))_

- **AI-built apps frequently ship with critical vulnerabilities**: Apps generated with AI often contain critical security flaws by default, making a dedicated post-build security scan a necessary step in the vibe-coding workflow. _Source: @zauthinc ([post](https://x.com/zauthinc/status/2054011037129539862))_

- **Weak liability blunts security incentives**: A cynical-but-real dynamic — security gets under-prioritized when the only recourse for breaches is litigation and damages are settled via insurance paying pennies to affected individuals, removing the pressure that would otherwise force investment. _Source: @suchenzang ([post](https://x.com/suchenzang/status/2061643065526476970))_

- **Third-party audits as a trust signal**: fwdemail published a Cure53 security audit, positioning itself as the only 100%-open-source email service to undergo independent third-party auditing — a model for how infrastructure providers can build verifiable trust. _Source: @fwdemail ([post](https://x.com/fwdemail/status/2061461326656848377))_

## Business & Strategy

- **Leverage comes from approach, not raw hours**: For most startups, the real leverage is in how differently you frame the problem and how well you execute — not piling on more hours, hiring, or activity. "More creates more" is true but low-leverage. _Source: @karrisaarinen ([post](https://x.com/karrisaarinen/status/2060977012471370054))_

- **Bounded intensity beats unbounded hours**: Jane Street's culture was demanding but time-bounded — ~9–10 hours of genuine work daily, then your time is your own — illustrating that high performance doesn't require always-on availability. _Source: @leilavclark ([post](https://x.com/leilavclark/status/2061013837365522837))_

- **Deep technical work yields ~4 productive hours/day**: 100-hour weeks characterize on-call jobs (e.g., banking) with little actual output; creative and technically precise work tops out around 4 productive hours daily. Agents shifted SWE toward the always-on-monitoring mode. _Source: @wordgrammer ([post](https://x.com/wordgrammer/status/2061456850600567206))_

- **Evaluate developers by output per cost, not tool mandates**: Rather than mandating or restricting tools, evaluate developers by output ÷ (salary + token spend) — aligning incentives with value created instead of process compliance. _Source: @bernhardsson ([post](https://x.com/bernhardsson/status/2061085226227421393))_

- **Prediction markets can be a small-business hedging tool**: Beyond speculation, prediction markets (e.g., Kalshi) let small businesses hedge real risk — an NYC bar promising free drinks contingent on an outcome uses the market to offset that liability. A novel, practical financial-engineering use case. _Source: @rennyzucker ([post](https://x.com/rennyzucker/status/2061556349696147574))_

- **The "AI Forward Deployed Engineer" is an emerging role**: FDEs embed inside client organizations to customize solutions — building and tuning agentic workflows for that client's specific needs. A new career archetype bridging product, consulting, and engineering. _Source: @AndrewYNg ([post](https://x.com/AndrewYNg/status/2061477558693384395))_

- **Product naming as durable strategy**: Anthropic naming its model "Claude" (a person's name) drives users to refer to it as a trusted colleague ("he's right on this one"), a stickier branding move than the verb-ification (Google/Xerox) that still left those as tools. _Source: @srijan_mahajan ([post](https://x.com/srijan_mahajan/status/2061412581718536395))_

- **A confidential S-1 is a real, defined SEC mechanism**: Anthropic confidentially submitted a draft S-1 registration statement to the SEC, which preserves the *option* to IPO pending review without committing — a standard pre-IPO step worth understanding (valuation debates aside). _Source: @_arohan_ ([post](https://x.com/_arohan_/status/2061498306879230264)), @tenobrus ([post](https://x.com/tenobrus/status/2061568991949381850)), @CuiMao ([post](https://x.com/CuiMao/status/2061571949311258664))_

- **Open source catches up when intelligence saturates**: A strategic thesis — once model intelligence saturates to "useful enough" for most humans, open source will close the gap to the closed frontier, and competition shifts to other axes (most likely serving cost). _Source: @MiniMax_AI ([post](https://x.com/MiniMax_AI/status/2061408200403062797))_

- **Consumption pricing reprices SaaS**: The shift from per-seat to consumption/usage-based pricing is treated as a structural change that makes two decades of cloud product design assumptions obsolete — and recently repriced large amounts of SaaS market value in days. _Source: @poezhao0605 ([post](https://x.com/poezhao0605/status/2061329526953853168)), @therollupco ([post](https://x.com/therollupco/status/2061175977795838427))_

- **FDV vs. market cap as an investment filter**: "Great product, bad investment" — a token/equity can be operationally strong yet a poor buy when fully-diluted valuation (FDV) sits far above market cap, because future unlocks dilute upside. _Source: @therollupco ([post](https://x.com/therollupco/status/2061175977795838427))_

- **AI lab capital concentration**: Reflection AI, Inflection AI, and Magic AI Labs have collectively raised on the order of $30.5B ($25B + $4B + $1.5B), illustrating how much capital is pooling into a small number of frontier-AI bets. _Source: @bycloudai ([post](https://x.com/bycloudai/status/2061579147760078910))_

- **The "last mile" still runs on phone numbers**: AI agents can pass the bar exam but can't order a pizza, because huge swaths of the real world (SMS verification, delivery coordination, account creation, calling a business) are gated behind phone numbers agents don't have — a concrete map of where agent capability stops. _Source: @davj ([post](https://x.com/davj/status/2061566773846217133))_

## Science & Research

- **NeRF earned an ACM Grace Hopper Award**: Neural Radiance Fields (NeRF) was recognized with the ACM Grace Hopper Award (given to under-35s for one specific contribution), placing it alongside past wins like Knuth's TAOCP and SDN — a marker of NeRF's foundational impact on 3D scene representation. _Source: @jon_barron ([post](https://x.com/jon_barron/status/2061476156038402129))_

- **A 50-year-old computational-economics challenge resolved**: A new paper provides a general method for the explicit numerical solution of the neoclassical model — answering Herbert Scarf's 50-year-old challenge on price-adjustment. Worth noting both for the result and as a case study in how hard novel theory can be to publish. _Source: @plazined ([post](https://x.com/plazined/status/2061537019352826345))_

- **Cryogenic cooldown effects scale with propellant mass**: With large liquid-oxygen loadouts (50–100 gal), cooldown effects don't dominate because tanks weigh ~1/10 of the propellant — but at several-gram cryogenic quantities, the thermal-mass ratio inverts and cooldown becomes the dominant effect. A scaling-regime engineering insight. _Source: @ID_AA_Carmack ([post](https://x.com/ID_AA_Carmack/status/2061454639434739826))_

- **Multi-agent environments for scientific discovery**: Station v1.5 structures an autonomous research loop and reports real math results — first AI to solve 3 of 9 target Diophantine equations and reproducing (and extending) AlphaEvolve's kissing-number lower bound — evidence that agentic systems can make genuine open-problem progress. _Source: @dualverse_ai ([post](https://x.com/dualverse_ai/status/2060778519714766961))_

- **Community-scale automated hypothesis testing**: An AutoDiscovery platform has run 45K+ community-submitted hypotheses, with 5+ manuscripts in progress across oncology, neuroscience, hydrology, and social science — a working example of AI accelerating the empirical research pipeline. _Source: @allen_ai ([post](https://x.com/allen_ai/status/2061529722392121631))_

## Key Takeaways

1. **Never give AI agents privileged write actions without strong authorization** — Meta's AI support agent could modify Instagram accounts with no identity verification, enabling mass account takeovers and bypassing 2FA by design. The single highest-impact security lesson in the feed. _Source: @kalomaze ([post](https://x.com/kalomaze/status/2061511867324076454)), @GergelyOrosz ([post](https://x.com/GergelyOrosz/status/2061474330609872928))_

2. **The hard problems in frontier AI are increasingly infrastructure, not algorithms** — RL training (now requiring coupled inference + training + sandboxes), long-video generation, VLM serving, and even model-weight movement and HBM supply are all being reframed as systems/infra challenges. _Source: @charles_irl ([post](https://x.com/charles_irl/status/2061527879607841040)), @bernhardsson ([post](https://x.com/bernhardsson/status/2061615757708906517)), @scitix ([post](https://x.com/scitix/status/2060755306721005912)), @stackmaps ([post](https://x.com/stackmaps/status/2061471685157556277))_

3. **Predict latent representations, not raw tokens, when data has hidden structure** — a formal proof shows JEPA/data2vec-style world models need exponentially less data than token-prediction LLMs, giving theoretical weight to a major architectural direction. _Source: @MatthieuWyart ([post](https://x.com/MatthieuWyart/status/2061317203857739846))_

4. **Small, specialized models keep punching above their weight** — Cosmos 3 Nano (8B), MiniMax M3's efficient MSA design, LFM2.5-8B-A1B for local tool calling, and a 149M late-interaction retriever leading its MLEB class all point to efficiency-first design beating brute scale for many tasks. _Source: @JonhernandezIA ([post](https://x.com/JonhernandezIA/status/2061406559914070191)), @MiniMax_AI ([post](https://x.com/MiniMax_AI/status/2061410380635860994)), @lateinteraction ([post](https://x.com/lateinteraction/status/2061643303007961404))_

5. **AI-generated code's real danger is silent, unflagged assumptions** — models pattern-match and assume the rest of a codebase behaves like the part they saw, so disciplined line-by-line review and "stand-behind-it" PR standards matter more than ever. _Source: @CodingBlindTech ([post](https://x.com/CodingBlindTech/status/2061501630890070361)), @jeremyphoward ([post](https://x.com/jeremyphoward/status/2060961743762084226))_
