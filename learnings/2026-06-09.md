# Learnings - 2026-06-09 16:20

## AI / Machine Learning

- **NVFP4 training**: Llama 3 8B and 405B were trained end-to-end in NVFP4 precision on NVIDIA Blackwell, running 1.31–1.73× faster than FP8 with zero accuracy loss — evidence that 4-bit floating point is now viable for full pretraining, not just inference. _Source: @NVIDIAAI ([post](https://x.com/NVIDIAAI/status/2064105188219134041))_
- **Apple on-device model architecture**: Apple's local Siri models are a 3B dense model and a 20B-total MoE with only ~1–4B active. To fit 20B params in RAM, a small model predicts from the prompt which experts to load — and an "early routing" decision is made once per prompt for the full model depth, rather than per-token. _Source: @eliebakouch ([post](https://x.com/eliebakouch/status/2064269593984545150)), @jeremyphoward ([post](https://x.com/jeremyphoward/status/2064243661542756681))_
- **Apple Private Compute context limit**: Apple's server-based Private Compute model reportedly ships with only a 32K context window. _Source: @heysaik ([post](https://x.com/heysaik/status/2064086700226494505))_
- **Local models / Intelligence-per-watt**: A Stanford "intelligence-per-watt" study finds local models can now accurately answer 71.3% of real-world chat and reasoning queries (up from 23.2% in 2023) at a fraction of frontier-API cost and energy — a concrete argument for routing most traffic to small/on-device LMs. _Source: @ClementDelangue ([post](https://x.com/ClementDelangue/status/2064034612133666915)), @ylecun ([post](https://x.com/ylecun/status/2064082422010925178))_
- **Workload distribution forecast**: A plausible near-term split is ~80% of workloads moving to 99%-cheaper models within 12–18 months, with ~20% staying on frontier models where maximum intelligence matters (scientific breakthroughs, hard reasoning). _Source: @ClementDelangue (quoting @brian_armstrong) ([post](https://x.com/ClementDelangue/status/2063972127804424383))_
- **Specialization trade space**: Expect a world of many distilled, specialized models, because you can always specialize a model to improve along one of three axes — quality, cost, or speed. _Source: @charles_irl ([post](https://x.com/charles_irl/status/2064013974707056850))_
- **Distillation robustness**: Hinton-style knowledge distillation shows surprising robustness with respect to the training data used — a result still not fully understood theoretically. _Source: @jeremyphoward (quoting @zhaisf) ([post](https://x.com/jeremyphoward/status/2063750854805778761))_
- **On-policy distillation (OPD)**: OPD combines on-policy rollouts with dense teacher supervision, but it is *not* a free lunch — there are at least three distinct failure modes to watch for when the student's distribution drifts from the teacher's. _Source: @lateinteraction ([post](https://x.com/lateinteraction/status/2064185909721899448))_
- **Optimizer numerics (Shampoo/Muon)**: Practical linear-algebra fixes for second-order optimizers: avoid taking square roots of small epsilon — use a pseudo-inverse instead (`inv_power_L = L.pow(-1.0/root)`). Conceptually, pseudo-inverse is a hard spectral cutoff while Tikhonov/damping is a soft spectral filter, and which matters may depend on batch size. _Source: @_arohan_ ([post](https://x.com/_arohan_/status/2064216022232826080), [post](https://x.com/_arohan_/status/2064239249843765437))_
- **Muon vs. Shampoo**: Muon without gradient accumulation is not Muon — it reduces to Spectral Descent, which is >2× slower; accumulation is precisely what makes Muon fast and distinguishes it from Shampoo. Relatedly, Muon breaks with the "preconditioning" framing entirely, since normalizing a gradient by gradient statistics *within the same batch* isn't preconditioning. _Source: @suchenzang ([post](https://x.com/suchenzang/status/2064150198310637970), [post](https://x.com/suchenzang/status/2064208131056611667))_
- **Optimizer benchmarks don't transfer**: Many recent optimizer records were tuned for attention/specific datasets (CIFAR, ImageNet) and do not transfer — which is why standardized AlgoPerf-style competitions were created. _Source: @_arohan_ ([post](https://x.com/_arohan_/status/2064172493943029804))_
- **Test-time compute is under-accounted**: Despite knowing about inference-time scaling since OpenAI o1, labs still report single scalar evals, and safety frameworks (RSPs) often ignore inference budget — so a model's capability can jump dramatically just by spending 100× more compute via a scaffold. _Source: @polynoamial ([post](https://x.com/polynoamial/status/2064370734806532289))_
- **Evals are multi-dimensional**: Benchmarks are better framed as three-way optimization problems between performance, cost, and latency for specific applications, rather than a single accuracy number. _Source: @SeanZCai ([post](https://x.com/SeanZCai/status/2064224327248286080))_
- **VQAScore for image-gen eval**: Asking a VLM "does this image show {prompt}?" and using P(Yes) as the score has become the go-to evaluation metric and reward model for visual generation, displacing CLIPScore across the field. _Source: @gneubig ([post](https://x.com/gneubig/status/2064317106787610719))_
- **Monosemanticity vs. superposition**: As models grow larger they develop more focused, monosemantic ("localist") representations, yet still rely heavily on superimposed concepts — both phenomena coexist rather than one replacing the other. _Source: @chrmanning ([post](https://x.com/chrmanning/status/2063768883509772785))_
- **VLA-JEPA**: A new vision-language-action model in LeRobot doesn't just map observations to actions — it uses a V-JEPA2 world model to learn action-relevant dynamics, conditioning the policy on learned world-model representations. _Source: @ylecun (RT @LeRobotHF) ([post](https://x.com/ylecun/status/2064102718377955693))_
- **Video tokenization for world models**: Compressing video to roughly one token per frame ("delta tokens") makes long-video world modeling far more efficient for both understanding and generation. _Source: @gabriberton ([post](https://x.com/gabriberton/status/2063723391727624608))_
- **Causal/streaming world models**: A causal version of the SANA world model achieves close to real-time inference on a single H100, showing autoregressive/streaming formulations can make world models interactive. _Source: @songhan_mit ([post](https://x.com/songhan_mit/status/2064033809746530426))_
- **Coding vs. biology progress gap**: Anthropic's science blog argues AI advances faster in coding than biology partly because, to an agent, biological databases are like sprawling cities with no clear map — navigability of the data, not just model capability, gates progress. _Source: @JosephJacks_ ([post](https://x.com/JosephJacks_/status/2064238242665488823))_
- **Training data shapes default behavior**: Model defaults reflect biases in internet data — secondary framings (rankings, summaries, hot takes) appear far more often in training corpora than the primary sources they compress, skewing model output toward those forms. _Source: @saranormous ([post](https://x.com/saranormous/status/2064208197309919556))_
- **FrontierCode eval**: Cognition's new coding benchmark uses tasks each requiring 40+ hours of work by open-source maintainers, and is the first to measure code *maintainability* ("would you actually merge this?"). It has Extended (150), Main (100), and Diamond (50) task sets; METR found over half of prior SWE-Bench "passes" were actually unmergeable slop. _Source: @cognition ([post](https://x.com/cognition/status/2064061031912288715)), @swyx ([post](https://x.com/swyx/status/2064081945567580323))_
- **CAD generation benchmarks**: CADGenBench evaluates AI generation of engineering-grade 3D CAD parts — a new output class needing eval tools distinct from text/code/image. A generated STEP file must first pass hard validity gates (well-formed, watertight, meshable, manifold; failing any zeroes the score), then geometric similarity is scored with Surface Distance F1 (are surfaces in the right place?) and Volume IoU (does it occupy the same volume?). _Source: @Thom_Wolf ([post](https://x.com/Thom_Wolf/status/2064029993638764672), [post](https://x.com/Thom_Wolf/status/2064029995731693679), [post](https://x.com/Thom_Wolf/status/2064029997749186989))_
- **Gemini 3.5 Live Translate**: A real-time speech-to-speech translation model covering 70+ languages that starts translating as you talk (streaming, low-latency), auto-detects multilingual input in one session, and preserves pitch/pacing with noise filtering. _Source: @OfficialLoganK ([post](https://x.com/OfficialLoganK/status/2064369125447864674)), @GoogleAI ([post](https://x.com/GoogleAI/status/2064366504112505266))_
- **Speech that preserves prosody**: Standard speech-to-text pipelines strip pacing, pauses, and tone; "Listening Fusion Gemma" (LFG-1) is an attempt to give a model the ability to *listen* to those paralinguistic cues rather than just transcribe words. _Source: @GlennCameronjr ([post](https://x.com/GlennCameronjr/status/2064075037544456584))_
- **On-device weight loading**: safetensors v0.8.0 now loads tensors directly into a Metal `MTLBuffer` on Apple Silicon and hands them to frameworks like PyTorch via DLPack, skipping needless host-to-device copies. _Source: @julien_c ([post](https://x.com/julien_c/status/2064316147235758265))_
- **RL generalization skepticism**: An argued limitation — current RL doesn't generalize broadly and is only clearly useful in a handful of domains, raising the question of whether models remain bound by human-generated training data. _Source: @martin_casado ([post](https://x.com/martin_casado/status/2064002191418102073))_
- **Reproducible RL environments**: A push to treat RL environments as first-class research objects — publishable, reproducible, and composable so others can build on and systematically study them (the OpenEnv initiative). _Source: @novasarc01 (quoting @ben_burtenshaw) ([post](https://x.com/novasarc01/status/2064016126510830064))_

## Software Engineering

- **Design loops, not prompts**: As agents get more capable, the higher-leverage skill is designing the *loops* that drive agents (orchestration, to-do triage, delegation, status checks) rather than hand-prompting each step — though this mainly pays off for those with effectively unlimited token budgets. _Source: @steipete ([post](https://x.com/steipete/status/2063697162748260627)), @gneubig ([post](https://x.com/gneubig/status/2064011013637234728)), @GergelyOrosz ([post](https://x.com/GergelyOrosz/status/2063997292290474066))_
- **Running agents autonomously**: For long-running autonomous agent work, use auto-permission mode (so the agent isn't blocked on approvals) and dynamic/orchestrated workflows so the agent manages its own context over long horizons. _Source: @bcherny ([post](https://x.com/bcherny/status/2063792263067754658))_
- **Nested subagents for context management**: Claude Code now supports agents spawning agents (capped at depth=5) — a technique for managing limited context windows by decomposing work across a tree of agents. _Source: @bcherny ([post](https://x.com/bcherny/status/2064327225504403752)), @lateinteraction ([post](https://x.com/lateinteraction/status/2064373207994413566))_
- **Recursive agents as an RL recipe**: AstraFlow v0.1.1 reproduces a dynamic recursive-agent RL recipe (with a Megatron backend) where models learn to automatically spawn sub-agents — turning the "agents spawning agents" pattern into something you can train for. _Source: @gneubig ([post](https://x.com/gneubig/status/2064319149027832298))_
- **Agentic bug-finding at scale**: Running an agentic "comb the whole codebase for bugs" workflow surfaced 144 real bugs in one large codebase — a signal that whole-repo agent sweeps can find issues (including embarrassing ones) that humans miss. _Source: @bcherny ([post](https://x.com/bcherny/status/2064097998309826318))_
- **Coding is the easy part**: Engineering is much more than writing code — debugging, operating/scaling services, deciding what to optimize, capacity/hardware planning, talking to users, and product planning remain largely unsolved by current agents. _Source: @bcherny ([post](https://x.com/bcherny/status/2064136590667256229))_
- **"Look at the data" → "look at the source code"**: The single most predictive skill among top engineers/researchers is obsessively examining the actual data; the same discipline applies to always reading the source code rather than trusting abstractions or reasoning from assumptions. _Source: @soldni ([post](https://x.com/soldni/status/2063652971607650345)), @ezyang ([post](https://x.com/ezyang/status/2064165479967600983)), @arb8020 ([post](https://x.com/arb8020/status/2064200820808196521))_
- **Framework API design**: Across decades of NN frameworks, the ones that endure are distinguished by understanding and applying good API-design principles, not just performance. _Source: @fchollet ([post](https://x.com/fchollet/status/2063809469801464007))_
- **Data-Oriented Design**: Andrew Kelley's talk "A Practical Guide to Applying Data Oriented Design" is a recommended, high-impact resource on structuring data for performance (cited as influential while building Ruff). _Source: @charliermarsh ([post](https://x.com/charliermarsh/status/2064106807921189014))_
- **AI's complementary strengths in writing vs. coding**: For prose, AI is weak on the first 50% (ideation/structure) but strong at polish; for code the relationship is roughly reversed — useful for deciding where to apply it. _Source: @wordgrammer ([post](https://x.com/wordgrammer/status/2064076438299361675))_

## Systems & Infrastructure

- **vLLM for agentic + omni-modal serving**: vLLM can serve production agentic workloads — persistent memory, reusable skills, and a built-in scheduler — and vLLM-Omni v0.22.0 adds omnimodal serving (text/image/audio/video/action in and out) for 30+ models (Qwen3-Omni, Flux2, Wan 2.2, BAGEL, etc.) across NVIDIA, AMD, Huawei Ascend, and Intel hardware, with day-0 NVIDIA Cosmos 3 world models, robot serving, and production TTS. _Source: @vllm_project ([post](https://x.com/vllm_project/status/2064083226105417989)), @woosuk_k ([post](https://x.com/woosuk_k/status/2064134014831837470)), @NVIDIAAI ([post](https://x.com/NVIDIAAI/status/2064020930696012092))_
- **Programmable serving layers pay off**: Vortex integrates sparse-attention directly into the SGLang serving stack and thereby converts *theoretical* sparse-attention speedups into real end-to-end gains — illustrating why a programmable, production-grade serving layer matters versus kernel-level wins alone. _Source: @ying11231 ([post](https://x.com/ying11231/status/2063663686506758423))_
- **S3 as a real filesystem**: ZeroFS exposes S3 as a real POSIX filesystem and block device (over NFS / 9P / NBD) with microsecond latencies, always-on encryption, and S3-level pricing — bridging object storage and block/file semantics. _Source: @_pierre_barre ([post](https://x.com/_pierre_barre/status/2064312765901046093))_
- **Apple Metal AI primitives**: Apple is exposing low-level AI building blocks in Metal — Metal tensors and TensorOps for quantized matmul, FlashAttention-style fusion, and Core AI custom ops — a notable on-device acceleration story buried in the toolkit. _Source: @SubhoGhosh02 ([post](https://x.com/SubhoGhosh02/status/2064221238315434203))_
- **Edge inference via Metal**: Careful llama.cpp Metal optimization now runs quantized small models (Gemma-4-E2B Q2, LFM2.5-1.2B Q4) fast directly on an iPhone's Metal backend — practical evidence of viable on-phone LLM inference. _Source: @ramin_m_h ([post](https://x.com/ramin_m_h/status/2063865294800519256))_
- **Scaling long-video training**: Long-video training systems (for both understanding and generation) are scaled primarily through parallelism strategies — the subject of a dedicated blog on the practical mechanics. _Source: @songhan_mit ([post](https://x.com/songhan_mit/status/2064037468853141659))_
- **LFM training parallelism**: Training Liquid Foundation Models at scale requires solving parallelism (data, tensor, and more) layer-by-layer, because not all architecture layers parallelize the same way; deeper work centers on compilers, quantization, long-context optimization, and communication-compute overlap. _Source: @mlech26l ([post](https://x.com/mlech26l/status/2063968167307968875), [post](https://x.com/mlech26l/status/2063740893421871265))_
- **Hilbert curves for cache locality**: Space-filling Hilbert-curve ordering keeps spatially-near data near in memory, improving L2 cache efficiency — a recurring trick in GPU/parallel programming. _Source: @SubhoGhosh02 ([post](https://x.com/SubhoGhosh02/status/2064358639171969334))_
- **Orbital data centers' real bottleneck**: The hard problem for space-based GPU clusters likely isn't power or cooling but networking — there's no obvious orbital equivalent of NVLink to fuse a million GPUs across satellites into one training fabric. _Source: @emerywells ([post](https://x.com/emerywells/status/2064124445761880083))_
- **Regression-loss choice biases predictions**: Fitting an ETA/wait-time model with mean-absolute-error (MAE) regression instead of ordinary least squares minimizes the median, not the mean — so on long-right-tailed distributions it systematically underestimates the mean wait time. A concrete lesson in matching loss function to the quantity you actually want. _Source: @bernhardsson ([post](https://x.com/bernhardsson/status/2063999728006939108))_
- **Audio engineering trick**: During keynotes, Apple reportedly notches out the 3k–6kHz band on the word "Siri" so nearby HomePods don't wake — a clever frequency-domain hack to defeat voice triggering. _Source: @luuk58 ([post](https://x.com/luuk58/status/2064085109980987720))_

## Security

- **Why the industry moved to token-based billing**: Flat-rate AI subscriptions were exploitable (e.g., tools commandeering OAuth tokens to resell unlimited usage), pushing providers toward usage/token-based pricing. The underlying economics: per-seat flat fees are heavily subsidized and priced well below what heavy real usage costs. _Source: @edandersen ([post](https://x.com/edandersen/status/2063926344573235704)), @ClementDelangue (quoting @Shaughnessy119) ([post](https://x.com/ClementDelangue/status/2064052257058930914))_
- **Cloud cache billing risk**: A real cautionary tale — a bug in Gemini's explicit cache feature billed a customer ~$1K/hour with no user-side way to delete the cache, and 24+ hours of support before resolution. Audit any managed caching feature's cost controls and kill-switches before relying on it. _Source: @_oliveiradanilo ([post](https://x.com/_oliveiradanilo/status/2063660986213490882)), acknowledged by @OfficialLoganK ([post](https://x.com/OfficialLoganK/status/2063679435635827132))_
- **Monitoring abstract agent tasks is harder**: Current agent-monitoring evaluations lean on the assumption that the agent has a concrete, narrowly scoped task; as agents take on increasingly abstract, high-level goals, that assumption breaks down and detecting misalignment gets materially harder. _Source: @METR_Evals ([post](https://x.com/METR_Evals/status/2063765607506665755))_

## Business & Strategy

- **Jevons paradox in tokens**: Cheaper tokens lead to a *bigger* total bill, not a smaller one (now consensus); the under-discussed question is where the margin actually accrues once the overall pie grows — that distribution, not the price drop, is what matters. _Source: @nebiustf ([post](https://x.com/nebiustf/status/2063990455453225355))_
- **OSS economics shift**: As teams seek cheaper model alternatives they're realizing two things simultaneously — open-source models are already very good, and the ability to train/serve them efficiently at scale meaningfully changes unit economics. _Source: @ClementDelangue ([post](https://x.com/ClementDelangue/status/2064052224393654480))_
- **Multi-model production workloads**: Real AI deployments are converging on using many models together (and increasingly adapting/post-training/optimizing them per use case) rather than a single frontier API — a maturation of the stack. _Source: @ClementDelangue ([post](https://x.com/ClementDelangue/status/2064029354863022156))_
- **AI layoffs are reallocation, not pure cuts**: Recent tech layoffs are largely about redirecting resources into AI — either internally (Meta, Microsoft) or because capital markets are rotating out of traditional software (Duolingo, Klarna) toward "AI stocks." _Source: @bernhardsson ([post](https://x.com/bernhardsson/status/2063685975797035480))_
- **Markets without insurance distortion**: LASIK and most cosmetic surgery aren't covered by insurance, yet both got cheaper and better every year — a case study in how direct-pay markets can drive down prices, contrary to the assumption that healthcare costs only rise. _Source: @BKRBusinessMin ([post](https://x.com/BKRBusinessMin/status/2064168702853984534))_
- **Paying for time vs. outcomes**: Most companies pay for time, but value rarely shows up evenly over time — the most expensive line item is often paying the same amount for radically different outcomes. _Source: @dylsphillips ([post](https://x.com/dylsphillips/status/2064229416419631414))_
- **Perpetual futures hit regulated US exchanges**: Perpetual futures — long a crypto-native instrument — have now launched on US-regulated exchanges, a structural change most retail traders are unfamiliar with before trading them. _Source: @chartsmeancash ([post](https://x.com/chartsmeancash/status/2064331967186010141))_

## Science & Research

- **History of open-sourcing in ML**: Landmark papers released far less than people assume — AlexNet, Seq2seq, and the Transformer shipped neither code nor weights; ResNet, GPT-2, BERT, and CLIP released weights but not training source code. Useful context for current "best paper must be reproducible" debates. _Source: @giffmana ([post](https://x.com/giffmana/status/2063695803978301668))_
- **Deep learning's origin anecdote**: Before AlexNet's fame, Hinton asked NVIDIA for a free GPU for students Krizhevsky and Sutskever and was declined; AlexNet was even presented in a rented hotel room beside NeurIPS because neural nets weren't yet accepted — a reminder how recently the field was fringe. _Source: @ABiggerSpalash ([post](https://x.com/ABiggerSpalash/status/2064279056418562076)), @MParakhin ([post](https://x.com/MParakhin/status/2064340577421037825))_
- **LLMs as news intermediaries**: About 1 in 10 U.S. adults already use AI chatbots for news, making reliability and failure modes of chatbots-as-news-medium a real and growing concern (Stanford HAI). _Source: @james_y_zou ([post](https://x.com/james_y_zou/status/2064133496810147939))_
- **Measuring AI overreliance**: The "Offloading Score" quantifies overreliance as the fraction of cognitive work handed to AI — computed by simulating how you'd have done each step without AI, then counting the steps the AI saved. _Source: @stanfordnlp ([post](https://x.com/stanfordnlp/status/2063699001845899558))_
- **Food microbiology**: Rare steak is safer than a rare burger because contamination lives on the *surface* (steak is cut straight from the muscle), which searing kills — whereas grinding mixes surface bacteria throughout the meat, so ground beef must be cooked all the way through. The same surface-area logic explains why pre-cut fruit spoils/poisons faster than whole fruit. _Source: @zeta_globin ([post](https://x.com/zeta_globin/status/2063819567864930583)), @arb8020 ([post](https://x.com/arb8020/status/2063832391529972107))_
- **Ship stability and the GM point**: A vessel stays upright because of balance, weight distribution, and water conditions captured by the metacentric height ("GM point") — the distance that determines how stable a ship *feels* in the water. _Source: @LittleFloatilla ([post](https://x.com/LittleFloatilla/status/2064326555346567182))_
- **What wheatpasting actually is**: Wheatpaste is a real adhesive made from wheat flour (or wheat starch) mixed with water — the literal "paste" behind street wheatpasting. _Source: @mma12261 ([post](https://x.com/mma12261/status/2063808249627885774))_

## Key Takeaways

- **4-bit training is real**: NVFP4 trained Llama 3 at up to 1.73× FP8 speed with zero accuracy loss — low-precision is moving from inference into full pretraining. _Source: @NVIDIAAI ([post](https://x.com/NVIDIAAI/status/2064105188219134041))_
- **71.3% of real-world queries can run on local models**: The strongest single data point for routing most inference to small/on-device LMs and rethinking cost and energy. _Source: @ClementDelangue ([post](https://x.com/ClementDelangue/status/2064034612133666915)), @ylecun ([post](https://x.com/ylecun/status/2064082422010925178))_
- **The agent skill is loop design**: The leverage point is shifting from prompting agents to designing orchestration loops (auto-permissions, dynamic workflows, nested subagents) that keep them coherent over long horizons. _Source: @steipete ([post](https://x.com/steipete/status/2063697162748260627)), @bcherny ([post](https://x.com/bcherny/status/2063792263067754658))_
- **Most benchmark "passes" are unmergeable slop**: METR found over half of SWE-Bench results wouldn't be merged; Cognition's FrontierCode now scores code *maintainability*, raising the bar for what "solved" means. _Source: @swyx ([post](https://x.com/swyx/status/2064081945567580323)), @cognition ([post](https://x.com/cognition/status/2064061031912288715))_
- **Match your loss function to the quantity you want**: MAE regression minimizes the median, so on long-tailed data (like wait times) it underestimates the mean — a subtle modeling bug that generalizes far beyond ETAs. _Source: @bernhardsson ([post](https://x.com/bernhardsson/status/2063999728006939108))_
