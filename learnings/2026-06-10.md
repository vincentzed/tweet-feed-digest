# Learnings - 2026-06-10 16:05

## AI / Machine Learning

- **4-bit training works at scale**: Llama 3 8B and 405B were trained end-to-end with NVFP4 (4-bit) precision on NVIDIA Blackwell, running 1.31–1.73× faster than FP8 with zero accuracy loss — evidence that 4-bit *training* (not just inference) is becoming viable on current hardware. _Source: @NVIDIAAI ([post](https://x.com/NVIDIAAI/status/2064105188219134041))_
- **Cheap instruction-tuning via distillation**: A Stanford lab reproduced ChatGPT-like instruction-following behavior for under $600 (the Alpaca recipe), and that approach became the template nearly every open-source instruction-tuned model now copies — behavior cloning from a strong teacher is far cheaper than training from scratch. _Source: @stanfordnlp ([post](https://x.com/stanfordnlp/status/2064471302170411324))_
- **A fully-disclosed frontier training recipe**: Microsoft's MAI-Thinking-1 ("Building a Hill Climbing Machine") paper released the full information needed to train a frontier model down to hyperparameters — including 30 trillion pre-training tokens plus a distinct mid-training phase, a rare level of openness for a frontier-scale run. _Source: @ylecun ([post](https://x.com/ylecun/status/2064678041180082318))_
- **Latent Context Language Models (LCLMs)**: Compressing long contexts into small latent representations can beat existing KV-cache compression methods on the latency/accuracy frontier. Yann LeCun notes the compression architecture is conceptually a ConvNet-style hierarchy (pooling/stride). _Source: @micahgoldblum ([post](https://x.com/micahgoldblum/status/2064361011994337772)), @ylecun ([post](https://x.com/ylecun/status/2064457360194593046))_
- **Parallax — a Pareto improvement on softmax attention**: A "local-linear correction" applied to softmax attention reportedly delivers a Pareto improvement over vanilla attention (better quality without a compute penalty trade-off). _Source: @novasarc01 ([post](https://x.com/novasarc01/status/2064398785728708976))_
- **Modularity-aware pretraining (EMO) helps distillation**: Pretraining a teacher with modularity awareness gave a +3.6pp downstream lift and ~87× lower pre-distillation perplexity on a dense student model versus distilling from a regular-MoE teacher. _Source: @sewon__min ([post](https://x.com/sewon__min/status/2064397977163374706))_
- **On-device MoE expert routing**: Apple's ~20B-parameter on-device model can't fit in RAM at reasonable precision, so a small model predicts *from the prompt* which experts to load — an exotic MoE design that trades a routing prediction step for memory feasibility. _Source: @jeremyphoward (RT @awnihannun) ([post](https://x.com/jeremyphoward/status/2064243661542756681))_
- **Specialize models along one of three axes**: The likely future is many distilled/specialized models, because you can always specialize a model to improve *one* of quality, cost, or speed — a useful framing for deciding which model to deploy where. _Source: @charles_irl ([post](https://x.com/charles_irl/status/2064013974707056850))_
- **Test-time compute is undercounted in evals**: Since OpenAI's o1, inference-time scaling has been known to materially change capability, yet labs still report scalar evals, safety orgs are surprised when scaffolds gain 100x via more inference, and responsible-scaling policies ignore inference budget when setting thresholds. A model's "score" is meaningless without specifying the inference budget. _Source: @polynoamial ([post](https://x.com/polynoamial/status/2064370734806532289))_
- **LLM memorization of rare entities**: With web search disabled, frontier models can recall niche details about people who have only 1–2 public internet sources — a concrete demonstration of training-data memorization. _Source: @raunakdoesdev ([post](https://x.com/raunakdoesdev/status/2064563273761186043))_
- **Behavioral steering techniques**: Prompt modification, steering vectors, and PEFT (parameter-efficient fine-tuning) are all mechanisms for selectively altering a model's behavior on specific task types — the toolkit for controlling model outputs post-training. _Source: @Hangsiin ([post](https://x.com/Hangsiin/status/2064397550434816088))_
- **The "Forgetting Wall" in video/world models**: Long-horizon video generation is bounded not only by compute but by how much of its own past the model can afford to remember — the KV cache becomes the effective memory, and limited memory is *why* long videos drift over time. _Source: @shiyi_c98 ([post](https://x.com/shiyi_c98/status/2064544050519400912))_
- **Multimodal training data is the bottleneck**: Most multimodal systems require data combining every modality at once, which is hard to source and expensive to build — a key reason joint text/audio/vision models are scarcer than single-modality ones. _Source: @ramin_m_h ([post](https://x.com/ramin_m_h/status/2064442262575104131))_
- **Diffusion/flow models make strong policies but are hard to RL-train**: Diffusion and flow models work excellently as policies, yet training them with RL is notoriously unstable — backprop-through-time blows up and standard RL objectives struggle over the iterative sampling process. _Source: @eigenron ([post](https://x.com/eigenron/status/2064566857747865991))_
- **Real-time chunking for robot policies**: RTC enables async execution of flow-matching policies, but its inpainting-based async capability comes from *inference-time corrections* rather than the base policy itself — so it adds little to the underlying policy's competence. _Source: @Outsider_pc ([post](https://x.com/Outsider_pc/status/2064415672369840457))_
- **Speech-to-speech translation**: Gemini 3.5 Flash Live Translate is a real-time speech-to-speech (not transcribe-then-translate) model covering 70+ languages in and out — built on one of Google's longest-running ML efforts. _Source: @JeffDean ([post](https://x.com/JeffDean/status/2064400689825288351))_

### Agentic RL & Post-Training Frameworks

- **Token-In-Token-Out (TITO) in agentic RL**: An agentic RL rollout is a chain of model calls, tool outputs, and resumed turns. TITO ensures the trainer evaluates the *exact* tokens the inference engine produced — if you break that invariant (detokenize-retokenize mismatch, or reasoning trajectories silently pruned by chat templates), training degrades. Getting chat templates consistent across multi-turn rollouts is trickier than most assume. _Source: @ying11231 ([post](https://x.com/ying11231/status/2064397571750256796), [post](https://x.com/ying11231/status/2064436105404105085))_
- **AsyncWebRL — async GRPO beats sync REINFORCE for web agents**: Replacing REINFORCE with asynchronous GRPO gave WebGym ~300% end-to-end throughput *and* better task performance, showing async rollout collection is a major lever for agent RL training efficiency. _Source: @PengchengWang19 ([post](https://x.com/PengchengWang19/status/2064596567672177118))_
- **Recursive-agent RL recipe (AstraFlow)**: AstraFlow v0.1.1 reproduces a dynamic recursive-agent RL recipe where models *learn to spawn their own sub-agents*, and adds a Megatron training backend — a reference for training Claude-style dynamic agent workflows. _Source: @gneubig ([post](https://x.com/gneubig/status/2064319149027832298))_
- **RL post-training tooling is consolidating around vLLM**: `vime` is a reference RL framework that ports slime's training paradigm into the vLLM ecosystem, designed to make vLLM the best rollout engine and to interoperate with the broader RL stack (SkyRL, Prime-RL, NeMo-RL, verl) rather than compete with it; `slime` itself is being forked (radixark's "Miles") for industrial-grade agentic RL. _Source: @vllm_project ([post](https://x.com/vllm_project/status/2064405738542502078)), @vllm_project ([post](https://x.com/vllm_project/status/2064397637634376174)), @slime_framework ([post](https://x.com/slime_framework/status/2064174799451287670))_

### Optimizers & Training Research

- **Shampoo → Muon lineage**: Muon belongs to the Shampoo family of preconditioned/second-order optimizers; Shampoo was itself a genuine step change over Adam. The debate over whether Muon is "equivalent" to Shampoo reflects a broader pattern of the field mining improvements within a local neighborhood of an existing formulation rather than questioning the formulation itself. _Source: @aurko79 ([post](https://x.com/aurko79/status/2064543792343228572)), @_arohan_ ([post](https://x.com/_arohan_/status/2064040861453955471))_
- **Muon vs. Shampoo, and the role of accumulation**: Muon *without* accumulation reduces to Spectral Descent, which is >2× slower — accumulation is what makes Muon fast, and the specific way it's added is what distinguishes Muon from Shampoo. A complementary view: Muon's real departure is breaking from "preconditioning" altogether, since normalizing a gradient by gradients in the same batch isn't preconditioning in the classical sense. _Source: @suchenzang ([post](https://x.com/suchenzang/status/2064150198310637970), [post](https://x.com/suchenzang/status/2064208131056611667))_
- **Meta's distributed_shampoo is a strong baseline**: With the right configuration (Nesterov momentum plus a learning-rate cooldown), Meta's off-the-shelf `distributed_shampoo` PyTorch package performs very competitively as a second-order optimizer — worth trying before hand-rolling one. _Source: @_arohan_ ([post](https://x.com/_arohan_/status/2064635756455313434))_
- **Distributed Shampoo in production**: It trains WeatherMesh-6, currently the most accurate weather-forecasting system in the world — a concrete, non-toy demonstration that Shampoo's gains hold on real large-scale workloads. _Source: @johndeanl ([post](https://x.com/johndeanl/status/2064384000681222532))_
- **Never materialize the Hessian**: A practical rule of large-scale optimization — work with Hessian-vector products and curvature approximations rather than forming the full Hessian matrix, which is intractable at model scale. _Source: @racerfunction ([post](https://x.com/racerfunction/status/2064417867697938798))_
- **Optimizer records don't transfer**: Many recent optimizer records tuned for Attention architectures fail to transfer to other settings; the proliferation of Adam variants came largely from overfitting to CIFAR/ImageNet, which is exactly why AlgoPerf-style competitions were created to measure optimizers fairly. _Source: @_arohan_ ([post](https://x.com/_arohan_/status/2064172493943029804))_
- **Nesterov momentum's origin**: The Nesterov momentum used in deep learning likely traces to James Martens' 2011 work on the importance of momentum in neural networks, and its derivation requires an approximation for the half-step. _Source: @_arohan_ ([post](https://x.com/_arohan_/status/2064083698065281241))_

## Software Engineering

- **AI-generated code can slow teams down**: More AI-generated code does not automatically make a team faster — the downstream costs (review, debugging, maintainability) can outweigh the speed of generation. _Source: @awscloud ([post](https://x.com/awscloud/status/2064449711155589396))_
- **Evals should measure mergeability, not just passing**: FrontierCode (Cognition) is a coding eval where each task took 40+ hours of work by leading OSS maintainers and is the first to measure "would you actually merge this code?" — capturing maintainability, not just whether tests pass. It's split into Extended (150), Main (100), and Diamond (50) task sets, and even SOTA models struggle. Relatedly, METR found that more than half of SWE-Bench "solutions" are unmergeable slop, undermining that benchmark's signal. _Source: @cognition ([post](https://x.com/cognition/status/2064061031912288715)), @xeophon ([post](https://x.com/xeophon/status/2064073724219789546)), @charles_irl ([post](https://x.com/charles_irl/status/2064083566217326950))_
- **Coding is the easy part of engineering**: The hard, still-unsolved parts are debugging, operating and scaling services, deciding what to optimize, provisioning hardware/capacity, talking to users, and product planning — AI accelerating code generation doesn't address most of the job. _Source: @bcherny ([post](https://x.com/bcherny/status/2064136590667256239))_
- **Designing agent loops over prompting**: A practical agentic workflow is: (1) pull context from email/Slack/GitHub/Linear to organize a prioritized to-do list, (2) walk it in priority order, (3) delegate each item — i.e., design the loop rather than hand-prompt each task. Nested subagents (agents spawning agents, capped at depth=5) are emerging as a way to manage context. _Source: @gneubig ([post](https://x.com/gneubig/status/2064011013637234728)), @bcherny ([post](https://x.com/bcherny/status/2064327225504403752))_
- **Inference-economics-shaped agent loops**: The Inferoa harness designs its agent loop around inference economics — prefix-cache discipline (structuring prompts so the KV cache is reused), context optimization, and routing between self-hosted and frontier models — a concrete pattern for building cost-aware agent systems on the vLLM stack. _Source: @vllm_project ([post](https://x.com/vllm_project/status/2064679109406740827))_
- **Profiling token spend**: In Claude Code, the `/usage` view has a detailed breakdown of which specific skills/MCPs/plugins are consuming tokens — useful for tuning agent cost. _Source: @bcherny ([post](https://x.com/bcherny/status/2064507661845246349))_
- **Performance wins are everywhere**: In most systems you can find enormous and often obvious performance wins simply because no one else has bothered to look — a reminder to actually profile before assuming a system is optimized. _Source: @charliermarsh ([post](https://x.com/charliermarsh/status/2064558313283678582))_
- **Data-Oriented Design**: Andrew Kelley's talk "A Practical Guide to Applying Data Oriented Design" is recommended as a high-impact resource (it influenced the early design of the Ruff linter). _Source: @charliermarsh ([post](https://x.com/charliermarsh/status/2064106807921189014))_
- **Confidential worktrees for sensitive code**: When coding with AI on confidential tasks, "confidential worktrees" let you keep sensitive code isolated from the model/provider — a workflow concept worth knowing if you work on proprietary or regulated code. _Source: @orgn_official ([post](https://x.com/orgn_official/status/2064386905668464771))_

## Systems & Infrastructure

- **GPU kernel optimization toolkit**: Modern high-performance kernel work involves writing Gluon kernels, warp specialization, and using TMA / `tcgen05` (tensor-memory) instructions — the building blocks behind kernels that now compete with expert humans on tasks like DSA, GDN, and MoE routing. _Source: @dogacel0 ([post](https://x.com/dogacel0/status/2064685767872348336))_
- **nvfp4 kernel gotcha: SFA and SFB layouts differ**: When implementing a 2-SM nvfp4 GEMM kernel, the scale-factor layouts for the A and B operands in shared/tensor memory (smem/tmem) are *different* — a non-obvious detail that silently breaks correctness if you assume symmetry. _Source: @m_sirovatka ([post](https://x.com/m_sirovatka/status/2064373427121590721))_
- **Pack Q heads in attention kernels**: Packing query heads — a technique that originated in MLA kernels — should generally be applied to normal GQA kernels too, since it improves memory layout/throughput. _Source: @gaunernst ([post](https://x.com/gaunernst/status/2064653262691893494))_
- **Hand-written FlashAttention is competitive but hard to keep there**: A pure-CUDA FlashAttention implementation (no CUTLASS) landed within ~5-6% of FA3 in FP8 before regressing to ~43% slower after changes, while its sliding-window attention path beat FA3 — illustrating both how close hand-rolled kernels can get and how fragile that margin is. _Source: @aybek9221 ([post](https://x.com/aybek9221/status/2064628136738787838))_
- **Hilbert-curve memory layout**: Mapping data along a Hilbert space-filling curve improves L2 cache efficiency by preserving spatial locality, a technique surfaced in the massively-parallel programming guide. _Source: @SubhoGhosh02 ([post](https://x.com/SubhoGhosh02/status/2064358639171969334))_
- **Agents stress-test your database layer**: Fleets of agents relentlessly hammering a service make good DB optimization suddenly critical — a firsthand reminder that agent-driven traffic has very different load characteristics than human traffic. _Source: @gneubig ([post](https://x.com/gneubig/status/2064377443620692370))_
- **Frontier-model latency targets in production**: Deep Cogito needed sub-500ms time-to-first-token at 1,000+ requests/minute for frontier reasoning models — a concrete data point on the latency/throughput bar for serving reasoning models at scale. _Source: @togethercompute ([post](https://x.com/togethercompute/status/2064420807653331262))_
- **Blackwell-optimized OSS inference is now click-deployable**: B200 and B300 capacity is being added to dedicated inference services with one-click deploy of a Blackwell-optimized engine and auto-scaling on frontier open-source models — signaling Blackwell is moving from scarce to readily rentable. _Source: @tri_dao ([post](https://x.com/tri_dao/status/2064426245652394337))_
- **SGLang's adoption curve**: SGLang hit 304M downloads with +744% growth in three months — evidence of how fast a high-performance open inference engine can spread once it reaches production maturity. _Source: @ying11231 ([post](https://x.com/ying11231/status/2064154584176865453))_
- **Full-stack transformer inference**: A single talk walks the entire cloud inference stack — from application-layer concerns down to hardware, debugging, and observability — as one mental model for deploying transformer inference. _Source: @charles_irl ([post](https://x.com/charles_irl/status/2064520569090109606)), @modal ([post](https://x.com/modal/status/2064522095934263574))_
- **Mac container machines**: A WWDC feature lets a Mac run a lightweight, persistent Linux environment with your home directory and repos automatically mounted — a low-friction local dev sandbox. _Source: @jeremyphoward (RT @timsneath) ([post](https://x.com/jeremyphoward/status/2064522152213393736))_
- **Bedrock "zero data retention" has exceptions**: AWS Bedrock's promise that data stays in the customer account/region — and "zero data retention" — does not apply to several models, an important caveat for anyone relying on Bedrock for data isolation. _Source: @apparentorder ([post](https://x.com/apparentorder/status/2064588498200400125))_

## Security

- **Chat memory can leak sensitive history**: When an assistant's "memory" feature is on by default and searches past chats for context — combined with a retention policy that includes human review — sensitive historical conversations can be silently pulled into new sessions, creating real NDA/compliance exposure. Audit memory defaults before using such tools on confidential work. _Source: @hammer_mt ([post](https://x.com/hammer_mt/status/2064513784241143835))_
- **Unrestricted API access for agents is a real risk**: Giving AI agents unrestricted access to your APIs is a major security exposure; the mitigation pattern is executing tools in isolated sandboxes with granular role-based permissions and human approval gates before sensitive (e.g., database) operations. _Source: @runtools_ai ([post](https://x.com/runtools_ai/status/2063057630109868456))_
- **Unified agent telemetry across endpoint, CI, and cloud**: Rather than becoming a "control plane" for agents, an OSS approach (Agent Beacon) focuses on unified observability/telemetry spanning endpoint, CI, and cloud agents — useful for detecting what your agents are actually doing across environments. _Source: @jqdsouza ([post](https://x.com/jqdsouza/status/2064703760216023225))_
- **Train on data you never see (MPC + encryption)**: Multi-party computation and encryption make it possible to train a model on data it never directly observes — the foundational technique behind privacy-preserving / confidential ML. _Source: @triton_one ([post](https://x.com/triton_one/status/2064298378746564814))_
- **Confidential Compute unblocks enterprise data**: NVIDIA's Confidential Compute (demoed with Protopia at GTC Taipei) lets enterprises use their most sensitive data with frontier models without exposing it — addressing a long-standing blocker to enterprise AI adoption around data sovereignty and security. _Source: @JoshJDMBA ([post](https://x.com/JoshJDMBA/status/2063113889173410012))_
- **Bitcoin Taproot `OP_IF` usage**: Of ~130M Taproot `OP_IF`s on-chain, 99.6% are hardcoded never to execute (the `If 0 = 1, execute X` pattern) — a window into how Taproot scripts are actually constructed versus how the opcode was designed to be used. _Source: @SteveSimple ([post](https://x.com/SteveSimple/status/2064699120959246523))_

## Business & Strategy

- **The economics case for open-source models**: Open-source models are already very good, and the ability to train and serve them efficiently at scale can shift the economics meaningfully — making reliance on closed models for anything important a real supply-chain risk. _Source: @ClementDelangue ([post](https://x.com/ClementDelangue/status/2064052224393654480))_
- **Unit economics of AI startups**: One AI startup founder generates ~40% annual return on GPU cost — about $400 in annual revenue for every $1,000 of hardware deployed — a rare concrete datapoint on the economics of GPU-bound AI businesses. _Source: @paulg ([post](https://x.com/paulg/status/2064690186412229026))_
- **Coding-agent companies are accelerating, not dying**: Despite the thesis that AI labs would absorb the coding-agent layer, Cursor, Lovable, and Cognition are growing — Lovable reports $500M annualized revenue and ~1M new projects/week. The application layer is capturing real value rather than being commoditized away. _Source: @saranormous ([post](https://x.com/saranormous/status/2064360899687989422)), @theCTO ([post](https://x.com/theCTO/status/2064490080979607792))_
- **Recursive self-commoditization as a lab strategy**: A proposed business model for frontier labs — keep the large models closed, enable distillation into open models *within the same family*, and capture value on both the closed (premium) and open (distribution) sides. _Source: @willccbb ([post](https://x.com/willccbb/status/2064494111604810082))_
- **Capability-gating as a competitive dynamic**: The Fable 5 / Mythos rollout reportedly degrades performance on "frontier AI research" tasks on purpose, and at least one benchmark org couldn't run verified evals due to a new data-access policy. Beyond the controversy, it surfaces a strategic concern: if only vetted incumbents get unrestricted frontier capability (especially for AI research itself), their lead becomes self-reinforcing. _Source: @novasarc01 ([post](https://x.com/novasarc01/status/2064423894581477400)), @eliebakouch ([post](https://x.com/eliebakouch/status/2064476806288159115))_
- **A new "use it, you license it back" license**: The Researcher Reciprocity License flips terms of use — "if you train on it, you let us generate" — and GPU MODE is moving its widely-used kernel datasets onto it, an emerging pattern for governing how open ML artifacts get reused in training. _Source: @jeremyphoward ([post](https://x.com/jeremyphoward/status/2064521172222615568)), @julien_c ([post](https://x.com/julien_c/status/2064655157741211920))_
- **Building is solved; distribution isn't**: With AI, shipping a product has become dramatically easier (Google AI Studio alone reports 1.2M apps/week), but finding product-market fit and the first real users has not — the bottleneck has shifted from building to distribution. _Source: @GergelyOrosz ([post](https://x.com/GergelyOrosz/status/2064717692423000153)), @OfficialLoganK ([post](https://x.com/OfficialLoganK/status/2064721080594665553))_
- **First-time vs. second-time founders**: "First-time founders focus on product; second-time founders focus on distribution" — Spotify's co-president uses this to explain why modern media strategy (e.g., short-form clipping) is built around distribution mechanics, not just the product itself. _Source: @internetkid69 ([post](https://x.com/internetkid69/status/2064542042391285857))_
- **Distribution beats the product**: Stephen King's pseudonymous "Richard Bachman" experiment showed that without an existing audience/brand, even great work finds only a small audience — directly relevant to claims (e.g., MrBeast's) that one could rebuild a huge following from scratch "anytime." _Source: @SethLargo ([post](https://x.com/SethLargo/status/2064467026664669365))_
- **Acquisition entrepreneurship as an alternative to startups**: ~9 of 10 startups fail, while millions of already-profitable businesses with existing customers and proven cash flow await new owners — buying an operating business ("search funds") is a lower-variance path than founding one. _Source: @searchers_com ([post](https://x.com/searchers_com/status/2021169774575878339))_
- **Tracking AI's macro impact**: Stanford's Digital Economy Lab launched "AI Economic Indicators," a platform for tracking how AI is reshaping work, productivity, adoption, and the broader economy — a data source for grounding AI-impact claims. _Source: @erikbryn ([post](https://x.com/erikbryn/status/2064713648480907373))_

## Science & Research

- **The "AlexNet moment" almost didn't get a stage**: The famous deep-learning breakthrough wasn't presented at the main NeurIPS conference — neural nets weren't considered cool enough to be accepted, so it was shown in a separately rented hotel room billed as an unrelated workshop (which was nonetheless packed). A reminder that paradigm shifts often start outside the gatekept venues. _Source: @MParakhin ([post](https://x.com/MParakhin/status/2064340577421037825))_
- **Fabrice Bellard's outsized, quiet impact**: A single Paris-based engineer authored foundational software the internet runs on — FFmpeg (powering video streaming across YouTube/Netflix/TikTok), QEMU (virtualization/emulation), the Tiny C Compiler, and QuickJS — illustrating how much core infrastructure can trace to one prolific individual. _Source: @ID_AA_Carmack ([post](https://x.com/ID_AA_Carmack/status/2064095424420487226)), @fchollet ([post](https://x.com/fchollet/status/2064095913036169589))_
- **Why AI advanced faster in coding than biology**: To agents, biological databases behave like sprawling, poorly-connected "cities" — the data is fragmented and hard to navigate — whereas code is structured, executable, and self-verifying, which is a large part of why coding progress has outpaced biology. _Source: @JosephJacks_ ([post](https://x.com/JosephJacks_/status/2064238242665488823))_
- **Benchmarking the "agents replace jobs" claim (Agents' Last Exam)**: From the group behind MMLU, MATH, CyberGym, and ExploitGym, ALE is a rolling benchmark built on real, labor-market-aligned work to test the forecast that AI agents will outperform humans at most jobs by 2026–27; on its hardest tier, top agents pass only 2.6% — a sobering empirical counter to the hype. _Source: @berkeley_ai (RT @YiyouSun) ([post](https://x.com/berkeley_ai/status/2064499740252004520)), @berkeley_ai ([post](https://x.com/berkeley_ai/status/2064499780181704852))_

## Key Takeaways

- **Inference budget is part of the result**: A model's benchmark score is meaningless without stating its test-time compute — scaffolds and extra inference can move capability dramatically, yet evals and safety thresholds still ignore this. _Source: @polynoamial ([post](https://x.com/polynoamial/status/2064370734806532289))_
- **4-bit training is real, and behavior-cloning a teacher is shockingly cheap**: Llama 3 (up to 405B) trained in NVFP4 on Blackwell ran ~1.7× faster than FP8 with no accuracy loss, while Stanford's ~$600 Alpaca reproduction became the template for the entire open-source instruction-tuned ecosystem — the two ends of how the open model wave got cheaper. _Source: @NVIDIAAI ([post](https://x.com/NVIDIAAI/status/2064105188219134041)), @stanfordnlp ([post](https://x.com/stanfordnlp/status/2064471302170411324))_
- **Measure mergeability, not pass rate**: More than half of SWE-Bench "solutions" are unmergeable slop; FrontierCode's "would you actually merge this?" framing is a better signal of real engineering value than tests-passing — and a caution against trusting raw AI-coding throughput. _Source: @charles_irl ([post](https://x.com/charles_irl/status/2064083566217326950)), @cognition ([post](https://x.com/cognition/status/2064061031912288715)), @awscloud ([post](https://x.com/awscloud/status/2064449711155589396))_
- **In agentic RL, token fidelity is everything**: Token-In-Token-Out ensures the trainer sees exactly the tokens the inference engine produced; chat-template mismatches and retokenization silently corrupt training if you ignore it. The Muon/Shampoo debate teaches the parallel lesson for optimizers — accumulation is what makes Muon fast, and its real novelty is stepping away from preconditioning entirely. _Source: @ying11231 ([post](https://x.com/ying11231/status/2064397571750256796)), @suchenzang ([post](https://x.com/suchenzang/status/2064150198310637970))_
- **Building is solved; distribution, PMF, and data trust are the new bottlenecks**: Shipping a product is now trivial (millions of apps/week), so advantage has moved to finding users and product-market fit — and "zero data retention" can carve out specific models (AWS Bedrock) while default-on chat memory can silently pull confidential history into new sessions, making data-trust diligence a competitive and compliance issue. _Source: @GergelyOrosz ([post](https://x.com/GergelyOrosz/status/2064717692423000153)), @apparentorder ([post](https://x.com/apparentorder/status/2064588498200400125)), @hammer_mt ([post](https://x.com/hammer_mt/status/2064513784241143835))_
