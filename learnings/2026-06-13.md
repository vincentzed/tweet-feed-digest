# Learning Points from X/Twitter Feed

## AI / Machine Learning

- **Model dependency graphs (ModSleuth)**: Modern LLMs are no longer built from human data alone — they depend on other models to generate/filter training data, evaluate outputs, provide rewards, and guide development. ModSleuth traces these chains and found Olmo 3 has 89 model + 183 dataset dependencies, while Nemotron 3 has 273 + 560 — dependencies that are deep, recursive, and not easily visible. This reframes "frontier model development" as far more interconnected than commonly assumed. _Source: @sewon__min ([post](https://x.com/sewon__min/status/2065115714906894648)), @berkeley_ai ([post](https://x.com/berkeley_ai/status/2065166768516309141))_
- **Multi-token prediction (MTP) in RL**: MTP acceptance length drops during RL not because of policy mismatch but because of higher entropy; combining rejection sampling with an end-to-end total-variation loss makes the process "entropy-free," reaching up to 95% MTP accept ratio, 2.4× faster rollouts, and 1.8× end-to-end RL speedup (used in Qwen3.5–3.7). _Source: @iofu728 ([post](https://x.com/iofu728/status/2065104577171775590))_
- **Self-distillation without RL (SD-Zero)**: When a reward model is available, you can self-improve using an on-policy distillation loss where the teacher is the *same* LLM as the student — but the teacher is shown the student's answer and its scalar reward. No reinforcement learning loop needed. _Source: @prfsanjeevarora ([post](https://x.com/prfsanjeevarora/status/2065544556091682902))_
- **Muon and Shampoo are the same geometry**: Both are spectral-norm steepest descent, as is most of modded-nanogpt's track-3 benchmark; the unexplored "entrywise sign" corner is filled by Lion and signSGD baselines. _Source: @LauraPGomez_ ([post](https://x.com/LauraPGomez_/status/2065543770779852992))_
- **Shampoo ≈ Muon equivalence (the cost caveat)**: Shampoo configured with `b2=0.0` and a momentum-based covariance (instead of gradient covariance), whitening only one side, is approximately equivalent to Muon up to numerical imprecision — but Shampoo tracks 4 states at ~2·n²·m² memory, so the equivalence may not be worth the overhead. _Source: @torchcompiled ([post](https://x.com/torchcompiled/status/2065203122256101543))_
- **Learned preconditioners (PSGD)**: An alternative to Shampoo/Adam-style adaptive methods where, instead of using a fixed preconditioner (second-moment estimate), you *learn* the preconditioner. Worth knowing as part of the current optimizer-research wave. _Source: @_arohan_ ([post](https://x.com/_arohan_/status/2065226144291631263)), @HessianFree ([post](https://x.com/HessianFree/status/2065239929450283309))_
- **Bias in preconditioned optimizers**: Adaptive optimizers (AdamW, Sophia, Shampoo) don't merely have *noisy* updates — their stochastic preconditioned updates are statistically *biased*. The "Correcting Stochastic Update Bias" paper proposes correcting this. _Source: @_arohan_ ([post](https://x.com/_arohan_/status/2065431106007306437))_
- **Why KFAC doesn't transfer cleanly to LLMs**: KFAC approximates the covariance E[gg^T] for a single sample's gradient, which works for vision, but next-token prediction solves T tasks simultaneously — even per-sample gradients from hooks are averages over T token losses, breaking the assumption. _Source: @_arohan_ ([post](https://x.com/_arohan_/status/2065448760898138612))_
- **SignSGD may suffice for RL**: Because RL gradient updates are inherently noisy, a cheap, low-memory optimizer like SignSGD (no momentum, no second-order terms) may be "good enough" — trading optimizer sophistication for speed and memory. _Source: @agarwl_ ([post](https://x.com/agarwl_/status/2065105336797716881))_
- **Diffusion LLMs hitting production speed (Mercury 2)**: A diffusion-based language model now reaches >1,000 tokens/sec (Groq/Cerebras-class throughput) with quality comparable to speed-optimized models like Claude Haiku — relevant for latency-sensitive workloads. _Source: @StefanoErmon ([post](https://x.com/StefanoErmon/status/2065112198356283516))_
- **o1 was an RL story all along**: For those with RL background, the o1 announcement was clearly a model trained with reinforcement learning — explaining why the "DeepSeek R1 moment" hype landed differently for RL-literate vs. general audiences. _Source: @proceduralia ([post](https://x.com/proceduralia/status/2065573888164344127))_
- **Supervised summarization for long contexts**: As task horizons grow, contexts can't scale forever. Self-summarization gives concise, interpretable contexts but at a performance cost; the fix is to *supervise* the information content of summaries by structuring them as natural-language belief states. _Source: @berkeley_ai ([post](https://x.com/berkeley_ai/status/2065167006375289140))_
- **Skip HTML parsing for web agents**: HTML parsing is a hidden bottleneck causing complexity and information loss; you can remove it entirely by directly retrieving and reading web *screenshots* through a VLM. _Source: @berkeley_ai ([post](https://x.com/berkeley_ai/status/2065165670007083016))_
- **Test-time compute in robotics is not free**: Naively spending more inference compute in robot planning isn't always worth it; *smart allocation* of test-time compute can recover frontier-level planning at a fraction of the cost. _Source: @StanfordAILab ([post](https://x.com/StanfordAILab/status/2065299105438396928))_
- **Flow Reversal Steering (FRS)**: A method to refine coarse actions produced by a robot policy's semantic reasoning into precise, similar actions — a way to elicit relevant behaviors from generalist policies on new tasks. _Source: @berkeley_ai ([post](https://x.com/berkeley_ai/status/2065672665558663382))_
- **On-device voice conversion (StyleStream)**: Zero-shot voice style conversion (timbre, accent, emotion) with *streaming* inference running locally on a single RTX 4060 — a marker of how far real-time speech models have compressed. _Source: @berkeley_ai ([post](https://x.com/berkeley_ai/status/2065672231691428047))_
- **Continuous I/O for real-world interaction**: Real-world machine interaction (e.g., live transcription) requires models that take *continuous* input and produce *continuous* output, rather than discrete turn-based generation. _Source: @PyTorch ([post](https://x.com/PyTorch/status/2065497226323914753))_
- **Deliberately restrained model size for local use (MiniMax M3)**: Despite the M3 series having a larger parameter count than M2, the team capped it at 428B specifically so local-model enthusiasts can run it affordably — a design tradeoff favoring deployability. _Source: @MiniMax_AI ([post](https://x.com/MiniMax_AI/status/2065582861743436100))_
- **Diffing models with an agent**: You can compare two models simply by asking an agent to do the diff — an "obvious thing" that turns out to work for model interpretability/comparison. _Source: @arb8020 ([post](https://x.com/arb8020/status/2065502999565259060))_
- **The "interp hammer" test for interpretability**: A litmus test for mechanistic-interpretability research — ask whether the project's results could have been achieved *without* interpretability techniques. If yes, the interp work may not be doing real load-bearing work. _Source: @StephenLCasper ([post](https://x.com/StephenLCasper/status/2065428641492676967))_
- **ViT patch-size limits constrain "Adaptive Patch" on video**: The max size of a patch is bounded by ViT depth, so even a trivially compressible input (e.g. an empty black frame repeated for an hour) can still require many tokens. _Source: @gabriberton ([post](https://x.com/gabriberton/status/2065307360676241839))_
- **General models beat specialized medical tools**: In a randomized, blinded evaluation by 12 US clinicians, general frontier models (Google/OpenAI/Anthropic) outperformed specialized medical systems (OpenEvidence, UpToDate) — a result the authors did not anticipate. _Source: @JeffDean ([post](https://x.com/JeffDean/status/2065467587207819761))_
- **Evals are getting too expensive to trust**: Eval researchers increasingly admit we don't actually know how capable current LLMs are because rigorous measurement costs too much; on serious cyber evals, the newest models are still improving past the 100M-token mark without plateauing. _Source: @nebiustf ([post](https://x.com/nebiustf/status/2065561661205864589))_
- **Deployment-centered evaluation**: Evaluating deployed clinical LLM systems benefits from predicting *query-level rejection risk* — i.e., when the system should decline — rather than only measuring static benchmark accuracy. _Source: @StanfordAILab ([post](https://x.com/StanfordAILab/status/2065488179034198298))_
- **Benchmarking agentic inference (AA-AgentPerf)**: A new benchmark measures inference performance on *real, long-context agentic coding* workloads (not just throughput), comparing the same model across NVIDIA Blackwell, Hopper, and AMD hardware. _Source: @ArtificialAnlys ([post](https://x.com/ArtificialAnlys/status/2065559824230957190))_
- **Benchmark choice changes rankings**: Swapping SWE-Bench Pro for Datacurve's DeepSWE in a coding-agent index reordered the leaderboard — a concrete reminder that agent rankings are highly sensitive to the underlying benchmark. _Source: @ArtificialAnlys ([post](https://x.com/ArtificialAnlys/status/2065328920514515037))_
- **The harness matters as much as the model**: With the same underlying model, Claude Code can be the *worst*-performing coding harness — behind opencode and cursor CLI — showing that agent scaffolding is a major, often-overlooked performance variable. _Source: @jeremyphoward ([post](https://x.com/jeremyphoward/status/2065569861888258139))_
- **"Don't build harnesses, build environments"**: The key lesson from #EinsteinArena — an agent-native research ecosystem (forums, verifiers, shared infrastructure) opened to any agent produced more advances on open science problems than narrow task harnesses. _Source: @james_y_zou ([post](https://x.com/james_y_zou/status/2065142357545414848))_
- **Cost to train a 1T-parameter frontier model**: A GPT-class 1T model from scratch (including failed runs, data acquisition/cleaning, RLHF, post-training, and team) needs roughly $250M of compute on an aggressive 3–4 month schedule, ~$500–600M all-in for a *dense* model — and MoE + fp8 can cut these costs substantially. _Source: @MohapatraHemant ([post](https://x.com/MohapatraHemant/status/2065746721762902076))_
- **Real-time animation at scale (MotionBricks)**: NVIDIA Research's open model serves real-time character animation from 350,000+ motion clips at 15,000 FPS, with no hand-crafted transitions or per-task fine-tuning, and transfers to robotics. _Source: @nvidia ([post](https://x.com/nvidia/status/2065476367609917682))_
- **Repurposing dead phones as compute**: Hundreds of millions of usable phones are discarded yearly; Google and UC San Diego are exploring turning them into "phone cluster" cloud-computing nodes — a path to a second life for retired hardware. _Source: @JeffDean ([post](https://x.com/JeffDean/status/2065649717573505188))_

## Software Engineering

- **Set ambitious goals for coding agents**: Giving a coding model a stretch goal ("improve performance by 10%") rather than a modest one ("by 1%") yields better results even when the model never hits the target — then ship PRs continuously and merge discrete improvements as they land. _Source: @charliermarsh ([post](https://x.com/charliermarsh/status/2065593370156609938))_
- **The failure mode of AI-assisted code is the *seam***: The bug usually isn't bad code in one file — it lives in the seam between two individually-correct pieces. Human review is structurally blind to this because we read one file at a time, top to bottom, looking for the wrong kind of error. _Source: @CristianPenaOK ([post](https://x.com/CristianPenaOK/status/2065575262079885693))_
- **LLMs reason locally**: A practical limitation to design around — models still reason very locally, which is why layered linting/automated review passes help but don't substitute for reading the code yourself. _Source: @arb8020 ([post](https://x.com/arb8020/status/2065152919440163174)), ([post](https://x.com/arb8020/status/2065152804537135252))_
- **Optimizing code style for weaker models**: LLMs could explore code structures that let progressively *weaker* models still complete tasks in a codebase — there are likely stylistic quirks that are unusually impactful to transformers. _Source: @ID_AA_Carmack ([post](https://x.com/ID_AA_Carmack/status/2065138674950414428))_
- **Logic, not syntax, was always the hard part**: Visual/no-code builders promised to spare non-coders from syntax, but a complex automation still needs loops, branching, and state in any medium. Expressing the same logic as a plain Python script wins because you can grep it, diff it, and roll it back. _Source: @aisle_sh ([post](https://x.com/aisle_sh/status/2062952515247734800))_
- **ty-pre-commit "just works" via uv**: Type-checker pre-commit hooks usually force you to enumerate dependencies in hook config or install them out-of-band; this hook uses uv to auto-install whatever's needed before type-checking. _Source: @charliermarsh ([post](https://x.com/charliermarsh/status/2065175171603046729))_
- **The Next.js catch-all route tax**: A single heavy page in a catch-all route makes *every* page pay its bundle cost; analyzing what each slug actually needs (e.g. with next-slug-splitter) cut light pages from ~1250 kB to 141 kB. _Source: @gurkerl83 ([post](https://x.com/gurkerl83/status/2063781108160831512))_
- **Offloading long-running agents (/handoff)**: Devin's open-sourced `/handoff` lets you hand a job from your local coding agent (Claude Code, Codex, etc.) off to a cloud agent and close your laptop without pausing the work. _Source: @imjaredz ([post](https://x.com/imjaredz/status/2065153770762154186))_

## Systems & Infrastructure

- **Always inspect PTX and SASS**: In CuteDSL, Python `max` for fp32 lowers to `setp`/`selp` (not a single max op), and consecutive `max.f32` in PTX fuses into a single `FMNMX3` instruction in SASS — apparently new on Blackwell, computing a 3-input min/max and reducing 3 registers to 1 instruction. Lesson: the high-level op you write isn't necessarily the instruction that runs. _Source: @gaunernst ([post](https://x.com/gaunernst/status/2065767017282769071))_
- **Breakable CUDA Graph (BCG) in SGLang**: SGLang v0.5.13's BCG delivers prefill efficiency comparable to Piecewise CUDA Graph (PCG) while being more flexible and compatible with advanced optimizations — and doubles as a debugging tool, since being able to "break" the graph aids inspection. _Source: @ying11231 ([post](https://x.com/ying11231/status/2065687428351017200)), @lmsysorg ([post](https://x.com/lmsysorg/status/2065695133077835951))_
- **Progressive-resolution diffusion (SPEED)**: Most denoising steps don't need full resolution. Spectral Progressive Diffusion grows resolution progressively during denoising for up to 2× speedup with quality untouched — now shipping in SGLang Diffusion for FLUX.1/2, Z-Image, Qwen-Image, and Wan. _Source: @ying11231 ([post](https://x.com/ying11231/status/2065557263088341452)), @BrianCChao via @ying11231 ([post](https://x.com/ying11231/status/2065191084905599069))_
- **SGLang inference record on GB300**: SGLang set a new throughput record of 12k tokens per GPU on NVIDIA's GB300 NVL72 — a useful current data point for inference cost/throughput planning on Blackwell-class hardware. _Source: @BanghuaZ ([post](https://x.com/BanghuaZ/status/2065533609134686352))_
- **BYOC sandboxes (SkyPilot Sandboxes)**: Most AI teams pay a >4× markup running sandboxes on hosted vendors' machines. SkyPilot Sandboxes run untrusted/LLM-generated code on Kubernetes clusters you already own — sub-second individual launches, 50,000+ sandboxes on a single cluster, and up to 10× cheaper than hosted vendors. _Source: @skypilot_org ([post](https://x.com/skypilot_org/status/2065464144745361801)), @jeremyphoward ([post](https://x.com/jeremyphoward/status/2065567737473368433))_
- **GPUs are for more than matmuls**: A new kernel competition (first problem: batched QR decomposition on B200) highlights that linear-algebra kernels — "old math, modern hardware" — are an open optimization frontier beyond matrix multiply, and can be iterated cheaply via Modal's fast cold starts. _Source: @marksaroufim ([post](https://x.com/marksaroufim/status/2065564124092355021)), @CoreAutoAI ([post](https://x.com/CoreAutoAI/status/2065565678401192335))_
- **The GPU + LPU combo for inference**: Groq's Language Processing Unit (LPU) was built specifically for inference; the emerging bet is pairing GPUs (training/throughput) with LPUs (low-latency inference) to power the long agentic chains that agentic AI requires. _Source: @nvidia ([post](https://x.com/nvidia/status/2065124171538137481))_
- **Cooling is now an architecture decision**: As GPU density rises, liquid cooling manages heat more efficiently and enables denser designs — making cooling architecture a strategic (not merely operational) choice in AI infrastructure. _Source: @ExascaleLabs22 ([post](https://x.com/ExascaleLabs22/status/2064812949412384994))_
- **Why old games "feel" harder now**: Modern emulation stacks insert ~30 layers of abstraction (Bluetooth controller driver stacks, general-purpose OS, thread coordination) between input and screen, adding latency; plugging an NES into an actual CRT (vs. LCD with composite input) makes games noticeably easier because the display itself adds frames of latency. _Source: @lauriewired ([post](https://x.com/lauriewired/status/2065553747078709500)), @ID_AA_Carmack ([post](https://x.com/ID_AA_Carmack/status/2065610869518553472))_
- **Europe already has the compute**: Italy's Leonardo supercomputer plus the Swiss National Supercomputing Centre together hold more than enough capacity to train a very large LLM — the constraint is organizational, not raw FLOPs. _Source: @antirez ([post](https://x.com/antirez/status/2065727949073010806))_

## Security

- **The "deemed export" rule**: US export controls can restrict a technology's access by *foreign nationals even inside the United States* (a "deemed export"), which is why a national-security directive could in principle bar non-citizen employees — including EB-1 visa holders and well-known researchers — from internal access. _Source: @AnthropicAI ([post](https://x.com/AnthropicAI/status/2065597531644743999)), @richa_lq ([post](https://x.com/richa_lq/status/2065635300022575248)), @bpodgursky ([post](https://x.com/bpodgursky/status/2065615526311624961))_
- **Export control on a deployed frontier model**: The US government issued an export-control directive citing national-security authorities to suspend foreign access to a leading lab's most powerful model — setting a precedent that regulators may now yank deployed frontier models over narrow misuse/bypass risks, treating them like controlled technology. _Source: @JosephJacks_ ([post](https://x.com/JosephJacks_/status/2065602517153800394)), @lindensli ([post](https://x.com/lindensli/status/2065618745058533728))_
- **Security-disclosure incident**: Per a WSJ report, Amazon (a major Anthropic investor) allegedly bypassed its Claude model's security protocols before notifying authorities, later disclosing the incident to the Department of Commerce — illustrating emerging regulatory reporting expectations for AI security events. _Source: @AKJr_Official ([post](https://x.com/AKJr_Official/status/2065761830098624563))_

## Business & Strategy

- **Owning weights as a hedge**: A direct corollary of the export-control news — you can't always rely on access to a frontier API, but you can always access frontier *capabilities* if you own the underlying weights, which is why open-weights momentum accelerates under such restrictions. _Source: @lindensli ([post](https://x.com/lindensli/status/2065618745058533728)), @JosephJacks_ ([post](https://x.com/JosephJacks_/status/2065602517153800394))_
- **Usage-based "coding plan" pricing can be structurally unprofitable**: A flat plan that loses money on heavy users is like "a gym that only profits because members never show up" — your most engaged customers become your biggest losses. _Source: @victor207755822 ([post](https://x.com/victor207755822/status/2065806753074446444))_
- **Product-market fit is a ratio, not a milestone**: PMF shows up when the *cost of acquiring each new dollar of revenue collapses* — e.g. the first $100K took 60 days, the second $100K took 28. _Source: @lucaslbennett ([post](https://x.com/lucaslbennett/status/2065042505851392294))_
- **When to stop training your own models**: Defog abandoned its own models in 2024 (despite 2M+ HF downloads) after o1, reasoning that frontier APIs would keep getting better and cheaper, so they'd inherit gains automatically instead of paying to retrain. _Source: @rishdotblog ([post](https://x.com/rishdotblog/status/2065760909201465656))_
- **Coding agents crossed a usability threshold ~6 months ago**: Lambda's CTO notes AI agents now genuinely work for software development whereas they didn't six months prior — but the capability frontier is uneven across domains, so agents aren't yet ready everywhere companies are deploying them. _Source: @LambdaAPI ([post](https://x.com/LambdaAPI/status/2065507444181368839))_
- **DeepSeek V4, read from inside China**: Three observations Western coverage misses — the release cadence isn't slower, just quieter; the cost-per-token gap is widening in China's favor; and distribution (not capability) remains the unsolved problem. _Source: @leon2mcp ([post](https://x.com/leon2mcp/status/2054634478404845593))_
- **Benchmark saturation shifts power to "neolabelers"**: Once-famous benchmarks like SWE-bench and HLE are now largely saturated and low-signal, pushing influence from data vendors like Scale toward newer data-labeling firms (e.g., Datacurve) — though newer benchmarks like DeepSWE carry their own false-positive / reward-hacking risks. _Source: @avikrishna ([post](https://x.com/avikrishna/status/2065602180162425111)), @spencermateega ([post](https://x.com/spencermateega/status/2065563681791091001))_
- **Pitch quality can be negatively correlated with founder success**: One investor's tracking found pitch polish *anti*-predicts outcomes — citing Zuckerberg's awkward early meetings — suggesting evaluators over-weight presentation. _Source: @adamshuaib ([post](https://x.com/adamshuaib/status/2065566875669372956))_
- **Pricing power from market dominance**: Netflix raised its full plan to $27/month — up ~50% in six years — while holding ~100M more subscribers than its nearest competitor, illustrating how a dominant position enables repeated price hikes. _Source: @BullMooseProj ([post](https://x.com/BullMooseProj/status/2061577780559507902))_
- **The data layer is an underbuilt niche**: Few new labs work on data collection itself; Macrodata Labs (founders behind FineWeb and FinePDF) is betting on filtering the messy multimodal/robotics data that frontier training increasingly depends on. _Source: @eliebakouch ([post](https://x.com/eliebakouch/status/2065114511439249852))_
- **Counter-cyclical early-career hiring**: While many firms cut new-grad hiring amid AI uncertainty, Cloudflare hired 1,111 interns, explicitly betting on people who can "function in really dynamic environments." _Source: @tbpn ([post](https://x.com/tbpn/status/2065530459627667951))_
- **Buffett's signature post-GFC bet**: On Nov 3, 2009 — about six months after the market's GFC lows — Berkshire Hathaway announced its $26.5B acquisition of Burlington Northern Santa Fe, the signature Berkshire investment of that era and a case study in deploying capital into cyclical assets near a bottom. _Source: @porterstansb ([post](https://x.com/porterstansb/status/2064345241830764720))_

## Science & Research

- **Laser phase plate for atomic-resolution imaging**: UC Berkeley announced the brightest continuous-wave laser in the world — ~100 million times the intensity of the Sun's surface — enabling phase-contrast imaging at atomic resolution, a tool now being funded (>$500M, open-science approach) to generate new biology-foundation-model training data. _Source: @ylecun ([post](https://x.com/ylecun/status/2065407719125746003)), @JosephJacks_ ([post](https://x.com/JosephJacks_/status/2065156916440084537))_

- **An unintended effect of dropping the SAT**: A Berkeley math professor notes that under test-optional admissions, the *more* successful a public high school is at preparing students, the *lower* its graduates' odds at top UCs — and that the admissions objective may not be to admit students who will perform best academically. _Source: @sfmcguire79 ([post](https://x.com/sfmcguire79/status/2065611600506024263)), @minilek ([post](https://x.com/minilek/status/2065805722898813439))_
- **Durable skills — whose job to teach them?**: Nearly 90% of teachers rate durable ("soft") skills as extremely/very important for schools to prioritize, yet most say the responsibility for teaching them falls mainly on parents. _Source: @edchoice ([post](https://x.com/edchoice/status/2052787594447016416))_

## Key Takeaways

- **The harness, not just the model, drives agent performance** — the same model scored worst inside Claude Code vs. other CLIs, and the broader lesson is to "build environments, not harnesses." _Source: @jeremyphoward ([post](https://x.com/jeremyphoward/status/2065569861888258139)), @james_y_zou ([post](https://x.com/james_y_zou/status/2065142357545414848))_
- **Frontier models are built on hundreds of other models and datasets** — ModSleuth's tracing (Olmo 3: 89 models + 183 datasets; Nemotron 3: 273 + 560) makes "model lineage" a measurable, security-relevant property, especially as governments begin restricting access to upstream frontier models. _Source: @sewon__min ([post](https://x.com/sewon__min/status/2065115714906894648)), @berkeley_ai ([post](https://x.com/berkeley_ai/status/2065166768516309141))_
- **Inspect the compiled output — your high-level op isn't the instruction that runs** — Python `max` lowering to `setp`/`selp` and `max.f32` fusing into Blackwell's `FMNMX3` is a vivid reminder for anyone writing performance-critical kernels. _Source: @gaunernst ([post](https://x.com/gaunernst/status/2065767017282769071))_
- **Export controls on frontier models make owning weights a strategic hedge** — a government directive suspending foreign API access shows deployed models can be pulled like controlled technology, and that "deemed export" rules can even reach non-citizen employees inside the US. _Source: @JosephJacks_ ([post](https://x.com/JosephJacks_/status/2065602517153800394)), @lindensli ([post](https://x.com/lindensli/status/2065618745058533728)), @AnthropicAI ([post](https://x.com/AnthropicAI/status/2065597531644743999))_
- **Frontier general models now beat specialized tools in serious domains** — in a blinded clinician evaluation, general LLMs outperformed dedicated medical references like UpToDate and OpenEvidence, while a 1T dense model still costs ~$500–600M all-in to train (MoE + fp8 cutting it materially) — pushing most builders to ride cheaper-and-better APIs rather than train their own. _Source: @JeffDean ([post](https://x.com/JeffDean/status/2065467587207819761)), @MohapatraHemant ([post](https://x.com/MohapatraHemant/status/2065746721762902076)), @rishdotblog ([post](https://x.com/rishdotblog/status/2065760909201465656))_
