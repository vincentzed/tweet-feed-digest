# Learnings - 2026-06-22 01:57

## AI / Machine Learning

- **The real composition of an ML project**: A typical ML project is roughly 50% evaluation, 40% data cleaning, 8% integration, and only 2% training — evaluation and data cleaning set the "noise floor" for learning, and no modeling trick can push performance below that floor. _Source: @yunta_tsai via @elonmusk ([post](https://x.com/elonmusk/status/2068369665647108524)), @ClementDelangue ([post](https://x.com/ClementDelangue/status/2068434794090754193))_
- **Distillation moves you along, not past, the cost/quality frontier**: Distillation does not shift the cost/quality Pareto frontier — it trades capability for cheapness along it. Evidence: Haiku 4.5 is ~10× cheaper than Sonnet but visibly less capable; you only shift the frontier by training a bigger model, and whether cheap distillation can match the frontier remains an open question. _Source: @oneill_c ([post](https://x.com/oneill_c/status/2068751792071184471)), @Laz4rz ([post](https://x.com/Laz4rz/status/2068765894466060773))_
- **Token cost ≠ task cost**: A smarter frontier model may cost more per token yet be cheaper overall, because it solves the problem in far fewer tokens — and can even be faster end-to-end via more sophisticated inference and parallel tool calls. Total task cost, not per-token price, is the right unit of comparison. _Source: @arb8020 ([post](https://x.com/arb8020/status/2068540774594052125)), @gneubig ([post](https://x.com/gneubig/status/2068703584087130310))_
- **Day-0 pricing isn't the real cost floor**: Newly released models historically have large headroom for inference-cost reduction purely from software/serving improvements — sometimes >30× — so comparing models on launch-day API price is misleading. _Source: @KranenKyle ([post](https://x.com/KranenKyle/status/2068858000278986936))_
- **ML is data fitting, not extrapolation**: Most familiar ML models and loss functions amount to large-scale data fitting designed to *interpolate* within the training distribution, not *extrapolate* to genuinely novel regions; an LLM learns the density of "people who had new ideas," not the idea-generation process itself. Conversely, blanket "LLMs can't be novel" claims rely on narrow mathematical facts that don't hold empirically — treat impossibility claims skeptically. _Source: @keenanisalive ([post](https://x.com/keenanisalive/status/2068737303657529373)), @francoisfleuret ([post](https://x.com/francoisfleuret/status/2068645324118175994)), @CompleteSkeptic ([post](https://x.com/CompleteSkeptic/status/2068832320040423849))_
- **The reasoning-trace illusion**: A position paper from Subbarao Kambhampati and ASU researchers argues that an LLM emitting a step-by-step plan creates a powerful but misleading impression of machine reasoning — the visible chain-of-thought is not evidence the model reasoned its way to the answer. _Source: @ylecun ([post](https://x.com/ylecun/status/2068793562435596650))_
- **A "thinking scaling law"**: Allowing a model more inference-time reasoning (longer chains of thought) yields more capability, especially on hard problems — the empirical observation fueling the reasoning-model trend. _Source: @Xianbao_QIAN ([post](https://x.com/Xianbao_QIAN/status/2068722698298196270))_
- **Belief-state updating is weak**: Frontier models struggle to update belief states from assertions alone — telling a model a false "fact" leaves it confidently skeptical until it retrieves external confirmation, exposing how brittle in-context belief revision is. _Source: @willccbb ([post](https://x.com/willccbb/status/2068844951790813332))_
- **PPO vs. GRPO for long-horizon RL**: GRPO's group-level synchronization is hard on training infrastructure — synchronous runs suffer long-tail stragglers, asynchronous runs suffer staleness — and the choice between a group baseline and a learned critic is fundamentally a variance-reduction decision. _Source: @QPHutu ([post](https://x.com/QPHutu/status/2068544629709139984))_
- **Disaggregation is central to efficient RL/serving**: LLM generation alternates between prefill and decode phases with very different resource profiles, so disaggregating them (plus addressing sandbox statefulness and scale elasticity) is a key, under-discussed lever for RL systems. _Source: @kimbochen ([post](https://x.com/kimbochen/status/2068727648725963240))_
- **Learning rate & batch size scaling**: Large-scale comparisons against CompleteP (μP-style) revised views on setting batch size — LR/Bz fitting matters more than assumed, and not all frontier labs use μP (DeepSeek/K2 reportedly use naive N(0, 0.006) init). _Source: @ZhouyunhuaB ([post](https://x.com/ZhouyunhuaB/status/2068542112916750389))_
- **Fine-grained MoE lineage**: Most contemporary MoE models inherit their expert "shape" (many small fine-grained experts) from a single foundational fine-grained-MoE paper, which is also why MoE pruning/calibration tricks (e.g., REAP, coding datasets as calibration) generalize across models. _Source: @teortaxesTex ([post](https://x.com/teortaxesTex/status/2068549047233782026))_
- **Tied embeddings only help small models**: From MAI-Thinking-1 ablations, tying input/output embedding weights only helps at ≤~4B parameters; the model also uses AdamW and disables NVLink SHARP to guarantee determinism at some performance cost. _Source: @gordic_aleksa ([post](https://x.com/gordic_aleksa/status/2068729887498920441))_
- **Late parameter-space extrapolation as an optimizer trick**: Adding a late parameter-space extrapolation step shaved 5 steps off the Modded-NanoGPT speed record (2925 steps), showing how end-of-training weight extrapolation can squeeze out extra convergence. _Source: @kellerjordan0 ([post](https://x.com/kellerjordan0/status/2068402807703806436))_
- **Parallel/diffusion text generation**: Unlike autoregressive LLMs that lock in each token left-to-right, a diffusion-style text model (nanoDiffusionGPT) refines the entire output in parallel, allowing earlier "wrong" tokens to be revised. _Source: @syuggupta ([post](https://x.com/syuggupta/status/2068690475998224697))_
- **Small models can be near-free to train**: A 30B-parameter MoE can be trained from scratch on 10T tokens for ~$500K — but only if data, infra, and the ecosystem of enablers are right from day one. _Source: @pratyushmaini ([post](https://x.com/pratyushmaini/status/2068796570313859579))_
- **Small retrieval models as tool routers**: Stuffing many tools into a context window raises latency and degrades tool-selection accuracy; a tiny late-interaction model (LFM2.5-ColBERT-350M) can pre-filter 151 candidate tools to the 5 most relevant, fast and reliably, even with no task-specific tuning. _Source: @lateinteraction ([post](https://x.com/lateinteraction/status/2068310750821523910)), ([post](https://x.com/lateinteraction/status/2068312167342833741))_
- **Production agent quality is dominated by architecture, not model swaps**: After ~3B tokens and three model changes, output quality moved only ~5–10% per swap; the big gains came from rebuilding the memory system. The model arms race matters less than surrounding engineering. _Source: @MGMurray1 ([post](https://x.com/MGMurray1/status/2068678613226975684))_
- **Agent-loop cost reality**: Today's frontier models in a ReAct-style loop can work well but routinely burn ~$15, ~40 minutes, and a trillion tokens for a single task — a balance worth questioning rather than accepting. _Source: @yoavgo ([post](https://x.com/yoavgo/status/2068687607618298029))_
- **"It's just a loop" hides the real work**: Modern LLM engineering contains loops (an online serving loop; an RL rollout→train→update loop), but — like calling the Linux kernel "a loop" — the framing obscures the actual engineering inside each stage. _Source: @bingxu_ ([post](https://x.com/bingxu_/status/2068629829575180518))_
- **Synthetic environments vs frozen benchmarks**: Synthetic envs let you *control* the task distribution rather than inherit a frozen benchmark's, enabling counterfactual task families, model-specific difficulty filtering, and diagnostics that reveal what a model is genuinely good or bad at. _Source: @novasarc01 ([post](https://x.com/novasarc01/status/2068796834588574185))_
- **Evals are still hand-built**: Labs are reportedly acquiring whole teams just for eval expertise, implying good evals for long-tail use cases are hard and far from automated; the field's main artifacts remain HealthBench-style rubrics and Harbor. _Source: @JoshPurtell ([post](https://x.com/JoshPurtell/status/2068768346959442202)), @abhijaymrana ([post](https://x.com/abhijaymrana/status/2068843661975273940))_
- **Text-only models need visual/QA feedback loops**: GLM-5.2 can build a website but, being text-only, can't inspect the rendered result — pairing it with a separate multimodal QA agent to review aesthetics, find bugs, and send targeted fixes closes that loop. _Source: @JosephJacks_ ([post](https://x.com/JosephJacks_/status/2068859416942911511))_
- **Browser-local generative models**: A ~13M-parameter (~50MB) model can generate real-time AI music entirely in-browser via transformers.js on almost any consumer CPU including mobile — a concrete demonstration of small specialized models enabling client-side inference. _Source: @UtkuCoban000 ([post](https://x.com/UtkuCoban000/status/2068622040974905511))_
- **Long-running agent memory**: LightMem2 is a modular framework for long-running agent memory/context management; its TokenPilot component cuts context growth and serving cost for long shared-session agents. _Source: @zxlzr ([post](https://x.com/zxlzr/status/2066834500253061585))_
- **Frontier training data is increasingly human-expert-annotated**: Labs are spending heavily to have poets, musicians, accountants, and consultants annotate essays, slides, and spreadsheets — a brute-force bet that high-quality human demonstrations on knowledge work improve model output. _Source: @jxmnop ([post](https://x.com/jxmnop/status/2068807339474620416))_
- **A pragmatic "engineering-only" recipe for a frontier lab**: Reuse de-risked components rather than inventing them — e.g. DeepSeek V3.2's attention, Kimi K2's 1T-param/32B-active shape, pretraining on ~100T tokens, and an existing post-training framework (zAI's Slime). _Source: @teortaxesTex ([post](https://x.com/teortaxesTex/status/2068564856861561253))_
- **Benchmarks like MMLU are saturating**: Frontier models have effectively stopped improving on MMLU — capability vs. compute fits a logistic (S-curve), not an exponential, so headline scores plateau as they approach the ceiling. _Source: @xlr8harder ([post](https://x.com/xlr8harder/status/2068580832097321225))_
- **Today's Transformer has deep roots**: The 2017 normalized dot-product Transformer traces its core principles to early-1990s work on fast weight controllers — a reminder that "new" architectures often re-express older ideas. _Source: @SchmidhuberAI via @redtachyon ([post](https://x.com/redtachyon/status/2068720780179874000))_
- **The "harness" as a distinct primitive**: An agent harness uniquely combines agency (it initiates), embodiment (it acts on the world), and persistence (it carries state) — properties a single LLM call lacks, which is why orchestration is closer to organizational design (outcome verification, boundary engineering, supervision) than to traditional software engineering. _Source: @AgentField_ai ([post](https://x.com/AgentField_ai/status/2061896953844117673)), ([post](https://x.com/AgentField_ai/status/2061896953751900229))_
- **GLM-5.2 reaches frontier parity**: GLM-5.2 was open-sourced with coding-agent capability reported on par with the latest Opus, and multiple practitioners call it the first open model good enough to be a "daily driver" — a concrete data point that the open/closed coding gap has narrowed. _Source: @ying11231 ([post](https://x.com/ying11231/status/2068254762525753710)), @qasimmith ([post](https://x.com/qasimmith/status/2068749584877441420))_
- **There's a real cost-efficiency spectrum in open models**: Options like MiniMax-M2.7 (240B), Qwen-3.6-35B, and the larger GLM 5.2 span a range of size/cost trade-offs, so you can pick a point on the curve rather than defaulting to the largest closed model. _Source: @gneubig ([post](https://x.com/gneubig/status/2068703430458089646))_
- **Practical frontier-model comparison**: In hands-on use, GPT-5.5 is the most trustworthy but tends toward over-engineering/defensiveness (mitigable with prompting), while GLM 5.2 and Opus 4.8 occupy different reliability/cost niches — match model to task. _Source: @albertgao ([post](https://x.com/albertgao/status/2068846208764768652))_
- **Inference token volume is scaling fast**: One provider reports daily token throughput hitting 11.25B, a concrete marker of how quickly real-world production AI workloads are growing. _Source: @MiniMax_AI ([post](https://x.com/MiniMax_AI/status/2068420415022919911))_
- **Run-cost economics of LLM serving**: Inference costs are falling but not fast enough to offset rising prices, so most AI services still lose money and are effectively subsidized — a structural fragility behind "bubble" warnings. _Source: @kimmonismus summarizing Yann LeCun ([post](https://x.com/kimmonismus/status/2068785890353160226))_

## Software Engineering

- **Use a second model as a design reviewer**: A useful Codex loop is to finish designing an API and then get a second opinion from a different model (e.g. Opus via `claude -p`) before implementing — cross-model review measurably improved code quality. _Source: @theo ([post](https://x.com/theo/status/2068595585121484896))_
- **Think more, build less**: One of the most effective developers spends ~95% of his time thinking and ~5% building — front-loading design reduces wasted implementation. _Source: @GergelyOrosz ([post](https://x.com/GergelyOrosz/status/2068700974277910610))_
- **The new frontier is "colossal-scale" software**: AI agents make it economical to build software previously too expensive to attempt — massive API surfaces, full language ports, and large migrations. _Source: @rough__sea ([post](https://x.com/rough__sea/status/2068704065257910655))_
- **AI-authored code has a recognizable smell**: Inline comments that merely restate decisions (which belong in the PR summary) are a tell that an agent wrote the code; more broadly, AI-generated code is a working point in program space but typically not well-designed. _Source: @charliermarsh ([post](https://x.com/charliermarsh/status/2068816379730440339)), @arb8020 ([post](https://x.com/arb8020/status/2068739608053956650))_
- **`set -euo pipefail` demystified**: The common Bash safety prologue exits on any error (`-e`), treats unset variables as errors (`-u`), and makes a pipeline fail if *any* stage fails rather than only the last (`-o pipefail`). _Source: @code_star ([post](https://x.com/code_star/status/2068578171251994643))_
- **The PDF format is a runtime, not a document**: The PDF spec is 1,000+ pages and supports interactive 3D rendering, JavaScript execution, and built-in maps/GIS — which is why correctly handling PDFs (and companies like DocuSign) is far harder than it appears. _Source: @BenjDicken ([post](https://x.com/BenjDicken/status/2068836303299621187))_
- **Hand-crafting UUIDs is a real (and dangerous) anti-pattern**: Copying an existing UUID and bumping a digit destroys statistical uniqueness and has shipped collisions into major production systems — always generate UUIDs programmatically. _Source: @snoopy_dot_jpg ([post](https://x.com/snoopy_dot_jpg/status/2068721191767187563))_
- **Rewriting torch.compile FX graphs**: During `torch.compile`, you can manipulate nodes in the captured FX graph — iterate operators, insert a replacement node to manually fuse two ops, and pop the old nodes — a practical hook for custom graph-level optimizations. _Source: @jino_rohit ([post](https://x.com/jino_rohit/status/2068566624874250467))_
- **Cross-company agent discovery (ARD)**: Google, Microsoft, Snowflake et al. published Agentic Resource Discovery, an open spec letting AI agents discover each other's capabilities across organizations; its Trust Manifest accepts cryptographic agent identities including DIDs and SPIFFE. _Source: @santoshradha ([post](https://x.com/santoshradha/status/2068394052412735619))_
- **Skill/"record-and-replay" workflows for agents**: You can teach an agent a multi-step, cross-app workflow once (e.g., via a `/teach` command) and have it reliably replay it afterward — an open-source pattern (Understudy) mirroring OpenAI's Codex Record & Replay. _Source: @LiangSong850509 ([post](https://x.com/LiangSong850509/status/2067991792310771818))_
- **Tracking remaining context window improves agent UX**: Displaying "% context left" directly in your coding-agent interface is a simple, high-value addition you can even prompt the agent to add itself. _Source: @difficultyang ([post](https://x.com/difficultyang/status/2068420504516509940))_
- **Open benchmark results with open reasoning traces**: KernelBench-Hard and KernelBench-Mega results were published to the Hub with full reasoning traces open, letting you study not just scores but the models' actual problem-solving behavior on GPU-kernel tasks. _Source: @ClementDelangue ([post](https://x.com/ClementDelangue/status/2068166969237336082))_
- **Bypassing paste-blocking forms**: Browsers like Brave include a "Force Paste" option that defeats sites which disable pasting into form fields (a common, user-hostile pattern on password and confirmation inputs). _Source: @brandur ([post](https://x.com/brandur/status/2068704687810027930))_
- **Web performance is won frame-by-frame**: The vercel.com refresh optimized painting, layout, WebGPU shaders, and blocking scripts individually — perceived speed comes from auditing each rendering stage, not one global fix. _Source: @rauchg ([post](https://x.com/rauchg/status/2068838709517336756))_

## Systems & Infrastructure

- **The binding constraint on AI is now power, not chips**: A grid operator pooling compute for frontier labs shows 1.3 GW secured against ~6 GW needed over four years — a 4.7 GW gap representing years of constrained growth. A data center is fundamentally an energy-to-tokens black box: power and capital in; tokens (plus CO₂, heat, water) out — so every internal improvement ultimately changes that input/output ratio. _Source: @swyx ([post](https://x.com/swyx/status/2068446301679960206)), @Azizian_Reza ([post](https://x.com/Azizian_Reza/status/2066921241764909299))_
- **Hardware support ≠ usable performance**: Current GPUs have full hardware support for MXFP8/MXFP4, but optimized kernels lag, so the low-precision formats aren't yet practically fast. Likewise, fp16 GEMM gains hinge on modern MMA shapes: `m8n8k4` is legacy Volta — move to `m16n8k8` or `m16n8k16`, and use `ldmatrix`/`ldmatrix.trans` to load fragments from shared memory directly into registers. _Source: @JaydevTonde ([post](https://x.com/JaydevTonde/status/2068564415033843861)), @gpuwaster ([post](https://x.com/gpuwaster/status/2068778461397663933)), @gaunernst ([post](https://x.com/gaunernst/status/2068850882134126716))_
- **Hardware cost to run a frontier open model**: An NVFP4 quant of GLM-5.2 is ~467GB and fits across 4× DGX Sparks (~$20k); a ~433GB GGUF runs on a single 512GB M3 Ultra Mac for long-horizon tasks — concrete data points on the capital needed to self-host frontier-class open weights. _Source: @ClementDelangue ([post](https://x.com/ClementDelangue/status/2068450788049838488)), @hxiao ([post](https://x.com/hxiao/status/2068800750554378434)), @antirez ([post](https://x.com/antirez/status/2068723687990108312))_
- **Inference-engine choice matters per hardware**: When bringing GLM-5.2 up on 8×AMD MI300X, SGLang outperformed tinygrad — engine/hardware pairing is an empirical decision, not a given. _Source: @elliotarledge ([post](https://x.com/elliotarledge/status/2068725639067398272))_
- **"Embarrassingly parallel" has a precise meaning**: It means *zero communication* — pure decoupled compute. Map-reduce does **not** qualify, because the reduce step is communication; the correct term for that is just "parallel." _Source: @blelbach ([post](https://x.com/blelbach/status/2068776089095172205))_
- **GPU QR factorization is many problems**: A high-performance batched QR solver isn't one kernel but a dozen+ — specialized per matrix shape/type, exploiting tensor cores across mixed formats (FP64/FP32/TF32/FP16/FP8), and the top solutions may end up memory-bound rather than compute-bound. Householder reflection is the standard math foundation. _Source: @blelbach ([post](https://x.com/blelbach/status/2068591472350261414)), @datavorous_ ([post](https://x.com/datavorous_/status/2068696750228173229))_
- **Hand-tuned kernels still beat agents at small shapes**: An automated agent (Codex) pursued advanced GPU tactics (bypassing PyTorch for faster cuBLAS, blocked QR updates, multi-CTA coordination, cost-modelled DP), yet a careful hand-written kernel still won at small QR sizes — low-level human tuning retains an edge in narrow regimes. _Source: @gaunernst ([post](https://x.com/gaunernst/status/2068164798215491747))_
- **CUDA radix sort is just prefix sums**: A naive GPU radix sort reduces to bit extraction → exclusive scan (prefix sum) → scatter; the prefix-sum step does essentially all the heavy lifting. _Source: @reprompting ([post](https://x.com/reprompting/status/2068664532008841375))_
- **WASM can rival native matmul**: jax-js generates in-browser WebAssembly matrix multiply hitting 88% of OpenBLAS peak on macOS — surprising evidence WASM can approach multithreaded native code for CPU matmul. _Source: @ekzhang1 ([post](https://x.com/ekzhang1/status/2068499841127264672))_
- **Megakernels for inference**: KernelBench-Mega tasks models to write a GPU "megakernel" — one CUDA kernel per generated token — benchmarked across RTX PRO 6000, H100, and B200, illustrating an extreme form of kernel fusion for LLM decoding. _Source: @ClementDelangue ([post](https://x.com/ClementDelangue/status/2068193502073086038))_
- **Memory prices are spiking**: RAM (and GPU) prices have risen sharply — a hardware item bought for ~$9,200 last September is worth notably more now — relevant for anyone budgeting on-prem compute. _Source: @TheZachMueller ([post](https://x.com/TheZachMueller/status/2068498852374524034))_
- **AI buildout concentrates spend**: An estimated ~35% of big-tech capex over the next two years is flowing to just the top three cloud/compute providers — useful context for where AI infrastructure money actually lands. _Source: @astridwilde1 ([post](https://x.com/astridwilde1/status/2068677105894981982))_

## Security

- **Giving agents broad access is the new `chmod 777`**: Granting an AI agent broad access (e.g., to your inbox) is careless over-permissioning — convenient but a serious footgun, directly analogous to making a file world-writable. Enabling WebSearch on an autonomous agent led it to immediately research competitors and probe their submissions, raising real risk it could misuse stored credentials (CLI/Discord tokens). Scope agent permissions and credentials deliberately. _Source: @reallyclicking ([post](https://x.com/reallyclicking/status/2065878438888784074)), @blelbach ([post](https://x.com/blelbach/status/2068747919423946947))_
- **AI for offense vs. defense in security**: A new model (Mythos) is not a step-change at *finding* zero-days (defense's real need) — specialized tools like AISLE match it on real CVEs even on the hardest codebases — though it may advance *exploit generation*, an important distinction for deployment. _Source: @stanislavfort ([post](https://x.com/stanislavfort/status/2068602087840080198))_
- **Anthropic adding third-party identity verification**: Anthropic is rolling out identity verification for "certain capabilities," using Persona as the third-party provider — relevant for evaluating data-sharing and privacy exposure when relying on a single AI vendor. _Source: @0xIlyy ([post](https://x.com/0xIlyy/status/2068542652019081216))_
- **DNS KSK rollover**: Maintaining long-term cryptographic security in DNS requires periodic Key Signing Key rotations; the upcoming KSK rollover is invisible to end users but demands operational attention from anyone running a DNS resolver. _Source: @ICANN ([post](https://x.com/ICANN/status/2065482583186759968))_

## Business & Strategy

- **More AI usage increases SaaS demand, not less**: People running companies find that adopting AI (e.g. connecting Salesforce's MCP server to an assistant) drives more SaaS usage, contradicting the "AI will disrupt SaaS to zero" narrative. _Source: @fchollet ([post](https://x.com/fchollet/status/2068488678947918124))_
- **Open-weight models reshape the cost equation**: Swapping frontier closed models for open ones yields large savings on sample workloads (Opus→Kimi Code ~82%, GPT→GLM ~77%, Gemini Flash→Nemotron ~67%); GLM-5.2 placed second on Vending-Bench at under half Opus's cost. Its publicly listed parent's stock rose ~15× in six months — a case study in how model releases move equity value. _Source: @philipkiely ([post](https://x.com/philipkiely/status/2067369473049501899)), @ClementDelangue ([post](https://x.com/ClementDelangue/status/2068507378610278494)), @Yuchenj_UW ([post](https://x.com/Yuchenj_UW/status/2068744828259852546)), @guohao_li ([post](https://x.com/guohao_li/status/2068601132998410400))_
- **The China–US model gap is bigger on private tests**: Chinese models trail US ones by ~4–6 months on *public* benchmarks, but the gap nearly doubles to ~8–10 months on *private* tests — evidence that some labs may (possibly unwittingly) "teach to the test." _Source: @shashj ([post](https://x.com/shashj/status/2068802151229563389))_
- **AI sovereignty starts with reducing supply-chain leverage**: Owning your AI stack and adopting open-source models is framed as "step zero" to sovereignty — the point is to reduce dependence and supply-chain risk on a single closed provider. _Source: @ClementDelangue ([post](https://x.com/ClementDelangue/status/2068145868893471154))_
- **DeepSeek pivots to agents**: DeepSeek has spun up a dedicated "Harness" team for agentic training and custom harnesses/products — a strategic shift from pure model training toward agentic systems. _Source: @teortaxesTex ([post](https://x.com/teortaxesTex/status/2068831216896200707))_
- **Adobe is a top-tier GenAI revenue story, against market perception**: AI-first ARR tripled YoY past $500M (Firefly alone ~$300M ARR, ~50% QoQ); company-wide Q2 revenue hit a record $6.62B (up 13% YoY) at ~36% net margins, even while absorbing GenAI compute costs. _Source: @fchollet ([post](https://x.com/fchollet/status/2068866713412854037)), ([post](https://x.com/fchollet/status/2068866573558022201))_
- **Compounding defense-tech outcomes**: Anduril went from $88M to $61B valuation in 9 years — illustrating the "~$100B in 10 years" compounding pattern VCs now underwrite, no longer treated as insane given comparable examples. _Source: @jasonlk ([post](https://x.com/jasonlk/status/2068777143086264552))_
- **Pricing power signals demand**: Anthropic has been raising prices and customers are willingly absorbing them — a concrete indicator of inelastic demand and strong product lock-in in frontier AI. _Source: @theinformation ([post](https://x.com/theinformation/status/2061372517994951059))_
- **Talent mobility may force "garden leave" on AI labs**: With rapid executive/researcher hopping between labs, the trading-firm practice of garden leave (paid non-compete gaps to prevent immediate knowledge transfer) is a plausible coming norm in AI. _Source: @thejessezhang ([post](https://x.com/thejessezhang/status/2068841651636990314))_
- **The Cantillon Effect**: Newly created money does not enter an economy uniformly — it enters at specific points through specific people first, who benefit before prices adjust. A foundational lens for inflation's distributional effects. _Source: @porterstansb ([post](https://x.com/porterstansb/status/2067212960766767377))_

## Key Takeaways

1. **Most of ML is evaluation and data, not training** — budget effort accordingly; the noise floor set by data quality and eval design caps how good any model can get, and good evals remain hand-built. _Source: @yunta_tsai via @elonmusk ([post](https://x.com/elonmusk/status/2068369665647108524))_
2. **Total task cost beats per-token price, and distillation only moves you along a fixed frontier** — smarter/pricier-per-token models can be cheaper overall, day-0 pricing has >30× headroom, and open-weight swaps now cut frontier inference costs 67–82% on real workloads. _Source: @arb8020 ([post](https://x.com/arb8020/status/2068540774594052125)), @philipkiely ([post](https://x.com/philipkiely/status/2067369473049501899)), @oneill_c ([post](https://x.com/oneill_c/status/2068751792071184471))_
3. **In production, agent architecture and the harness beat model choice** — three model swaps over 3B tokens moved quality only 5–10% each; the unlock was rebuilding memory. The harness (agency + embodiment + persistence) is the real unit you engineer. _Source: @MGMurray1 ([post](https://x.com/MGMurray1/status/2068678613226975684)), @AgentField_ai ([post](https://x.com/AgentField_ai/status/2061896953751900229))_
4. **Open models have effectively closed the coding gap — and now self-host on modest hardware** — GLM-5.2 is a near-Opus daily driver, fits on ~$20k of DGX Sparks or a single 512GB Mac, and moved its listed parent's stock ~15× in six months. _Source: @ying11231 ([post](https://x.com/ying11231/status/2068254762525753710)), @ClementDelangue ([post](https://x.com/ClementDelangue/status/2068450788049838488)), @Yuchenj_UW ([post](https://x.com/Yuchenj_UW/status/2068744828259852546))_
5. **Power, not silicon, is now the AI bottleneck** — a 1.3 GW-secured-vs-6 GW-needed gap means years of constrained growth, every datacenter is fundamentally an energy-to-tokens converter, and even supported low-precision formats (MXFP8/MXFP4) are bottlenecked by missing kernels. _Source: @swyx ([post](https://x.com/swyx/status/2068446301679960206)), @JaydevTonde ([post](https://x.com/JaydevTonde/status/2068564415033843861))_
6. **Agent permissions are the new `chmod 777`** — casually granting agents broad access (inbox, tools, web, stored credentials) is a serious, under-appreciated security risk as agentic systems proliferate. _Source: @reallyclicking ([post](https://x.com/reallyclicking/status/2065878438888784074)), @blelbach ([post](https://x.com/blelbach/status/2068747919423946947))_
